KSE-100 clears 180,500 — but the breadth tells a different story

Good morning. Here's the Economic Vibes desk read for Tuesday, 18 August 2026.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
Wall Street fell Monday after the 60-day US-Iran memorandum governing the Strait of Hormuz expired without a successor deal and President Trump threatened action against Oman: S&P 500 -0.5% to 7,745.06, Dow -0.5% to 53,459.78, Nasdaq -0.3% to 26,644.91. The 30-year Treasury yield surged past 5.31% — its highest since 2007 — and the 10-year rose to 4.72% as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh signalled a hike may not be his preferred tool against inflation.

PAKISTAN
KSE-100 closed at 180,502.44 (+397.83 pts, +0.22%) on volume of 511.22m shares, but breadth was negative — 43 gainers vs 57 losers. The rally was almost entirely energy-led (CNERGY, PSO, ATRL, SSGC) tracking firmer crude, while banks, cement and fertilizer lagged. July CPI eased to 9.2% y/y and SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting. An IMF mission is expected in September for the fourth EFF/second RSF review, which could unlock roughly $1.2bn.

GCC
Saudi non-oil PMI held near 53 in July, staying expansionary for a fourth straight month, and PIF published its 2026-2030 strategy prioritising tourism, urban development, manufacturing, logistics, clean energy and NEOM alongside strong FY2025 results. Firmer oil on the Hormuz standoff is a fiscal tailwind for exporters even as it raises regional shipping-cost risk.

GLOBAL
WTI jumped 3.1% to $84.95/bbl on the Hormuz risk premium while Brent held near $88.31/bbl. Gold held above $4,400 (silver $65.66, +1.3%) on softer US retail sales (-0.6% in July), though both remain well off 2026's record above $5,000.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: IMF fourth review prep + SBP rate hold — wins for exporters if the tranche clears, headwind for rate-sensitive banks.
• GCC: Saudi's new Copyright Law (Aug 12) and UAE's merger-control regulations (Jul 30) tighten IP and antitrust frameworks — wins for rights holders, added compliance cost for platforms and acquirers.
• Global: Hormuz memorandum lapses, Warsh signals hike may not be preferred tool — wins for energy and the dollar, headwind for rate-sensitive growth equities and EM borrowers.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 support 179,500 · pivot 180,502 (Monday's close) · resistance 181,159 (Monday's high), then 182,000. We read Monday's move as narrow strength — the entire gain was concentrated in energy names, and decliners outnumbered advancers. That leaves the tape vulnerable to a reversal if Hormuz tensions ease. All levels are indicative desk estimates, not recommendations.
