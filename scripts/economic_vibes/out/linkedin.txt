KSE-100 rockets 2,593 points (+1.56%) to 168,460 in a furious reversal, clawing back most of Monday's rout, as Wall Street notches fresh S&P 500 and Nasdaq records even as Houthi strikes on Saudi Aramco facilities keep oil whipsawing near $100.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US equities extended their run Tuesday as easing long-dated yields and AI-driven tech buying offset a global bond sell-off: the S&P 500 rose 0.58% to a record 7,818.93, the Dow added 0.49% (+253.38 pts) to 51,521.28, and the Nasdaq climbed 0.45% to a record 27,599.79, led by Nvidia and AMD. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to roughly 5.27% and the 2-year to 4.80%, off Monday's multi-decade highs. Minutes from the Fed's hawkish 15-16 September meeting, where the FOMC hiked to 3.75-4.00%, are due today at 2:00pm ET, with prediction markets pricing roughly 81-84% odds of a hold at the 28 October meeting. Gold firmed to near $4,164 (+0.6%) and silver to $61.41 (+0.8%) as yields eased. Brent settled at $100.30 and WTI at $89.18 Tuesday on improving Gulf export flows and a G7 reserve release, before Houthi strikes on Saudi Aramco facilities pushed Brent back toward $101.60 into Wednesday.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 surged 2,592.79 points (+1.56%) Tuesday to settle at 168,460.10, clawing back most of Monday's 2,288-point rout as buying returned broadly across autos, cement, banks, E&Ps, OMCs, power and refineries. Volume rose to roughly 193.4 million shares worth Rs7.71 billion, with advancers beating decliners 267 to 199 across 570 names. The SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a third straight meeting on 14 September as September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y (from August's 11.1%), and the rupee held broadly steady near 277.15 interbank. An IMF mission led by Iva Petrova continues the fourth EFF/third RSF review, opened by Finance Minister Aurangzeb around 29 September; a successful conclusion would unlock roughly $1.2 billion combined, with 10,000 federal employees required to digitally declare assets by 30 October.

GCC
Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI climbed to a seven-month high of 55.3 in September (from 53.8 in August), with new orders growing at their fastest pace since February even as export orders fell for a seventh straight month on Red Sea-linked shipping disruption. OPEC+'s seven core producers held November output steady at 31 million bpd on 4 October, with Gulf exports ex-Iran near 81% of pre-war levels, before Houthi missile and drone strikes on Saudi Aramco facilities in Riyadh and Khurais revived supply-risk premia over the weekend.

GLOBAL
Wall Street's record run came alongside a cooling in long-dated yields and today's pending FOMC minutes release, while a bipartisan continuing resolution signed 2 September keeps Washington funded through 11 December, removing shutdown risk into the midterm run-up even as full-year appropriations remain unresolved.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
Pakistan: IMF fourth EFF/third RSF review + federal asset-declaration mandate - wins for banks and fiscal credibility if the $1.2bn tranche unlocks, risk for consumers facing potential energy-tariff adjustments.
GCC: OPEC+ November output hold + Saudi OSP cuts + PIF Vision 2030 strategy - wins for Gulf exporters and non-oil/tourism plays, risk for shippers and insurers pricing renewed Hormuz/Red Sea threats.
Global: US continuing resolution through 11 December + today's hawkish FOMC minutes - wins for federally funded contractors and large-cap tech, risk for rate-sensitive housing and small-caps.
