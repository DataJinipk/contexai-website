KSE-100 sheds 509 points as Iran widens war to Kuwait and Bahrain

Good morning — here's the ContexAi Research desk's read on markets for Wednesday, 02 September 2026, covering Tuesday's close.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
The S&P 500 fell 0.71% to 7,631.47, the Dow dropped 419 points (0.79%) to 52,766.88 and the Nasdaq lost 271 points (1.03%) to 26,099.77 as renewed US-Iran strikes pushed oil sharply higher and a global bond selloff drove the 10-year Treasury yield to 4.81% — within a stone's throw of 5% — with the 2-year near 4.40%. CME-tracked odds of a hike at the Fed's 15-16 September FOMC meeting climbed to roughly 68%, up from about 57.5% a day earlier and 35% before Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks. Energy majors Chevron (+2.1%) and ExxonMobil (+2.7%) bucked the selloff, while gold slipped 1.6% to $4,374.54/oz and silver fell 2.69% to $64.76/oz.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 shed 508.69 points (0.29%) to close at 176,466.99 on Tuesday, its second straight decline. Ready-market volume was 784.4 million shares worth Rs37.4bn, with breadth negative (174 advancers vs 292 decliners). Commercial banks and cement led losses — Meezan Bank, MCB Bank, Bank AlFalah, Lucky Cement — while Systems Ltd, Engro Holdings, Engro Fertiliser and OGDC found selective buying. August CPI accelerated sharply to 11.1% y/y from July's 9.2%, on double-digit food and transport inflation; the SBP has held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting (next MPC 14 September), and USD/PKR interbank held steady near 277.68. The IMF's 4th EFF and 3rd RSF reviews are set to open in September alongside continued Senate scrutiny of the Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill 2026.

GCC
Brent rose to roughly $91.3/bbl and WTI jumped to ~$90.8/bbl (+5.9%) after Iran widened its response beyond the US-Iran axis, striking Kuwait (a fire at a power and water plant), Bahrain's Sheikh Isa Air Base, and Jordan, while a supertanker caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz after hitting naval mines and President Trump threatened Iran's Kharg Island oil-export hub. OPEC+ finalized a 188,000 bpd September output increase on 2 August. Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI eased to 53.1 in July while the UAE's climbed to a four-month high of 52.7, though Tuesday's strikes on Gulf soil test that resilience heading into August data.

GLOBAL
A hawkish Fed (September hike odds near 68%) and a 10-year Treasury yield closing in on 5% are tightening financial conditions just as oil-driven inflation risk resurfaces from the widening Gulf conflict.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill 2026 remains before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance ahead of September's IMF reviews.
• GCC: OPEC+ output discipline holds even as Iran's strikes reach Kuwaiti and Bahraini territory for the first time.
• Global: The Fed's 15-16 September FOMC meeting looms with hike odds near 68% amid a global bond selloff.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 — Support 176,000 (deeper support 175,000) · Pivot 176,467 (Tuesday's close) · Resistance 177,800 (Tuesday's intraday high, then 178,050).

Desk stance: Tuesday's decline looks like a continuation of the risk-off drift rather than a trend break — the index held above 176,000 and breadth held up better than Monday's session. The core risk is imported and geopolitical: Iran's strikes on Kuwait and Bahrain threaten Pakistan's import bill just as CPI accelerates past 11%, complicating the SBP's hold-steady stance. We favour a defensive tilt toward E&P, fertiliser and IMF-programme beneficiaries while watching further Gulf-wide escalation, CPI, and the Fed.
