🌅 Economic Vibes — Morning Brief | Tuesday, 11 August 2026

Wall Street took a breather from last week's records, oil spiked 5% on stalled Strait of Hormuz talks, and the KSE-100 slipped a marginal 0.07% as sector rotation offset a flat headline print — here's what the desk is watching this morning.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
S&P 500 -0.06% to 7,753.11, Dow -0.11% to 53,975.98, and Nasdaq -0.32% to 26,605.36 on Monday, a mild pullback from last week's records as Nvidia dropped roughly 3% on AI infrastructure funding reports and rising oil prices added a note of caution. The 10-year Treasury yield held near 4.65% (2-year 4.25%), still digesting the Fed's 29 July decision — a divided 9-3 hold at 3.50%-3.75% with the first unified three-way dissent since September 2016.

PAKISTAN
KSE-100 eased 0.07% (-119.74 pts) to close at 181,310.28, though the broader All-Share Index gained 0.21%; breadth was split 50/50 as CNERGY, ATRL and PABC led gainers and HUBC, AICL and TRG led losers, with Power Generation and Fertilizer stocks offsetting gains in Oil & Gas Exploration and Refinery names that tracked Monday's oil spike. CPI eased to 9.2% YoY in July from 11.1% in June, and the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting; six-month KIBOR tracks near 11.70-11.90% (desk est.). USD/PKR held steady near 277.70 interbank, with SBP-held FX reserves at $17.04bn.

GCC
OPEC+ confirmed a further 188,000 bpd increase for September, its sixth straight monthly hike completing the 2023 cuts unwind. Saudi's non-oil PMI held at 53.1 in July, a fourth consecutive month of expansion, while the PIF continues rolling out its 2026-2030 strategy toward tourism, entertainment and localisation. Oil surged on the back of stalled diplomacy — Brent +5.0% to $87.72, WTI +5.1% to $82.13 — as Iran-Oman talks on reopening the Strait of Hormuz remained stalled and a Saudi refinery attack near the Red Sea compounded supply concerns.

GLOBAL
Gold ticked up to roughly $4,427/oz (+0.8%), and silver held near $66.3, on renewed haven demand. The Fed's rate path is getting more contested: September hike odds, which fell to roughly 44-46% after July's weak jobs print, are rebuilding as Hormuz-driven oil gains revive inflation risk ahead of Wednesday's July CPI release. Washington's trade agenda keeps moving too: a 50% Section 338 tariff on Canadian dairy, alcohol and motor-vehicle exports lands in eight days (19 August), while Pakistan's own reciprocal US tariff sits at a comparatively favourable 19%.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
Pakistan faces a 16 July EU GSP+ compliance review on labour rights, with India's zero-tariff EU access threatening to undercut Pakistani textile exporters from 2027.
OPEC+'s September output hike and Saudi's PIF 2026-2030 strategy reshape Gulf energy and sovereign-wealth positioning, even as the Hormuz standoff lifts Gulf producers' realised prices.
The 19 August Section 338 tariff step-up on Canadian goods contrasts with Pakistan's steadier 19% US tariff rate.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100: support 180,000 (psychological, near Monday's 180,820 low; deeper at 178,500), pivot 181,310 (Monday's close), resistance 182,350 (Monday's intraday high, then the 2026 high of 189,167 set in January).
Desk view: Monday's dip masks a sharp sector rotation rather than broad risk-off, with oil's Hormuz-driven spike the key swing factor for Pakistan's import bill and disinflation path, and for the Fed's increasingly contested rate path into September.
