🌅 Economic Vibes — Morning Brief | Thursday, 06 August 2026

The KSE-100 stormed back above 180,000 while Wall Street closed mixed at fresh records — here's what the desk is watching this morning.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
Dow +0.5% to a record 54,349 (fifth straight record close), while the S&P 500 slipped 0.17% to 7,723.55 and the Nasdaq fell 0.83% to 26,363.44, snapping a four-day tech rally as investors rotated out of megacap growth. US 10Y sits at 4.63%, 2Y at 4.21%, with September rate-hike odds easing to ~61% (from over 82% pre-FOMC) after Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish 29 July hold.

PAKISTAN
KSE-100 surged 1.66% to close at 180,014.93 (+2,931.71 pts), reclaiming the 180,000 level on broad, breadth-confirmed buying — 303 advancers vs 158 decliners on 740.6m shares (Rs35.9bn) — led by banks, fertiliser, cement and E&P. Foreign investors were net buyers (Rs771.9m). Reports emerged that Islamabad is seeking Chinese refinancing of a $1.3bn commercial loan. On the macro side: CPI eased to 9.2% YoY in July, and the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting; six-month KIBOR tracks near 11.70–11.90% (desk est.).

GCC
Saudi's non-oil PMI held at a robust 53.1 in July; the UAE's jumped to 52.7 from 50.8 as new export orders grew for the first time since March — Gulf economies rebounding from wartime lows. Oil kept sliding: Brent fell to $79.15, WTI toward $75, as Iran reportedly reached an agreement with Oman on a Hormuz shipping route, deepening a two-day 10%+ drawdown.

GLOBAL
Gold is holding near $4,096 (+0.44%), and silver popped to $62.50 — its highest since June — as havens stayed bid despite record US equities. Washington's trade agenda keeps moving: the 50% tariff on Canadian goods lands in five days (19 August), and Section 301 forced-labor tariffs (10–12.5% on ~60 economies, including Saudi Arabia and the UAE) remain in force; Pakistan's own reciprocal tariff with the US sits at a comparatively favourable 19%.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
Pakistan's Finance Act 2026 digital-creator withholding tax (5%/10%) continues to be deducted at source by banks.
Section 301 forced-labor duties keep Saudi/UAE exporters exposed even as new corporate-transparency rules (Saudi Companies Law, UAE Cabinet Decision 59/2026) tighten disclosure.
The Canada tariff step-up (19 August) and Pakistan's 19% US tariff rate reshape relative trade competitiveness across textiles and manufacturing.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100: support 178,500 (Wednesday's intraday low, deeper at 177,100), pivot 180,015 (Wednesday's close), resistance 180,200 (Wednesday's high, then the psychological 182,000).
Desk view: breadth-confirmed buying and foreign inflows argue for a healthier tape than last week's profit-taking; falling oil remains the key tailwind for Pakistan's import bill. All levels are indicative desk estimates, not recommendations.
