KSE-100 slips 348 points as the KP ratification vote slides to Thursday, while Tadawul rebounds above 10,300 and Fed December-hike odds ease to roughly 72%.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US stocks closed lower Tuesday as memory-chip weakness and mixed bank earnings outweighed an otherwise resilient tape: the Dow fell 0.35% to 51,670.10, the S&P 500 eased 0.02% to 7,805.45 and the Nasdaq lost 0.15% to 27,204.38, with traders cautious ahead of Wednesday's US CPI print. Treasury yields stayed elevated (10Y 5.21%, 2Y 4.81%) even as Fed-funds futures trimmed December hike odds to roughly 72% from ~88% a week earlier; the same mix of a softer dollar and persistent geopolitical risk pulled gold down 0.24% to $4,160.80 and silver down 1.82% to $59.09.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 opened over 900 points higher on hopes of a quick KP resolution, but late selling flipped the session to a close of 164,776.44, down 347.97 points (-0.21%), after Tuesday's NA/Senate sessions failed to ratify the KP Governor's Rule as expected — a joint sitting is now set for Thursday, 15 October. Index-heavy banks, cement and energy names traded green intraday before profit-taking reasserted itself; market-wide volume rose to 383.75m shares from 362.95m. September CPI was confirmed at 10.3% y/y, the SBP held its rate at 11.5% for a third straight meeting, and the rupee held flat at 276.96 interbank; the $1.2bn IMF tranche still awaits Executive Board approval.

GCC
Saudi Arabia's Tadawul All-Share Index rebounded 0.33% to close at 10,318.14 on SR3.28bn turnover, recovering from Monday's attack-driven dip even as breadth stayed soft (104 advancers vs 146 decliners) and the Hormuz/tanker-attack risk overhang persisted. Brent held near $105.67 and WTI near $94.14 after OPEC+ kept November quotas unchanged. Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI climbed to 55.3 in September, a six-month high, while the UAE's non-oil PMI held at the same level.

GLOBAL
Washington's funding picture stays parked under the continuing resolution through 11 December, though the narrower DHS funding lapse remains unresolved. Easing (but still elevated) Fed hike odds offered modest relief to rate-sensitive assets, while precious metals stayed under pressure from firm yields.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
- Pakistan: KP Governor's Rule ratification pushed to Thursday's joint sitting — the federal government/security apparatus retains control; banks and equities absorb two more days of political-risk premium.
- GCC: OPEC+ holding November output steady + UAE VAT rules now in force — oil producers and compliant corporates win; UAE SMEs and broader Gulf sentiment lag.
- Global: US funding certainty through 11 December vs. cooling-but-still-elevated Fed hike odds — bonds and the broader government stabilise; precious metals and DHS-linked contractors stay exposed.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 support 164,000 (deeper support 162,500 if Thursday's vote disappoints), pivot 164,776 (Tuesday's close), resistance 166,070 then 167,089 if the KP vote clears cleanly. Our stance: stay selective into Thursday's vote, favour index-heavy banks and energy names only on confirmed breadth, and treat a push through 166,070 as a signal to watch rather than a reason to chase.
