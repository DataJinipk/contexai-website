KSE-100 plunges 1,690 points as fresh US strikes hit Iran near Hormuz

Good morning — here's the ContexAi Research desk's read on markets for Thursday, 03 September 2026, covering Wednesday's close.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
The S&P 500 rose 0.46% to 7,666.60, the Dow added 295 points (0.56%) to 53,061.95 and the Nasdaq gained 0.45% to 26,217.83, snapping a three-day losing streak even as the US struck Iranian air-defence, radar and maritime assets near the Strait of Hormuz mid-session. The 10-year Treasury yield held near 4.79% — its highest since 2023 — without extending its climb, and the 2-year sat near 4.37%. CME FedWatch continued to price roughly 66% odds of a hike at the 16 September FOMC meeting. Gold steadied near a four-week low at $4,336/oz (+0.18%) while silver slipped to $63.87/oz (-0.35%).

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 tumbled 1,690.40 points (0.96%) to close at 174,776.60 on Wednesday, its steepest drop this week. Ready-market volume was 610.18 million shares worth Rs31.86bn, with breadth sharply negative (113 advancers vs 356 decliners). Commercial banks, cement and E&P names led the rout — Meezan Bank, United Bank, Pakistan Petroleum, Fauji Fertiliser, Lucky Cement, OGDC and Habib Bank — as fresh Gulf strikes stoked import-bill fears. August CPI is running near 11% y/y, and the SBP is expected to hold its policy rate at 11.5% at the 14 September MPC; USD/PKR interbank was little changed near 277.55. The IMF's 4th EFF and 3rd RSF reviews are set to open in September alongside continued Senate scrutiny of the Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill 2026.

GCC
Brent rose to roughly $94.9/bbl and WTI held near $90.7/bbl after US forces struck IRGC targets near the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, with one strike killing five at a wedding and Iran retaliating with missiles and drones. OPEC+ completed its 188,000 bpd September output increase on 2 August. Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI climbed to a six-month high of 53.8 in August, while the UAE's surged to 55.3 — its sharpest improvement since December 2024 — even as the fresh strikes test that momentum.

GLOBAL
Wall Street shrugged off the same Hormuz headlines that hit Karachi hard, snapping a three-day losing streak as Treasury yields paused near a multi-year high. The divergence suggests global investors see the conflict as regionally contained, for now, while Pakistan prices the direct energy and fiscal pass-through.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill 2026 remains before the Senate Standing Committee, which has sought clause-by-clause justification ahead of September's IMF reviews.
• GCC: OPEC+ output discipline holds even as fresh US strikes near Hormuz reignite the six-month conflict.
• Global: The Fed's 16 September FOMC meeting looms with hike odds near 66% as the 10-year yield holds near 4.79%.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 — Support 174,000 (deeper support 173,000) · Pivot 174,777 (Wednesday's close) · Resistance 175,842 (Wednesday's intraday high, then 176,467).

Desk stance: Wednesday's sharper, broader decline — breadth deteriorated to 113 advancers vs 356 decliners on firm volume — looks like genuine distribution rather than thin-market noise. We favour a defensive tilt toward E&P, fertiliser and IMF-programme beneficiaries while watching further Hormuz escalation, the MPC decision, and the SWF bill.
