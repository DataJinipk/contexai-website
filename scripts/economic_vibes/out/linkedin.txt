KSE-100 extends its winning streak to five sessions — but the real story today is in the bond market.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
Wall Street slid Wednesday as the 10-year Treasury yield rocketed to 5.10%, a 19-year high, on hawkish Fed commentary and a weak five-year auction. S&P 500 -0.76% to 7,706.03, Dow -1.03% to 51,511.59, Nasdaq -0.69% to 26,936.04. Oil snapped a five-day losing streak — Brent +3.9% to $103.08, WTI +1.8% to $92.16 — after Iran's president vowed at the UN that Tehran "will not surrender," dimming hopes of a Hormuz reopening. Gold fell to $4,290 (-1.5%) and silver dropped 3.1% to $65.06 as the dollar firmed.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 rose 830 points (+0.48%) to 172,232.51 — a fifth straight gain and the heaviest volume since 4 September — led by refineries and select banks on easing oil prices and improving US-Iran diplomatic signals. The backdrop is more mixed: August CPI accelerated to 11.15% y/y, the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5%, and the IMF has begun talks on the fourth EFF review, with up to $1.2bn potentially unlocked pending energy-sector and Sovereign Wealth Fund reforms.

GCC
Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI held at 56.6 (25th straight month of growth) and the UAE's hit 55.3, its fastest pace since December 2024 — both outrunning a Hormuz-driven disruption that has over 10 mb/d of Gulf oil output still shut in. PIF's board has approved its 2026-2030 strategy, shifting focus toward private-capital mobilisation as Vision 2030 matures.

GLOBAL
The Fed's hawkish tilt (25bp hike to 3.75-4.00% in September, more flagged by Governor Barr) is repricing the yield curve just as Washington widens its tariff agenda — a 100% duty on patented pharmaceuticals and expanded Section 338 tariffs on Canadian goods both take effect 29 September.
