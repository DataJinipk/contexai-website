KSE-100 slips 206 points to 180,104 even as Wall Street notches fresh record highs on cooling US inflation.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US equities closed at fresh records Thursday — S&P 500 +0.6% to a record near 7,799, Nasdaq +0.8% to 26,803.03, Dow +0.13% (69.72 pts) to 53,839.99 — after a flat July producer-price print reinforced bets the Fed will hold rates in September. Market-implied odds of a September hike eased to roughly 40% from about 50% a day earlier, and the 10-year Treasury yield slipped to about 4.66% (2-year near 4.16%). Brent and WTI eased to $87.18 and $81.27 as markets await clarity on a prospective US-Iran-Oman arrangement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Gold and silver pulled back from recent highs, easing 1.3% to $4,349.80/oz and 1.4% to $64.29/oz as cooling yields softened near-term safe-haven demand.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 shed 206.60 points (-0.11%) to close at 180,104.61, giving back part of Wednesday's rebound as fertiliser and pharmaceutical counters saw profit-taking while oil & gas exploration and cement held up. Breadth turned negative with 269 decliners against 197 advancers (29 unchanged) on turnover of 388 million shares, led by CNERGY, BOP, KEL and SSGC. The exchange is now closed for an extended Independence Day weekend, reopening Monday. SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight review, with July CPI at 9.2% even as food inflation ran hotter at 10.6%. Six-month KIBOR stood near 11.51/11.76% as of August 7. USD/PKR interbank held broadly steady at 277.65. An IMF EFF/RSF review mission is due in Islamabad next month, with Pakistan eyeing roughly $1.2bn combined, with circular debt and privatisation progress the key tests.

GCC
OPEC+ finalised a further 188,000 bpd output increase for September, completing the rollback of 2023's voluntary cuts, with the group next due to meet September 6. Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI eased slightly to 53.1 in July (from 53.3) but stayed firmly expansionary for a fourth straight month, even as export orders fell for a fifth month on high freight costs. The PIF, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, has approved its 2026–2030 strategy, pivoting toward value creation with tourism, travel and entertainment as priority sectors under Vision 2030.

GLOBAL
A benign US producer-price print and a nearing Hormuz endgame are the two swing factors reshaping global risk sentiment. Washington, Tehran and Muscat are closing in on an interim arrangement to reopen the Strait, though Iran insists on further concessions before a full reopening.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: 5th IMF EFF/RSF review due next month (~$1.2bn at stake) — wins: banks, FX reserves, sovereign risk; loses: energy consumers facing tariff conditionality.
• GCC: OPEC+ finalises 188,000 bpd September hike, next review Sept 6; PIF's 2026–2030 strategy backs tourism/entertainment — wins: oil-importing economies, Saudi non-oil diversification plays; loses: OPEC+ producers' per-barrel revenue.
• Global: flat July PPI pulls September Fed-hike odds to ~40% — wins: rate-sensitive equities, EM FX including the PKR; loses: USD bulls, money-market yield-seekers.
