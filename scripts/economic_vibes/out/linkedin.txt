KSE-100 rebounds 465 points to 180,311 as cooling US inflation and a nearing Hormuz endgame lift sentiment.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US equities closed mostly higher Wednesday — S&P 500 +0.3% to 7,748.50, Nasdaq +0.5% to 26,588.49, Dow -0.04% to 53,770.27 — after July CPI matched forecasts (3.4% headline, 2.5% core) and eased pressure on the Fed to hike in September. Market-implied odds of a 25bp September hike fell back to roughly 50% from over 60% a week earlier, and the 10-year Treasury yield eased to about 4.65%. Brent and WTI were little changed near $88.98 and $83.27 as deadly attacks on vessels in the Red Sea and Gulf of Oman kept shipping risk in focus. Gold extended its advance to $4,428/oz and silver jumped to $66.20/oz on safe-haven demand.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 gained 464.53 points (+0.26%) to close at 180,311.22, recovering part of Tuesday's 1,464-point drop as oil & gas exploration names (Pakistan Oilfields, Mari Energies), Habib Bank and Hub Power led the rebound. Breadth improved to 249 advancers versus 211 decliners on turnover of 220 million shares worth Rs20bn. SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight review even as July CPI cooled to 9.2% from 11.1% (food inflation, notably, still accelerated to 10.6%). USD/PKR interbank held broadly steady at 277.66. Islamabad is preparing for a fifth IMF EFF/RSF review mission next month that could unlock roughly $1.2bn, with energy circular debt and privatisation progress the key tests.

GCC
OPEC+ finalised a further 188,000 bpd output increase for September, completing the rollback of 2023 voluntary cuts, while signalling a pause in additional hikes from the fourth quarter. Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI eased slightly to 53.1 in July (from 53.3) but stayed firmly expansionary, with Kuwait's reading rebounding. The PIF, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, approved its 2026–2030 strategy, pivoting toward value creation with tourism, travel and entertainment as priority sectors under Vision 2030.

GLOBAL
A benign US CPI print and a nearing Hormuz endgame are the two swing factors reshaping global risk sentiment. Pakistan is racing to broker a US-Iran arrangement on the Strait before an August 16 deadline, even as Washington and Tehran maintain hardened public positions.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: 5th IMF EFF/RSF review due next month (~$1.2bn at stake) — wins: banks, FX reserves, sovereign risk; loses: energy consumers facing tariff conditionality.
• GCC: OPEC+ finalises ~188,000 bpd September hike, signals Q4 pause; PIF's 2026–2030 strategy backs tourism/entertainment — wins: oil-importing economies, Saudi non-oil diversification plays; loses: OPEC+ producers' per-barrel revenue.
• Global: July CPI cools September Fed-hike odds to ~50% — wins: rate-sensitive equities, EM FX including the PKR; loses: USD bulls, money-market yield-seekers.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 support 179,900 (deeper support 178,500) · pivot 180,311 (Wednesday's close) · resistance 180,901 (Wednesday's high), then Monday's 181,310. We read this as a de-escalation and dovish-repricing rebound — a close back above 180,900 reopens the path toward record territory; a slip below 179,900 points to a retest of 178,500.
