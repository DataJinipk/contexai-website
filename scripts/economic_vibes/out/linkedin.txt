KSE-100 sheds 1,965 points as Islamabad imposes emergency in KP — while a deadly Houthi strike on Riyadh's airport jolts Gulf markets and sends Brent above $106.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US stocks closed mixed Monday as an oil-driven inflation scare and a slide in memory-chip names offset a rotation into value: the Dow added 0.38% to 51,850.55, while the S&P 500 eased 0.06% to 7,807.24 and the Nasdaq fell 0.44% to 27,245.90. Treasury yields held near multi-decade highs (10Y 5.24%, 2Y 4.80%) as Fed-funds futures now price roughly 88% odds of a December hike versus just ~17% for the 28 October meeting, consistent with 16 of 19 FOMC members' dot-plot calls for a further 2026 move. The same repricing pulled gold down 0.59% to $4,168.90 and silver down 0.96% to $60.12.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 fell 1,964.86 points (-1.18%) to close at 165,124.41, reversing from an intraday high of 166,988.32 after President Asif Ali Zardari approved Governor's Rule/emergency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa on Sunday, with a joint sitting of parliament called for today to ratify the move following the collapse of government-PTI talks over counterterrorism policy. Breadth was lopsided 85 decliners to 13 advancers on thinned volume (104.27m index shares, Rs14.5bn market-wide); United Bank, Oil & Gas Development, Habib Bank and Meezan Bank led the rout, with the 14-bank sub-index alone costing 734 points (37% of the fall). The selloff overshadowed real progress: the IMF's $1.2bn staff-level deal awaits Executive Board approval, the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5%, September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y, and the rupee held steady at 276.97 interbank.

GCC
A Houthi missile strike on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport over the weekend killed more than a dozen people and injured over 300, grounding flights and sending the Tadawul All-Share Index down 0.2% to 10,361 while Qatar's index slid 1.5% to a post-May-2020 low. Brent settled up 1.8% at $106.65 and WTI up 2.0% at $93.74, and OPEC+ held November quotas steady on 4 October. Diversification momentum held firm regardless: Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI sat near a seven-month high of 55.3 in September and the UAE's matched that reading.

GLOBAL
Washington's funding fight stays parked under the continuing resolution through 11 December, though a narrower DHS funding lapse persists separately. The Fed's hawkish dot-plot keeps rate-sensitive equities and precious metals exposed as a December hike firms up.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: KP Governor's Rule heads to a parliamentary vote today — federal government/security apparatus wins administrative control; banks and equities absorb a political-risk discount.
• GCC: UAE VAT-rule overhaul + OPEC+ holding November output steady — compliant corporates and oil producers win; SMEs and broader Gulf equities lose ground after the Riyadh attack.
• Global: US funding certainty through 11 December vs. a ~88%-priced December Fed hike — contractors and bonds stabilise; tech equities and precious metals stay exposed.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 support 164,000 (deeper support 162,500), pivot 165,124 (Monday's close), resistance 167,089 then 168,580 if the KP crisis de-escalates. Our stance: trim leverage into the parliamentary vote, avoid chasing a dead-cat bounce in banks, and watch Brent/WTI and KP headlines as the swing factors for both the rupee and risk appetite.
