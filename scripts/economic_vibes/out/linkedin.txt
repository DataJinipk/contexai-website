KSE-100 clears 166,186 as parliament ratifies KP governor's rule 214-56, while an AI-chip rout rotates Wall Street into the Dow and gold nears $4,170.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
A chip-led sell-off that started in Asia on AI-financing worries pared its losses through the US session: the Dow rose 1.08% to 52,177.69, the S&P 500 added 0.30% to 7,744.06 and the Nasdaq edged up 0.08% to 26,713.03, as strong bank earnings and a bond rally offset the AI jitters. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.09% and the 2-year to 4.76%, yet December Fed hike odds held near 91%. Gold rose 1.6% to $4,169.24 and silver jumped 3.2% to $57.31 on the flight to safety. Oil diverged: WTI jumped 3.03% to $97.97 on an 8.6-million-barrel US inventory draw, while Brent eased 0.13% to $105.92 after the IEA agreed to release over 20 million barrels of diesel and remaining emergency reserves.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 extended its rebound, climbing 768.66 points (+0.46%) to close at 166,186.17, with breadth firmly positive (292 advancers vs 169 decliners) on turnover of 517.6 million shares worth Rs23.57 billion. PTC, FFC, HUBC, OGDC and SYS led, adding roughly 340 points combined, with buying concentrated in banks, energy, autos and power generation. The catalyst: a joint sitting of Parliament voted 214-56 to ratify the Article 232 emergency and Article 234 governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, over loud PTI objections. The rupee held steady at 277.16 interbank, the SBP policy rate remains at 11.5%, September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y, and the IMF's 8 October staff-level agreement on a $1.2bn tranche now awaits Executive Board approval.

GCC
Saudi Arabia's Tadawul All Share Index fell 0.55% to 10,083 on negative breadth (218 decliners vs 99 advancers), even as OPEC+ held November output quotas at 31.01 million bpd and Saudi/UAE non-oil PMI both held at 55.3. The PIF's newly approved 2026-2030 strategy scales back costly megaprojects like The Line to prioritise industry, minerals, AI and tourism amid fiscal pressure from softer oil revenue.

GLOBAL
A bipartisan Senate push, the Trade Review Act, would force congressional sign-off on new tariffs within 60 days; the White House has threatened a veto, underscoring the political cost of the tariff programme heading into the midterms. The IEA's reserve release adds a second supply-side lever easing global diesel and crude costs.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
- Pakistan: Parliament's 214-56 vote ratifies KP governor's rule under Article 234 - the federal government, PPP and equities gain; PTI and KP's provincial autonomy lose.
- GCC: PIF's 2026-2030 strategy reset + OPEC+ holding November output steady - oil producers and financial-return-focused PIF allocations win; megaproject contractors and near-term Gulf equity sentiment (Tadawul -0.55%) lose.
- Global: The Trade Review Act advances to check tariff authority while the IEA releases strategic reserves - import-dependent industries and energy consumers win; executive tariff flexibility and exposed GOP candidates lose.
