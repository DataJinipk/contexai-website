KSE-100 sheds 1,138 points as Hormuz tanker attacks spike oil and rattle Wall Street — even as Pakistan locks in a $1.2bn IMF deal.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US stocks closed mixed Thursday as Iran's escalating attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz sent Brent up 4.0% to $104.28 and WTI up 3.6% to $91.49, reviving inflation fears. The Nasdaq fell 1.25% to 27,193.34 and the S&P 500 lost 0.47% to 7,765.36, while the Dow edged up 0.10% to 51,231.64 on energy strength. Treasury yields held near multi-decade highs (10Y ~5.23%, 2Y ~4.77%) after Fed Governor Christopher Waller said further hikes are "probably necessary" — futures price ~81-83% odds of an October hold but better-than-75% odds of a December hike.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 fell 1,138.50 points (-0.68%) to 167,441.90, reversing from an intraday high of 169,486.60 as United Bank, Fauji Fertilizer, Systems Limited and Engro Fertilizers led the selling; Pakistan State Oil was a rare gainer on the crude spike. Volume thinned to 152.07m shares (Rs17.72bn). The pullback came despite genuine good news: Islamabad and the IMF reached a staff-level agreement on 7 October on the fourth EFF and third RSF reviews, unlocking roughly $1.2bn pending Executive Board approval. The rupee held steady at 277.01 interbank, September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y, and the SBP's policy rate stayed on hold at 11.5%.

GCC
Gulf budgets caught a tailwind from the oil spike, while diversification kept humming: the UAE's S&P Global PMI held at 55.3 in September and Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI jumped to a seven-month high of 55.3 from 53.8. Saudi's PIF approved its 2026-2030 strategy, directing 80% of capital into domestic sectors — tourism, NEOM, advanced manufacturing — as foreign allocation is trimmed to 20% from a 30% peak.

GLOBAL
Washington's funding risk is on ice for now — the continuing resolution signed 2 September funds the government through 11 December — but the Fed's hawkish dot plot (16 of 18 members projecting a further 2026 hike) keeps rate-sensitive equities exposed, and a fresh shutdown fight looms once the stopgap lapses.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: IMF staff-level deal unlocks ~$1.2bn — banks and FX reserves win; importers and borrowers face exchange-rate and rate risk.
• GCC: UAE VAT-compliance crackdown + Saudi PIF's domestic tilt — large compliant corporates and PIF-backed tourism/NEOM win; SMEs and foreign asset managers lose ground.
• Global: US funding certainty through 11 December vs. a likely December Fed hike — contractors and bonds stabilise; growth and tech equities stay exposed.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 support 166,500 (deeper support 165,000), pivot 167,442 (Thursday's close), resistance 168,580 then 169,490. Our stance: hold core positions, avoid chasing strength into resistance, and watch Brent/WTI and Hormuz headlines as the swing factor for both the rupee and risk appetite.
