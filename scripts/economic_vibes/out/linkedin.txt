KSE-100 rebounds 369 points to 169,969 as IMF optimism offsets a hawkish Fed — but 170,000 is still proving sticky.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
Wall Street closed out September mixed: S&P 500 -0.25% to 7,651.54, Dow -0.86% (-443.87 pts) to 50,906.05, Nasdaq +0.24% to 26,861.06. The 10-year Treasury yield sits at 5.29% and the 2-year at 4.92% after the Fed's 25bp hike to 3.75-4% on 16 September — its first increase since July 2023 under new Chair Kevin Warsh. A funding deal signed 2 September keeps Washington open through 11 December. Gold slipped 0.60% to $4,168.01 and silver fell 1.39% to $61.13 as real yields bit into bullion. Brent settled at $97.46 and WTI at $90.60, well below mid-September's Hormuz-driven spike.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 surged as much as 1.12% intraday to 171,492.53 before late profit-taking trimmed gains to a close of 169,969.33, up 368.92 points (+0.22%) on volume of 591.2 million shares. Breadth was positive at 253 advancers to 195 decliners. The rally was driven by optimism around the IMF's fourth EFF/third RSF review (talks began 23 Sep), a new Strategic Action Plan for the local-currency bond market, and a proposal to let private firms import LNG directly. The SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a third straight meeting on 14 September; August CPI printed at 11.1% y/y, with September estimated near 10.2-10.3% y/y ahead of the official PBS release. The rupee firmed a marginal Re0.01 to 277.11 interbank.

GCC
Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI climbed to a six-month high of 57.8 in September as the Crown Prince approved PIF's 2026-2030 strategy, leaning further into tourism, travel and entertainment. OPEC+ held its October output target steady at 31.1 million bpd, confirming the group has fully unwound its voluntary cuts; a formal decision follows at the 4 October meeting. Mid-September's Saudi East-West pipeline shutdown after drone strikes from Iraq, and UAE pump-price increases of up to 16.6% for October, are reminders that the Hormuz risk premium hasn't fully cleared even as regional crude flows recover toward 80% of pre-war levels.

GLOBAL
The EU and US finalised a tariff framework locking in a 15% US tariff on most EU exports (with carve-outs for aircraft, generic pharma and scarce raw materials), while Section 232 steel and aluminium tariffs remain in place. Separately, the SEC's proposed 'Regulation Crypto Assets' framework is in public comment through 20 October.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
Pakistan: IMF review + local bond-market plan + LNG import liberalisation — wins for banks and primary dealers, risk for energy-tariff-exposed consumers.
GCC: PIF 2026-2030 strategy + OPEC+ output hold — wins for non-oil/tourism plays, risk for shippers and insurers still pricing Hormuz.
Global: EU-US tariff deal — wins for carved-out EU exporters and US importers, losses for EU steel/aluminium exporters.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 support 169,600 (deeper support 168,500) · pivot 169,969 · resistance 171,490 (then 172,000). Desk stance: a tactical bounce inside a wider risk-off regime, not a trend reversal — fade strength into 171,000-171,500 and watch for a close back below 169,600.
