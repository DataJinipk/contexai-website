KSE-100 slides 0.81% to 168,022 as the Fed delivers its first rate hike since 2023 — and oil, gold and the IMF calendar all move at once.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
The FOMC voted 12-0 to raise the fed funds target range 25bp to 3.75%-4.00%, its first hike since 2023, as Chair Kevin Warsh flagged persistent inflation. The updated dot plot now points to a median 4.1%-4.4% by year-end 2026, up from 3.6%-4.1% previously — a higher-for-longer signal. Wall Street sold off in response: the Dow fell 631.21 points (−1.21%) to 51,461.90, the S&P 500 dropped 0.45% to 7,551.81, and the Nasdaq slipped 0.01% to 25,978.42. The 10-year Treasury touched a fresh 5.04% high before settling near 4.97%; the 2-year sits near 4.64%.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 fell 1,370.52 points (−0.81%) on Wednesday to close at 168,021.80, giving back most of Tuesday's reserves-driven bounce. Breadth was sharply negative — 320 decliners against 121 advancers on 356.14m shares traded — with FFC, ENGRO, HBL and LUCK the heaviest drags while MLCF and PPL bucked the trend. The State Bank held its policy rate at 11.5% at its September 14 meeting despite August CPI at 11.15% y/y; 6-month KIBOR sits near 11.86%. The rupee held broadly steady near 277.70 interbank. Pakistan and the IMF open talks around September 22-23 on the fourth EFF and third RSF reviews plus the first Article IV consultation in two years, with ~$1.2bn in combined disbursement at stake.

GCC
Brent settled at $105.83 (−2.7%) and WTI at $102.43 (−3.2%) as US officials signalled Saudi Arabia's damaged East-West pipeline would restart within days, easing the acute Hormuz-linked supply scare. OPEC+'s core seven nonetheless held October output unchanged at their September 6 meeting. Non-oil momentum stays firm — Saudi PMI hit a six-month high of 53.8, UAE's composite reading rebounded to 55.3 from 52.7. PIF advanced its 2026-2030 strategy, launching the Gulf Coast Development Company on September 7 for an Al-Khafji coastal project.

GLOBAL
Gold rallied 1.07% to about $4,338/oz and silver jumped 1.65% to roughly $64.70, as safe-haven demand outweighed the hawkish Fed. In Washington, Congress returned for a brief pre-midterm session with renewed pressure to regulate AI, but the legislative window is closing fast as the House adjourns and lawmakers turn to the November 3 midterms.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: IMF's 4th EFF/3rd RSF review + first Article IV consultation (~Sep 22-23) — wins for banks/sovereign credit on the ~$1.2bn disbursement path; loses for importers on a still-elevated oil bill.
• GCC: OPEC+ holds October output steady + PIF's Gulf Coast Development Company — wins for oil exporters and PIF-backed tourism/logistics; loses for oil-importing economies absorbing higher landed costs.
• Global: Fed hikes to 3.75-4.00% as Congress's AI-regulation window closes — wins for the dollar and short-duration savers; loses for long-duration bonds and rate-sensitive growth/tech names.

THE DAY AHEAD

We'd fade strength into resistance and treat a close below support as confirmation that downside momentum is building, especially if the IMF talks stumble.
