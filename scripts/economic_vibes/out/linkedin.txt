KSE-100 climbs 374 points to 177,697 as Wall Street wobbles on a hawkish Fed

Good morning — here's the ContexAi Research desk's read on markets for Monday, 31 August 2026, covering Friday's close.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
The S&P 500 slipped 0.25% to 7,711.76, the Dow was roughly flat (-0.02% to 53,559.99) and the Nasdaq fell 0.52% to 26,402.42 as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote struck a hawkish tone — he flagged that underlying inflation trends haven't meaningfully improved despite a resilient economy. Markets responded by roughly doubling the odds of a September rate hike to a coin-flip; the 10-year Treasury yield firmed to ~4.72% and the 2-year to ~4.34%. Gold held above $4,600/oz and silver spiked toward $71/oz on haven demand into the speech.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 added 373.73 points (0.21%) to close at 177,696.51 on Friday, capping a +530-point (0.3%) week. Ready-market volume rose to 658.3 million shares worth Rs31.45bn; breadth was modestly negative (202 advancers vs 257 decliners) even as banks and Systems Ltd led gains. The SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting, while August CPI is projected near 11.0% y/y — a sharp acceleration worth watching. USD/PKR interbank held steady around 277.51/277.71, and the IMF's 4th EFF and 3rd RSF reviews are set to begin in September, with roughly $1.2bn in combined disbursement in view.

GCC
Brent steadied near $90.1/bbl and WTI near $83.6/bbl as Strait of Hormuz shipping flows reportedly eased. The seven-nation OPEC+ group confirmed a further 188,000 bpd September output increase, with the UAE remaining outside the group after its 1 May exit amid a quota dispute with Riyadh. Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI eased slightly to 53.1 in July (from 53.3) while UAE's improved to 52.7; PIF's 2025 annual report showed profit more than doubling to $17bn with AUM crossing $900bn, underpinning its newly approved 2026-2030 diversification strategy.

GLOBAL
Beyond the Fed, Washington's tariff regime keeps evolving: after the Supreme Court ruled in February that IEEPA doesn't authorise presidential tariffs, the administration rebuilt a 15% global tariff via the time-limited Section 122 mechanism — a fresh cliff-edge due around Q4 2026 that export-dependent economies, including Pakistan's textile sector, should watch closely.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: Finance Act 2026's faceless tax administration and social-media withholding tax — wins for compliance transparency, costs for digital creators.
• GCC: OPEC+ output hike and UAE's exit reshape the group's pricing power.
• Global: Section 122 tariffs extend trade uncertainty into Q4 2026.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 — Support 177,000 (intraday low 177,165; deeper support 176,300) · Pivot 177,697 (Friday's close) · Resistance 178,050 (Friday's high, then 178,700).

Desk stance: Friday's gain was low-conviction — negative breadth on a rising index suggests narrow, large-cap-led buying. We favour a defensive tilt toward banks and export-linked names that benefit from PKR stability and IMF continuity, while watching CPI, the Fed, and Hormuz-linked oil risk.
