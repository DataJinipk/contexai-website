KSE-100 falls for a second day to 172,642 as an unresolved US-Iran conflict, a widening US-Canada trade war and a coin-flip Fed decision weigh on sentiment

Good morning — here's the ContexAi Research desk's read on markets for Wednesday, 09 September 2026, covering Tuesday's close.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US equities fell for a second straight session Tuesday: the S&P 500 slipped 0.58% to 7,673.52, the Dow dropped 1.18% (over 600 points) to 52,786.07 and the Nasdaq eased 0.32% to 26,421.41, as a firmer oil tape and an escalating US-Canada trade war outweighed any Fed relief. The 10-year Treasury briefly topped 4.8% before settling near 4.79%, with the 2-year flat at 4.38%. Gold eased to $4,394/oz and silver held near $65.7/oz. Brent settled near $97.4/bbl and WTI near $92.8/bbl, both close to three-month highs.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 fell 993.92 points (0.57%) to close at 172,642.16 on Tuesday, extending its slide to almost 2,700 points over two sessions as the ongoing US-Iran conflict and firmer oil prices unsettled investors. Breadth stayed negative at 101 advancers to 359 decliners out of 496 names, with UBL, LUCK, MEBL, NBP and PPL the heaviest drags (~485 points) against a partial offset from PSEL, MCB and ATRL (~199 points); volume rose to 722.62 million shares worth Rs27.94bn. On policy, the government's Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2026 is before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue, a governance overhaul the IMF is expected to scrutinise during its fourth EFF/third RSF review mission this month. August CPI held at 11.1% y/y, the SBP's policy rate stays at 11.5%, 6-month KIBOR sits at 11.86%, and USD/PKR interbank held near 277.37.

GCC
Gulf oil benchmarks held near three-month highs as the conflict kept traffic through the Strait of Hormuz severely restricted. OPEC+'s seven core producers — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman, following the UAE's exit from the group in May — held October quotas unchanged at their 6 September meeting. Saudi's PIF is executing its board-approved 2026–2030 strategy, having launched the Gulf Coast Development Company on 7 September to build an integrated coastal destination at Al-Khafji, part of a broader Vision 2030 push into tourism, urban development and clean energy.

GLOBAL
Canada's retaliatory tariffs on roughly $20–27.6bn of US goods — spanning steel, aluminium (duties doubled to 50%), dairy, appliances and pulp and paper — took effect Tuesday, matching Washington's levies dollar-for-dollar; the Trump administration answered with fresh import bans on Canadian dairy, motorcycles and alcohol from 29 September. CME FedWatch odds of a 25bp hike at the 16 September FOMC have eased to roughly 50–56%, with Kalshi and Polymarket both pricing a near coin-flip outcome, as traders weigh Chair Warsh's hawkish tone against fiscal-risk concerns flagged by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: The Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2026 is before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue, tightening governance ahead of the IMF's review mission.
• GCC: OPEC+ held October quotas steady at its 6 September meeting, continuing its roadmap into year-end.
• Global: Canada's retaliatory tariffs on $20–27.6bn of US goods took effect 8 September, doubling steel and aluminium duties to 50%.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 — Support 171,500 (deeper support 170,000) · Pivot 172,642 (Tuesday's close) · Resistance 173,740 (Tuesday's intraday high, then 175,350).

Desk stance: Tuesday's heavy-cap drag from UBL, LUCK, MEBL, NBP and PPL against a thin offsetting gain, rising volume and a roughly 3.5-to-1 negative breadth ratio suggest the two-day, nearly 2,700-point slide reflects sustained distribution.
