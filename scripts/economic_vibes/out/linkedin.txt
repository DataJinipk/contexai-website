KSE-100 rallies 1,647 points to 170,512 on Hormuz relief; oil jumps anew as Saudi pipeline stays shut and Iran-GCC talks are postponed into FOMC week

Good morning — here's the ContexAi Research desk's read on markets for Monday, 14 September 2026, covering Friday's close.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US equities snapped a four-day losing streak Friday: the S&P 500 rose 0.9% (+65.28 pts) to 7,656.98, the Dow gained 1.0% (+509.19 pts) to 52,573.29 and the Nasdaq added 1.0% (+251.31 pts) to 26,333.04, as August CPI (+0.4% m/m, +3.4% y/y) matched forecasts even as core inflation firmed. Fed funds futures still pushed the odds of a 25bp hike at next week's 15-16 September FOMC meeting to roughly 86-90%, up from about 70-72% a day earlier. The 10-year Treasury yield eased slightly to 4.95% after nearing 5% mid-week, while the 2-year held near 4.63%. Brent eased to $104.61/bbl and WTI to $100.05 on Friday's diplomatic hopes; gold held near $4,385.61/oz and silver fell 2.6% to $64.90/oz.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 surged 1,646.81 points (0.98%) to close at 170,511.85 on Friday, clawing back roughly half of Thursday's 3,079-point rout as reports of a planned Iran-GCC meeting on Hormuz lifted sentiment. Volume jumped to 687.47 million shares (Rs33.70bn) with 248 of 500 names advancing against 210 decliners; Meezan Bank, Fauji Fertilizer, Lucky Cement, PPL and OGDC together added 1,032 points. The State Bank's Monetary Policy Committee meets today and is widely expected to hold the policy rate at 11.5%, with August CPI at 11.15% y/y and 6-month KIBOR near 11.87%. USD/PKR interbank was steady around 277.35-277.55. The Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2026 remains before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue ahead of the IMF's fourth EFF/third RSF review mission, expected around 22-23 September.

GCC
Friday's oil relief unwound fast: Oman postponed indefinitely its Monday summit between Iran and the GCC on reopening the Strait of Hormuz after Bahrain declined to attend, while Saudi Arabia's 7m-bpd East-West pipeline — the kingdom's main Hormuz bypass for roughly 5m bpd of crude — remains shut after 10-11 September drone strikes on pump stations near Riyadh and Medina. That follows Houthi strikes that wounded 73 and disrupted Saudi energy sites including the 400,000-bpd Jazan refinery. OPEC+'s seven core producers held October quotas unchanged near 31.01m bpd. Non-oil momentum held up: UAE and Saudi PMIs stood at 55.3 and 53.8 in August, both multi-month highs.

GLOBAL
US equities rebounded Friday as CPI matched forecasts, but a firmer core reading and the weekend's oil shock pushed Fed hike odds for next week's FOMC meeting to roughly 86-90%. Congress's stopgap funding bill already extends federal funding through 11 December, removing near-term shutdown risk. Oil gapped higher in early Monday trade as the Hormuz and pipeline risks compounded.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: The Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2026 remains before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue; the SBP decides on rates today ahead of the IMF's review mission expected around 22-23 September.
• GCC: Oman postponed Iran-GCC Hormuz talks indefinitely on 13 September, while Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline stays shut and OPEC+ holds October quotas steady.
• Global: The Fed's 15-16 September FOMC meeting carries roughly 86-90% odds of a 25bp hike after a firmer core CPI print and a fresh oil shock, with a funding stopgap already passed through 11 December.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 — Support 168,865 (Thursday's close, now first support; deeper support at Friday's low 166,141) · Pivot 170,512 (Friday's close) · Resistance 170,765 (Friday's intraday high, then the psychological 172,000 level).

Desk stance: Friday's rebound was constructive on the numbers, but the de-escalation hopes behind it unravelled over the weekend as talks were postponed and the Saudi pipeline stayed shut. With oil gapping higher and the SBP's rate decision due today ahead of a near-coin-flip FOMC hike, we'd trim broad-beta exposure into both events while retaining selective exposure to E&P and refinery-adjacent names geared to a firmer oil tape, and to non-oil GCC plays benefiting from resilient UAE and Saudi PMIs. A close back below 168,865 would reopen risk toward Friday's 166,141 low. All levels are indicative desk estimates, not recommendations.
