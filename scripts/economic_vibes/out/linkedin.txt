KSE-100 falls for a third straight session to 168,155 as political uncertainty meets a China-driven oil-price surge — even as Wall Street rallies to a Nasdaq record on cooling Fed-hike bets.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US equities surged Friday after September payrolls rose just 29,000 versus 84,000 expected and unemployment ticked up to 4.2%: the S&P 500 gained 0.73% to 7,722.72, the Dow added 0.49% (+250.40 pts) to 51,176.96, and the Nasdaq jumped 1.19% to a record-high 27,190.86. CME FedWatch priced October Fed-hike odds at roughly 17-21%, down from 64% a week earlier, even as the 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.28% and the 2-year at 4.83% following Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish September debut hike to 3.75-4%. A bipartisan continuing resolution keeps Washington funded into early-to-mid December. Gold gave back its early jobs-data bounce to settle near $4,142.00 (-0.83%) and silver fell 1.04% to $60.23 as a firm dollar and elevated yields dominated. Brent settled at $102.25 and WTI at $91.11 after China suspended oil-product exports, tightening global fuel supply on top of lingering Strait of Hormuz disruption.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 fell for a third straight session, down 481.36 points (-0.29%) Friday to 168,155.49, after shedding 1,332.47 points (-0.78%) Thursday — a combined roughly 1,814-point slide. Total market volume slipped to 493.96 million shares on traded value of Rs17.49 billion, with decliners leading advancers 321 to 128 (45 unchanged) across 494 names; Commercial Banks dragged the index down 121.77 points while Oil & Gas Exploration Companies added 72.68 points. The index opened Monday under renewed pressure, falling over 1,600 points intraday to 166,554.66 as political uncertainty and the oil-price surge persisted. The SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a third straight meeting on 14 September as September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y (from August's 11.1%), and the rupee firmed 3 paisa to 277.07 interbank. An IMF mission under Iva Petrova continues the fourth EFF/third RSF review, kicked off by Finance Minister Aurangzeb on 29 September; a successful conclusion would unlock roughly $1.2 billion combined.

GCC
Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI held at a six-month high of 57.8 in September as the Crown Prince's PIF 2026-2030 strategy reallocated roughly $41 billion from construction into Hormuz-immune digital infrastructure, with non-oil activity now 55% of non-oil GDP. OPEC+'s seven core producers held November output steady at 31.01 million bpd on 4 October, extending October's pause after four consecutive monthly hikes, with Gulf members still pumping only 60-80% of normal levels amid continuing Strait of Hormuz export disruption; the group next meets 1 November.

GLOBAL
A weak September jobs report reset Fed-hike expectations for October to roughly 17-21%, even as yields stayed elevated and Chair Warsh's dot plot continues to flag one further 2026 hike. Washington's bipartisan continuing resolution removes shutdown risk into the midterm run-up. China's suspension of oil-product exports added a fresh supply-tightening impulse to global crude markets.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
Pakistan: IMF fourth EFF/third RSF review + federal asset-declaration mandate — wins for banks and fiscal credibility if the $1.2bn tranche unlocks, risk for consumers facing energy-tariff adjustments.
GCC: PIF 2026-2030 capex pivot + OPEC+ November output hold — wins for digital-infrastructure and tourism plays, risk for construction firms and Hormuz-exposed shippers/insurers.
Global: US continuing resolution + Fed's hawkish hike-and-hold — wins for federally funded contractors and large-cap tech, risk for rate-sensitive housing and small-caps.
