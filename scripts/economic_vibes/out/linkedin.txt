KSE-100 sheds 2,547 points as Hormuz tanker strike and a hospital transfer rattle sentiment

Good morning. Here's the Economic Vibes desk read for Wednesday, 19 August 2026.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
Wall Street fell Tuesday as a chip-stock sell-off compounded pressure from elevated oil prices and bond yields: S&P 500 -0.69% to 7,691.76, Dow -0.22% to 53,343.40, Nasdaq -1.33% to 26,289.71. The 10-year Treasury yield stood at 4.74% and the 2-year at 4.19%, with the 30-year still near a 19-year high above 5.3%. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish July tone -- nine of 18 FOMC participants project a 2026 hike -- keeps a September rate hike in play.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 tumbled 2,546.94 points (1.41%) to close at 177,955.51, wiping out Monday's gain after the Supreme Court ordered former PM Imran Khan's transfer to Shifa International Hospital for medical examination, compounding fading US-Iran deal prospects. Selling was broad -- banks, oil & gas, cement, fertiliser, power and autos all fell -- on volume of 1.03bn shares worth Rs50.7bn. July CPI eased to 9.2% y/y and SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting. An IMF mission is expected next month for the fourth EFF/second RSF review, with roughly $1.2bn in view.

GCC
A vessel transiting the Strait of Hormuz near Oman was struck by a projectile, sustaining engine-room damage and a crew casualty, after the 60-day US-Iran negotiation window lapsed without progress; Iran says the strait stays "closed" until US sanctions conditions are addressed. Brent held near $91.0/bbl on the risk premium. Seven OPEC+ members are raising output by 188,000 bpd from August, and Saudi's non-oil private sector stayed expansionary in July (PMI 53.1), underpinning the PIF's 2026-2030 diversification push.

GLOBAL
WTI held near $85.0/bbl alongside Brent's climb, while gold eased 0.6% to $4,388/oz (silver -1.2% to $64.90) as higher yields capped haven demand despite the risk-off mood. Both remain well off 2026's highs.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
- Pakistan: IMF mission prep for the ~$1.2bn EFF/RSF tranche -- wins for exporters if it clears on schedule, headwind for utilities under circular-debt reform pressure.
- GCC: OPEC+ 188,000 bpd output hike amid the Hormuz tanker attack -- wins for Gulf budgets and exporters, headwind for shippers and insurers exposed to transit risk.
- Global: Hormuz tanker strike + Warsh's hawkish tilt toward a September hike -- wins for energy and the dollar, headwind for rate-sensitive equities and EM borrowers.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 support 177,000 (below Tuesday's intraday low of 177,866) - pivot 177,956 (Tuesday's close) - resistance 180,600 (Tuesday's high), then 181,000. We read Tuesday's move as genuine de-risking -- selling was broad-based and triggered by confirmed news, not rumour -- arguing for a cautious near-term stance until Hormuz headlines stabilise.
