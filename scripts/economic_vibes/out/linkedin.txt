🌅 Economic Vibes — Morning Brief | Wednesday, 05 August 2026

Wall Street closed at records overnight while the KSE-100 took a breather — here's what the desk is watching this morning.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
S&P 500 +1.79% to 7,736.52, Dow +1.71% to 54,085.88 (first close above 54,000), Nasdaq +2.59% to 26,584.99 — all-time highs driven by easing US-Iran tensions over the Strait of Hormuz and a tech rebound. US 10Y sits at 4.63%, 2Y at 4.21%, with markets pricing rising odds of a Fed hike (not a cut) in September after Chair Kevin Warsh's FOMC voted 9-3 to hold on 29 July.

PAKISTAN
KSE-100 slipped 0.63% to 177,083.22 (−1,116.80 pts) on institutional profit-taking in banks, E&P and cement, as breadth turned negative (161 advancers vs 295 decliners, 711.3m shares, Rs25.9bn) — though foreign investors stayed net buyers (Rs166.3m). UBL, Engro Holdings, OGDC, Lucky Cement and PPL led the drag; Meezan Bank, PSO and Power Cement held up. On the macro side: CPI eased to 9.2% YoY in July from 11.1%, and the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting. The IMF's Executive Board cleared the third EFF/second RSF review in May, unlocking roughly $1.3bn combined; the next review is expected in H2 2026.

GCC
Seven OPEC+ producers added 188,000 bpd of August supply even as Brent (−5.9% to $78.87) and WTI (down 5%+) tumbled on Hormuz de-escalation hopes. Saudi's non-oil PMI held at 53.1 in July; the UAE's cooled toward its softest pace in years on regional disruptions — a mixed but still-expansionary read for Vision 2030 diversification.

GLOBAL
Gold is holding near $4,076, silver near $58.74, as Fed policy uncertainty keeps havens bid even with equities at records. Washington's trade agenda is heating up: a 50% tariff on Canadian goods lands 19 August, and Section 301 forced-labor tariffs (10–12.5% on ~60 economies) are already in force since 24 July.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
Pakistan's Finance Act 2026 brings a 5% withholding tax (10% for non-filers) on social media earnings, now being deducted at source by banks — a cost for creators, a win for compliance-tech vendors.
Saudi's new Companies Law and the UAE's Cabinet Decision 59/2026 competition rules tighten disclosure — wins for institutional investors, added overhead for SMEs.
US tariffs on Canada (50%) and forced-labor goods (10–12.5%, ~60 economies) raise input costs for importers and retailers even as they shield targeted US manufacturers.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100: support 176,000 (deeper at 174,500), pivot 177,083 (Tuesday's close), resistance 178,200 (Monday's close/high, then 179,500).
Desk view: Tuesday's dip reads as profit-taking, not reversal — falling oil is a tailwind for Pakistan's import bill, and global risk appetite stays supportive. All levels are indicative desk estimates, not recommendations.
