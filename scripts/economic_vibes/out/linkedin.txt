PSX reopens today after its Independence Day break as Wall Street cools from record highs and oil, gold firm on Fed Chair Warsh's hawkish tone.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US equities eased back from record territory Friday — the S&P 500 slipped 0.2% to 7,785.76, the Dow shed 0.2% to 53,732.41 and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.3% to 26,729.16 — after a preliminary University of Michigan reading showed August consumer sentiment tumbling to 51 from July's 55.2, well below the 55 expected. Even so, the S&P notched a third straight weekly gain. The 10-year Treasury yield rose 5bp to about 4.67% (30-year 5.27%) and the 2-year held near 4.17%, as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh kept a hawkish tone on the Fed's strict 2% inflation target ahead of the September 15-16 FOMC meeting, even as prediction markets price only a roughly 26% chance of a hike. Brent and WTI firmed to $88.52 and $82.40 as OPEC+'s finalised September output hike failed to fully offset a lingering Middle East risk premium. Gold rose 0.53% to $4,373.50/oz and silver gained 0.32% to $64.53/oz as haven demand held firm against the still-hawkish Fed backdrop.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 resumes trading today unchanged from Thursday's close of 180,104.61, after the Pakistan Stock Exchange stayed shut Friday for the Independence Day holiday. Thursday's session itself closed 206.60 points lower (-0.11%, -0.73% on the week) as profit-taking hit fertiliser and pharmaceutical counters even as oil & gas exploration and cement held firm; breadth was negative with 269 decliners against 197 advancers (29 unchanged) on turnover of 891.33 million shares worth Rs36.69bn, led by Cnergyico (+4.35%) and Service Industries (+3.91%) among gainers against Systems Ltd (-1.59%) and Engro Holdings (-0.94%) among laggards. SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight review, with July CPI at 9.2% even as food inflation ran hotter at 10.6%. Six-month KIBOR stood near 11.51/11.76% as of August 7. USD/PKR interbank was last quoted at 277.65 Thursday, with banks and the exchange closed Friday. An IMF mission is due in Islamabad for the fourth EFF review and second RSF review, with roughly $1.2bn combined at stake, with tax collection, energy reforms and privatisation progress the key tests.

GCC
OPEC+ finalised a further 188,000 bpd output increase for September, completing the rollback of 2023's voluntary cuts, with delegates signalling quotas will likely hold steady through year-end pending the next JMMC review on October 4. Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI eased slightly to 53.1 in July (from 53.3) but stayed firmly expansionary for a fourth straight month, even as export orders fell for a fifth month on high freight costs. The PIF, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, is executing its 2026-2030 strategy across Vision, Strategic and Financial portfolios, prioritising tourism, travel, entertainment and sport — including a July tie-up between SURJ Sports Investment, Live Nation and Oak View Group to launch Radia, and a $2bn co-investment MoU with I Squared Capital.

GLOBAL
A hawkish Fed and a firm Middle East risk premium are the two swing factors reshaping global risk sentiment. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has held rates steady since June but continues to stress the Fed's strict 2% inflation target, keeping yields elevated even as prediction markets lean toward a September hold.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: IMF mission for 4th EFF / 2nd RSF review due next month (~$1.2bn at stake) — wins: banks, FX reserves, sovereign risk; loses: energy consumers facing tariff conditionality.
• GCC: OPEC+ finalises 188,000 bpd September hike, next review Oct 4; PIF's 2026-2030 strategy backs tourism/entertainment/sport — wins: Saudi non-oil diversification plays, producer revenue; loses: oil-importing economies including Pakistan.
• Global: Fed Chair Warsh's hawkish tone vs ~26% priced odds of a September hike — wins: EM FX including the PKR, rate-sensitive equities if the Fed holds; loses: risk assets and short-duration yield-seekers if hawkish rhetoric hardens into a hike.
