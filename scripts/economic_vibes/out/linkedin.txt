KSE-100 sheds 825 points as the Hormuz risk premium widens — and the IMF just heard about it directly.

Economic Vibes · Wednesday, 30 September 2026

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
Wall Street drifted lower as the bond selloff extended: S&P 500 -0.17% to 7,671, Dow -0.25% to 51,350, Nasdaq -0.09% to 26,797. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.28% and the 30-year touched its highest level since 2002 at 5.61%. Losses were cushioned after NY Fed President John Williams floated a December pause, though futures still price 68% odds of an October hike and 95% for December. Gold (+1.42% to $4,172.40) and silver (+1.23% to $61.27) rebounded from Monday's selloff.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 fell 0.48% (-825.22 pts) to 169,600.40 as reports that Washington rejected an Iranian ceasefire proposal on the Strait of Hormuz drove broad-based selling across autos, cement, banks, fertiliser, E&P and OMCs. Breadth was deeply negative: 305 decliners vs 154 advancers. The government briefed the IMF that Gulf-conflict disruptions have already caused revenue losses and slowed economic activity. SBP holds its policy rate at 11.5% for a third straight meeting; CPI stayed in double digits at 11.15% YoY in August. Today is also the FBR's final tax return deadline for salaried individuals — no extension granted, penalties jumping to Rs25,000.

GCC
Brent held near $105.31 on the Hormuz premium while WTI eased 3.48% to $89.38 on SPR tapping. OPEC+'s seven core producers held October output at September levels, with Saudi near 9.89mn bpd and the next meeting set for 4 October. Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI cooled to 56.6 from 57.7 — still a 25th straight month of growth, but decelerating.

GLOBAL
New Section 338 tariff revisions and Canadian import restrictions took effect 29 September. Congress's August stopgap funding bill keeps federal agencies funded through 11 December, averting a shutdown at today's fiscal year-end — but the fiscal cliff is deferred, not resolved.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: FBR tax deadline today — wins: compliant filers, revenue documentation. Loses: IRIS-delayed and late filers facing a 25x penalty jump.
• GCC: OPEC+ output hold — wins: Gulf oil exporters on a firmer Brent floor. Loses: oil-importing economies including Pakistan.
• Global: US funding bill through 11 Dec — wins: federal contractors. Loses: Canadian exporters, duration-sensitive equities.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 support 169,000 (deeper at 168,200) · pivot 169,600 (Tuesday's close) · resistance 170,900 (Tuesday's open), then 171,700. The desk reads this as an external, oil-and-rates-driven risk-off move rather than a local growth story. All levels are indicative desk estimates, not recommendations.
