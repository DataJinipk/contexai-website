KSE-100 plunges 3,079 points to 168,865 as Brent tops $101 on Hormuz tanker war, Wall Street falls a fourth day on hot PPI

Good morning — here's the ContexAi Research desk's read on markets for Friday, 11 September 2026, covering Thursday's close.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US equities fell for a fourth straight session: the S&P 500 slipped 0.58% to 7,591.70, the Dow dropped 0.60% (-316.56 pts) to 52,064.10 and the Nasdaq eased 0.65% to 26,081.72, as August PPI data (+0.4% m/m, +5.4% y/y) reaccelerated wholesale inflation and oil topped $100/bbl. The 10-year Treasury yield jumped nearly 10bps to 4.94%, its highest since October 2023, while the 2-year held near 4.38% as fed funds futures priced roughly a 71% chance of a 25bp hike at next week's 15-16 September FOMC meeting under Chair Kevin Warsh. Gold eased to $4,383/oz (-0.3%) and silver to $66.62/oz. Brent settled at $101.21/bbl (+3.4%), its highest close since May, and WTI climbed to $96.05.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 plunged 3,078.55 points (1.79%) to close at 168,865.04 on Thursday, its steepest single-day fall in weeks, as intensifying US-Iran tanker strikes near the Strait of Hormuz and a surging oil tape triggered panic selling led by Pakistan Refinery, SSGC and TPL. Ready-market volume surged to 628.67 million shares (Rs27.02bn) from Wednesday's 477.67 million, with only 58 of 499 traded names advancing. On policy, the government's Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2026 remains before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue ahead of the IMF's fourth EFF/third RSF review mission due 23 September. August CPI held elevated at 11.15% y/y, the SBP's policy rate stays at 11.5% ahead of Monday's rate announcement, 6-month KIBOR sits near 11.87%, and USD/PKR interbank was broadly steady at 277.35.

GCC
Gulf oil benchmarks surged as the US-Iran conflict intensified around the Strait of Hormuz, with Brent settling at $101.21/bbl, its highest close since May, even as OPEC+'s seven core producers held October quotas unchanged at their 6 September meeting, keeping the combined ceiling near 31.01m bpd. Non-oil momentum held up well despite the war premium: the UAE's PMI hit an August reading of 55.3, its best since December 2024, and Saudi Arabia's PMI rose to a six-month high of 53.8.

GLOBAL
US equities fell for a fourth consecutive session as hot PPI data and oil above $100/bbl weighed on sentiment, while Congress has already passed a stopgap funding bill extending federal funding through 11 December, removing near-term shutdown risk. Traders are pricing a near-coin-flip 15-16 September FOMC decision under Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, who struck a hawkish tone at Jackson Hole. Gold and silver eased as investors squared positions ahead of the Fed decision.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: The Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2026 remains before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue, tightening governance ahead of the IMF's review mission due 23 September.
• GCC: OPEC+ held October quotas steady at its 6 September meeting, prioritising stability as Brent trades above $101/bbl.
• Global: The Fed's 15-16 September FOMC meeting is a near-coin-flip on a 25bp hike after hot PPI data, with a funding stopgap already passed through 11 December.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 — Support 168,472 (Thursday's intraday low; deeper support 167,000) · Pivot 168,865 (Thursday's close) · Resistance 171,946 (Thursday's intraday high, then Wednesday's close near 171,944).

Desk stance: Thursday's surge in volume alongside the sharpest single-day loss in weeks points to genuine distribution rather than a technical wobble, as Brent above $101/bbl on an unresolved Hormuz tanker war and a hot PPI print reinforcing a near-coin-flip FOMC decision both remain unresolved risks. With the index holding just above the 168,000 psychological floor, we'd stay defensive on broad beta into next week's SBP rate decision and FOMC, while retaining selective exposure to E&P and refinery-adjacent names geared to a firmer oil tape and to non-oil GCC plays benefiting from resilient UAE and Saudi PMIs.
