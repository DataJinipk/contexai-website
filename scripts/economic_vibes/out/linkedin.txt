KSE-100 dips 200 points — but a Moody's sovereign upgrade to B3 changes the medium-term story

Good morning. Tuesday, 25 August 2026. Here's the desk's read on overnight and Monday's close.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
Wall Street was split Monday: the Dow added 0.3% to 53,417.16, the S&P 500 slipped 0.3% to 7,652.86 and the Nasdaq fell 0.8% to 25,980.19 as chip stocks sold off (Micron -5.8%, AMD and Broadcom down more than 3% and 2%). The 10-year Treasury eased to ~4.72% and the 2-year to ~4.19% as markets look ahead to Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole address on Friday, 28 August — nineteen days before the 16 September FOMC decision. Gold held firm near $4,652-4,657/oz and silver near $69/oz.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 slipped 199.84 points (0.11%) to close at 176,966.69, swinging between an intraday high of 178,122.88 and a low of 176,735.57. Profit-taking in ENGROH, MEBL, SRVI, SYS and MCB (-453 points combined) offset gains in OGDC, PPL, MLCF, DGKC and FCCL (+392 points); volume held firm at 930m shares worth Rs44bn, led by CNERGY. The session followed Moody's upgrade of Pakistan's sovereign rating to B3 from Caa1 (stable outlook), citing FX reserves near $17bn (from $14bn a year earlier) and interest payments falling to ~35% of revenue from 49%. July CPI eased to 9.2% y/y, the SBP held its rate at 11.5%, and an IMF mission is expected in September for the next EFF/RSF review (~$1.2bn in view).

GCC
Brent fell 2.5% to $92.06/bbl and WTI to $84.89/bbl even as Washington unveiled its toughest-ever Iran sanctions package, with Tehran warning of possible Hormuz ship seizures. OPEC+ continued a fifth straight 188,000 bpd monthly output increase, while Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI eased slightly to 53.1 in July. PIF's 2026-2030 strategy, published 12 August, deepens its tourism and private-sector-led diversification push, on the heels of its completed Electronic Arts acquisition.

GLOBAL
The US rolled out "Operation Economic Outcast" against Iran — Treasury Secretary Bessent's "economic D-Day" — while Canada vowed retaliatory tariffs after President Trump threatened a 50% levy on Canadian autos, parts and steel.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
- Pakistan: Moody's B3 upgrade + SBP hold at 11.5% + September IMF review. Wins: bondholders, banks, exporters. Loses: leveraged borrowers still at 11.5%.
- GCC: OPEC+ output rollover + PIF 2026-2030 strategy. Wins: PIF-linked tourism/gaming, oil exporters. Loses: Saudi non-oil momentum, shippers/insurers.
- Global: US Iran sanctions + Canada tariff retaliation. Wins: gold, havens, compliance advisors. Loses: chipmakers, autos/steel exporters.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 support 175,000 (deeper support 173,500) · pivot 176,967 (Monday's close) · resistance 178,100 (Monday's intraday high, then 178,700). We'd fade weakness toward support and take a cautious approach above resistance pending clarity from Jackson Hole and the September IMF mission.
