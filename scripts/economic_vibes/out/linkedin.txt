KSE-100 falls a third day to 171,944 as Brent tops $100/bbl on intensifying Hormuz strikes and Wall Street extends its slide

Good morning — here's the ContexAi Research desk's read on markets for Thursday, 10 September 2026, covering Wednesday's close.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US equities fell for a third straight session: the S&P 500 slipped 0.48% to 7,636.46, the Dow dropped 0.77% to 52,381.02 and the Nasdaq eased 0.64% to 26,253.34, as rising oil prices and firm Treasury yields pressured margins and growth names. The 10-year Treasury held near 4.80% and the 2-year near 4.40%, both close to multi-month highs, ahead of a coin-flip 16 September FOMC decision under Chair Kevin Warsh. Gold reclaimed $4,400/oz (+1.1%) and silver rose to $66.58/oz. Brent topped $100/bbl for the first time since July and WTI climbed to about $96.2.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 shed 698.56 points (0.40%) to close at 171,943.60 on Wednesday, extending its slide to nearly 3,400 points over three sessions as the widening US-Iran conflict and surging oil prices drove broad-based selling in banks, E&P, cement, fertiliser and OMC names, led by UBL, OGDC, PPL, MCB, Fauji Fertiliser and Lucky Cement. Ready-market volume eased to 477.67 million shares from Tuesday's 722.62 million. On policy, the government's Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2026 remains before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue ahead of the IMF's fourth EFF/third RSF review mission this month. August CPI jumped to 11.15% y/y from July's four-month low of 9.2%, the SBP's policy rate stays at 11.5%, 6-month KIBOR sits at 11.86%, and USD/PKR interbank has drifted to roughly 279.40 from Tuesday's 277.37.

GCC
Gulf oil benchmarks surged as the US-Iran conflict intensified around the Strait of Hormuz, with Brent topping $100/bbl for the first time since July even as OPEC+'s seven core producers held October quotas unchanged at their 6 September meeting, keeping the combined ceiling near 31.01m bpd. Non-oil momentum held up well despite the war premium: the UAE's PMI hit an August reading of 55.3, its best since December 2024, and Saudi Arabia's PMI rose to a six-month high of 53.8. Saudi's PIF launched the Gulf Coast Development Company on 7 September, part of its board-approved 2026–2030 strategy under Vision 2030.

GLOBAL
US equities fell for a third consecutive session as oil and yields weighed on sentiment, while Congress continues negotiating a stopgap funding bill to avert a shutdown at the 30 September fiscal year-end. Traders are pricing a coin-flip 16 September FOMC decision under new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, balancing hawkish jobs data against Governor Waller's dovish lean. Gold and silver extended their safe-haven bid against this hawkish-Fed, oil-shock backdrop.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: The Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2026 remains before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue, tightening governance ahead of the IMF's review mission.
• GCC: OPEC+ held October quotas steady at its 6 September meeting, prioritising stability as Brent trades above $100/bbl.
• Global: US Congress works toward a stopgap funding bill ahead of the 30 September deadline, alongside a finely balanced 16 September FOMC.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 — Support 171,000 (near Wednesday's intraday low of 171,802; deeper support 169,500) · Pivot 171,944 (Wednesday's close) · Resistance 173,174 (Wednesday's intraday high, then Tuesday's close near 172,642).

Desk stance: Wednesday's thinner volume alongside a third consecutive loss suggests the three-day, nearly 3,400-point slide is losing some distribution intensity even as Brent above $100/bbl and a finely balanced FOMC remain unresolved. With the index holding just above the 171,000 psychological floor, we'd stay defensive on broad beta into the FOMC while retaining selective exposure to banks and E&P names geared to a firmer oil tape, alongside SWF-adjacent names positioned to benefit from tighter governance.
