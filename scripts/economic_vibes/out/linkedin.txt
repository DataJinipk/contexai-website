🌅 Economic Vibes — Morning Brief | Monday, 10 August 2026

Wall Street closed last week at a record even as a weak July jobs report muddied the Fed's path, oil firmed on an unresolved Hormuz standoff, and the KSE-100 paused after a four-session rally — here's what the desk is watching this morning.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
S&P 500 +0.62% to a record 7,757.64, Nasdaq +1.30% to 26,690.62, and Dow +0.28% to 54,036.93 on Friday, after July nonfarm payrolls unexpectedly fell 23,000 and May-June prints were revised down a combined 103,000. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.64% (2-year 4.20%), even as the Fed's own 29 July decision was a divided 9-3 hold at 3.50%-3.75%, with three dissenters pushing for a hike.

PAKISTAN
KSE-100 eased 0.19% (−346.57 pts) to close at 181,430.02, snapping a four-session advance as profit-taking hit MCB and UBL even as MARI, CNERGY and BOP led gainers; breadth turned negative at 207 advancers vs 260 decliners on turnover of 716.04m shares (Rs34.1bn). CPI eased to 9.2% YoY in July from 11.1% in June, and the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting; six-month KIBOR tracks near 11.70–11.90% (desk est.). USD/PKR held steady near 277.85 interbank, and SBP-held FX reserves rose to $17.04bn.

GCC
OPEC+ confirmed a further 188,000 bpd increase for September, a sixth straight monthly hike ahead of a planned Q4 pause. Saudi's non-oil PMI held at 53.1 in July, a fourth consecutive month of expansion, while the PIF continues rolling out its 2026-2030 strategy toward tourism, entertainment and localisation. Oil firmed on the back of it — Brent +1.3% to $83.55, WTI +1.15% to $78.18 — as Strait of Hormuz reopening talks between Iran and Oman remained unresolved.

GLOBAL
Gold climbed to roughly $4,350/oz (+2.4%), and silver rose to $63.54, as the weak payrolls print revived haven demand despite record US equities. Washington's trade agenda keeps moving: a 50% Section 338 tariff on Canadian dairy, alcohol and motor-vehicle exports lands in nine days (19 August), while Pakistan's own reciprocal US tariff sits at a comparatively favourable 19%.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
Pakistan faces a 16 July EU GSP+ compliance review on labour rights, with India's zero-tariff EU access threatening to undercut Pakistani textile exporters from 2027.
OPEC+'s September output hike and Saudi's PIF 2026-2030 strategy reshape Gulf energy and sovereign-wealth positioning.
The 19 August Section 338 tariff step-up on Canadian goods contrasts with Pakistan's steadier 19% US tariff rate.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100: support 180,000 (psychological, deeper at 178,500), pivot 181,430 (Friday's close), resistance 181,780 (Thursday's close, then the 2026 high of 189,167 set in January).
Desk view: Friday's dip looks like digestion after a rally of more than 3% since 20 July, with oil's Hormuz-driven firmness the key swing factor for Pakistan's import bill and disinflation path. All levels are indicative desk estimates, not recommendations.
