KSE-100 slips 1,109 points on refinery-pricing jitters as Wall Street ticks up on Treasury's bond-market rescue

Good morning. Here's the Economic Vibes desk read for Thursday, 20 August 2026.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
Wall Street edged higher Wednesday after the US Treasury said it would double liquidity-support buybacks of longer-dated debt to at least $4bn per operation from September: S&P 500 +0.2% to 7,707.98, Dow +0.2% to 53,463.05, Nasdaq +0.2% to 26,331.09. Long-dated yields pulled back from multi-decade highs -- the 10-year eased to roughly 4.65%, the 30-year to near 5.19% -- putting fresh pressure on Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish tilt (nine of 18 FOMC participants project a 2026 hike).

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 slipped 1,109.14 points (0.62%) to close at 176,846.36, giving back an early rally that had lifted the index to an intraday high of 178,942.31, after media reports that the petroleum ministry would negotiate diesel-price cuts with local refineries triggered broad selling in refinery, banking, fertiliser and E&P names -- UBL, Engro Holdings, HBL, FFC, Attock Refinery, Meezan Bank, Mari Energies, Systems, Cnergyico and National Bank together erased 859 points. Breadth turned negative (185 gainers vs 271 losers) on volume of 787.9m shares worth Rs39.6bn. July CPI eased to 9.2% y/y and SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting; an IMF mission is expected in September for the next EFF/RSF review, with roughly $1.2bn in view.

GCC
Brent held near $92.4/bbl and WTI near $85.6/bbl, extending a fourth straight session of gains, as Strait of Hormuz shipping traffic collapsed to roughly three vessels over the weekend against a pre-conflict norm near 130 daily. Seven OPEC+ members continue raising output by 188,000 bpd a month, and Saudi Arabia's non-oil private sector held a fourth straight month of expansion in July (PMI 53.1). Separately, the PIF board approved its 2026-2030 strategy, prioritising tourism, travel and entertainment alongside deeper private-sector participation.

GLOBAL
Gold jumped roughly 3.7% to $4,493/oz on the lower-yield, softer-dollar backdrop even as silver eased to $63.87/oz. The Treasury's buyback move is effectively easing financial conditions the Fed itself has resisted loosening, a tension likely to dominate the next FOMC debate.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
- Pakistan: Petroleum ministry in talks with refineries on diesel-price cuts -- wins for consumers and exporters if the IMF tranche clears, headwind for refiners and rate-sensitive banks.
- GCC: OPEC+ output hike plus PIF's new 2026-2030 strategy amid a near-total Hormuz transit halt -- wins for Gulf budgets and PIF-linked tourism names, headwind for shippers and insurers.
- Global: Treasury doubles bond buybacks, pressuring Fed Chair Warsh's hawkish stance -- wins for duration assets and gold, headwind for the Fed-independence narrative and the dollar.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 support 176,600 (below Wednesday's intraday low of 176,638) - pivot 176,846 (Wednesday's close) - resistance 178,942 (Wednesday's high), then 180,000. We read Wednesday's reversal as sector-specific rather than broad risk-off, arguing for a cautious-but-not-defensive stance while watching whether the 176,600-176,850 band holds.
