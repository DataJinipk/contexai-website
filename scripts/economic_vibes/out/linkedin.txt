KSE-100 notches a third straight gain to 170,765.22 as Wall Street closes out a strong week, even as the 10-year Treasury yield hits a 19-year high and oil snaps back above $105 after Trump rejects Iran's Hormuz offer.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US equities finished Friday higher across the board: S&amp;P 500 +0.51% to 7,743.41, Dow +0.93% to 51,828.62, Nasdaq +0.5% to 27,068.72, all three posting weekly gains on fresh tech optimism. The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.17%, its highest since 2007, with the 2-year at 4.81%, as markets price roughly 70% odds of an October Fed hike under Chair Kevin Warsh.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 added 266.28 points (+0.16%) to 170,765.22, a third consecutive positive session, on narrowing breadth (57 up, 41 down) as banks, OMCs and cement led (HBL, UBL, BAFL, BAHL, PSO) against declines in tech, power and pharma names (MEBL, FFC, HUBC). Volume eased to 212.46m shares on Rs19.28bn traded value; PSO confirmed a Rs10/share dividend alongside lower earnings. The IMF's fourth EFF / third RSF review mission, led by Iva Petrova, is in Islamabad through early October examining fiscal performance and the Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill still before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue; wheat and sugar market intervention was flagged as a policy slippage. August CPI held at 11.15% y/y; the SBP kept its policy rate at 11.5% on 14 September; 6-month KIBOR sits at 11.72/11.97%.

GCC
Saudi Arabia's PMI held at a six-month high of 53.8 in August; the UAE's non-oil PMI hit 55.3, its fastest pace since December 2024 — both resilient even as Trump rejected Iran's latest proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days, sending Brent back above $105 on Monday. The region is also absorbing the fallout from Houthi strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure earlier this month. OPEC+'s JMMC meets 4 October to review production policy, while PIF continues executing its 2026-2030 strategy across tourism, NEOM and advanced manufacturing.

GLOBAL
Brent settled Friday at $104.32 and WTI at $92.41, both down on brief US-Iran truce hopes, before reversing Monday to roughly $105.8/$94.1 (EST.) on Trump's rejection of Iran's Hormuz offer. Gold edged up 0.12% to $4,280.19 and silver gained 0.33% to $64.04. Washington's tariff agenda widens 29 September: the 100% Section 232 duty on patented pharmaceuticals and APIs extends to all remaining importers, having applied to large Annex III firms since 31 July; generics and biosimilars stay exempt pending a mandated one-year review.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
Pakistan: Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill remains in Senate committee under IMF scrutiny — wins: institutional investors, IMF compliance; loses: SOE management facing tighter oversight.
GCC: OPEC+ JMMC meets 4 October amid renewed Hormuz tension — wins: non-oil diversification plays; loses: export-constrained Gulf energy exporters.
Global: Section 232 pharma tariff widens from 29 September — wins: onshoring pharma majors, generics; loses: mid-size branded importers newly exposed.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 support 169,900 (deeper support 168,700), pivot 170,765 (Friday's close), resistance 171,160 (then Thursday's 172,491 high). The desk reads Friday's move as a continuation of the three-session recovery rather than a breakout, with Monday's oil reversal, a 19-year-high 10-year yield and roughly 70% October hike odds arguing for a cautious, rate- and energy-sensitive session.
