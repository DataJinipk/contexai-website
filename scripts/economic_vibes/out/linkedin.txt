KSE-100 grinds 0.23% higher to 175,329 as hot US jobs data revives Fed hike bets and Brent nears $97 on Iran-Kuwait strikes

Good morning — here's the ContexAi Research desk's read on markets for Monday, 07 September 2026, covering Friday's close.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
The S&P 500 fell 0.38% to 7,718.60, the Dow lost 0.51% to 53,414.25 and the Nasdaq eased 0.29% to 26,506.99 on Friday after a blowout August payrolls report — +162,000 jobs versus 53,000 expected, unemployment steady at 4.1% — pushed the 10-year Treasury yield to 4.78% and the 2-year to 4.34%, lifting the odds of a hike at the 15–16 September FOMC to near 60% on CME FedWatch, even as Fed Governor Waller argues the committee can "wait one meeting" for disinflation. Gold slid 1.3% to $4,420/oz and silver eased to $66.30 as the dollar firmed. Brent pushed toward $97.5/bbl and WTI near $92.6/bbl on renewed Iran-Kuwait strikes, approaching three-month highs.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 added 399.14 points (0.23%) to close at 175,328.82 on Friday, swinging between 174,515 and 175,418 intraday. Ready-market volume rose to 874.32 million shares worth Rs32.42bn, with breadth split 235 advancers to 218 decliners. MEBL, BAHL, MARI, PPL and OGDC led gainers while UBL, ATRL, PSEL, Cnergyico and FFC lagged as rising oil prices kept investors cautious. August CPI accelerated to 11.1% y/y from 9.2% in July, complicating the SBP's path after it held its policy rate at 11.5% in July. SBP reserves rose to $17.12bn and USD/PKR interbank held near 277.50.

GCC
The UAE's non-oil PMI rose to 55.3 in August, its fastest pace since December 2024, while Saudi Arabia's PMI climbed to a six-month high of 53.8, both showing resilience despite the conflict backdrop. OPEC+'s seven core members head into a 6 September meeting expected to hold quotas steady into Q4. Saudi's PIF continues executing its board-approved 2026–2030 strategy prioritising tourism, travel and domestic investment under Vision 2030.

GLOBAL
The hot US jobs report reset the rate-cut narrative just as it looked like the Fed had room to ease, sending yields and the dollar higher and pressuring both equities and gold. The other swing factor remains the Middle East, where continued US-Iran strikes, including missiles on Kuwait, have pushed Brent within reach of $100/bbl for the first time since Q2.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
• Pakistan: FBR SRO 1495(I)/2026 amends the 2026 tax return form weeks ahead of the 30 September filing deadline.
• GCC: OPEC+ heads into its 6 September meeting expected to hold quotas steady into Q4.
• Global: The Fed's 15–16 September FOMC meeting looms with hike odds near 60% after the jobs shock.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 — Support 174,500 (deeper support 173,500) · Pivot 175,329 (Friday's close) · Resistance 175,400 (Friday's intraday high, then 176,000).

Desk stance: Friday's gain masks a two-sided tape — E&P and banks did the heavy lifting on the oil rally while broader breadth stayed thin. The bigger risk sits offshore: revived Fed hike odds and Brent's push toward $100/bbl worsen Pakistan's import bill just as CPI printed back in double digits. We favour a selective tilt toward E&P and banks while trimming broader beta into the SBP's September MPC and any fresh Hormuz escalation.
