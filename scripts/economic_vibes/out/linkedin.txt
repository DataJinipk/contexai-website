KSE-100 grinds to a third straight gain near 168,580 in a choppy session, as Wall Street retreats from Tuesday's records and gold slumps to a two-month low on hawkish Fed minutes.

THE OVERNIGHT TAPE
US equities pulled back Wednesday after Tuesday's records: the S&P 500 fell 0.22% to 7,801.77, the Dow lost 0.66% (-341.41 pts) to 51,179.87, and the Nasdaq slipped 0.22% to 27,538.69, as long-dated Treasury yields climbed back toward 5.32% on the 10-year and 4.80% on the 2-year, and the dollar firmed. Minutes from the Fed's hawkish 15-16 September meeting, where the FOMC hiked to 3.75-4.00%, were released at 2:00pm ET and kept a further 2026 move on the table, even as prediction markets still price roughly 83-84% odds of a hold at the 28 October meeting after a soft September payrolls print (+29,000 vs. +80,000 expected). Gold slumped about 1.3% to near $4,110, a two-month low, and silver fell about 1.1% to $60.68, as yields and the dollar pressured metals. Brent firmed to $100.87 and WTI to $90.09 after OPEC+ held November output steady.

PAKISTAN
The KSE-100 edged up 120.30 points (+0.07%) Wednesday to close at 168,580.40, a choppy session bounded by an intraday high of 169,271.98 and a low of 168,065.86. Overall volume rose to roughly 586.86 million shares worth Rs22.37 billion, with advancers beating decliners 272 to 184 across 570 names; UBL, Systems Limited, PSO, PTCL and K-Electric led the index higher, while Mari Energies, Meezan Bank, Lucky Cement, Hub Power and OGDC lagged. The SBP told the IMF its 11.5% policy rate remains appropriate as September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y (from 11.1% in August), while three-month KIBOR sat near 11.97% and six-month near 12.39%. Islamabad and the Fund remain split on exchange-rate policy as the IMF's review mission works a draft Memorandum of Economic and Financial Policies toward a staff-level agreement; the rupee firmed marginally to 277.02 interbank and the current account deficit narrowed 36% y/y to $543 million in July-August FY27.

GCC
OPEC+'s seven core producers held November output targets unchanged at their 4 October meeting, extending the pause as Hormuz- and Red Sea-linked disruptions keep real flows below quota. Saudi Arabia's PIF, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, approved its 2026-2030 strategy, pivoting from rapid asset growth toward sustained value creation across tourism, NEOM, advanced manufacturing, logistics and clean energy as the kingdom leans further into non-oil diversification under Vision 2030.

GLOBAL
Wall Street's pullback came alongside rising yields and the Fed's hawkish September minutes, while a bipartisan continuing resolution signed 2 September keeps Washington funded through 11 December, with forecasters pricing roughly a 24% chance of a fresh shutdown once that stopgap expires.

LEGISLATIVE TRACKER
Pakistan: IMF's push for a market-based rupee + draft MEFP toward a staff-level agreement - wins for banks and FX reserves if the next tranche unlocks, risk for importers and consumers if the rupee depreciates under a market-based regime.
GCC: OPEC+ November output hold + PIF's new 2026-2030 strategy - wins for Gulf exporters and PIF-backed tourism/NEOM assets, risk for shippers and insurers still pricing Hormuz/Red Sea threats.
Global: 11 December funding deadline + hawkish Fed minutes ahead of 28 October FOMC - wins for federally funded contractors and markets pricing an October hold, risk for rate-sensitive housing/small-caps and a ~24%-priced December shutdown.

THE DAY AHEAD
KSE-100 support 168,066 (deeper support at 166,500) - pivot 168,580 - resistance 169,272 (then 170,000 psychological). Desk stance: Wednesday's narrow gain looks like consolidation rather than fresh momentum - watch the IMF-Islamabad exchange-rate gap, the Fed's hawkish tone into 28 October, and whether firmer oil keeps OMCs and E&Ps supported.
