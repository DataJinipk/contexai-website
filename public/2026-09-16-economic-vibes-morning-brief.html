01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • PSX Rebound The KSE-100 gained 1,421.67 points (+0.85%) on Tuesday to close at 169,392.33, recovering most of Monday's Hormuz-driven rout. Breadth was strongly positive — 280 of 494 traded names advanced against 166 decliners and 48 unchanged — with UBL, FFC, LUCK, BAFL and PSEL collectively contributing roughly 640 points, while MEBL, JVDC and SAZEW dragged about 179 points off the index. The rally tracked the State Bank's FX reserves hitting an all-time high of $21.4bn on Eurobond inflows. Business Recorder and Daily Independent confirm the print. Sentiment: firmer on reserves.
  • Fed Hike Odds Surge Ahead of today's 2pm ET decision, CME FedWatch pricing has swung to roughly 87-90% odds of a 25bp hike — a sharp shift from the coin-flip odds priced just weeks ago — which would lift the fed funds target range from 3.50-3.75% to 3.75-4.00%. The move is being driven by hot inflation and a resilient jobs report. Yahoo Finance and FedRateCalc. Sentiment: hawkish tension.
  • Wall Street Retreats US equities softened into the decision: the S&P 500 fell 0.45% to 7,585.73, the Dow shed 0.63% to 52,093.11, and the Nasdaq dropped 0.78% to 25,981.57, as the 10-year Treasury yield briefly touched 5.04% intraday — its highest level since 2007 — before settling near 5.01%; the 2-year sits at 4.39%. CNBC and Yahoo Finance. Sentiment: risk-off.
  • Hormuz/Pipeline Oil Spike Brent rose to about $107.50/bbl, a four-month high, and WTI to $102.69, after Saudi Arabia reportedly cancelled export shipments following drone strikes that forced closure of its East-West pipeline, compounding the Hormuz-linked supply risk premium. Per Trading Economics and Fortune. A costlier import bill remains a direct headwind for Pakistan's current account. Sentiment: import-bill headwind.
  • Gold's Six-Week Low Gold slipped roughly 1% to a six-week low near $4,270/oz, while silver eased to about $62.82, as a firmer dollar (DXY near 99.6) and the looming Fed decision pulled traders to the sidelines. Per USAGOLD and Yahoo Finance. Sentiment: profit-taking.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Tuesday's 0.85% PSX rebound to 169,392 was driven by the State Bank's FX reserves hitting a record $21.4bn on Eurobond proceeds — surpassing the December 2026 target of $20.2bn ahead of schedule, per Business Recorder. The SBP has held its policy rate at 11.5% even as August CPI ran hot at 11.15% y/y, per Trading Economics, well above the 5-7% target band. The rupee held broadly steady at 277.30 in the interbank market, per Business Recorder. Pakistan and the IMF are set to open talks around September 22 on the fourth EFF and third RSF reviews, alongside the country's first Article IV consultation in two years, with roughly $1.2bn in combined disbursement at stake per Pakistan Times and ProPakistani. Separately, the Finance Bill 2026 tax overhaul remains in its implementation phase, with the FBR's September 30 filing deadline for Tax Year 2026 approaching.

B. GCC & Middle East

The Hormuz/Red Sea risk premium remains the region's dominant swing factor: Saudi Arabia's drone-damaged East-West pipeline stayed shut and reported shipment cancellations pushed Brent to a four-month high near $107.50. Against that backdrop, OPEC+ completed its rollback of voluntary output cuts, adding roughly 188,000 bpd in September before a planned Q4 pause. Non-oil momentum stays intact — the bloc's composite PMI held above 50 for a 21st straight month at 51.5, with Saudi Arabia's reading at 56.6 (25th month of growth) and the UAE's climbing to 54.2, its best in seven months, per MUFG Research. Riyadh's PIF continues executing its 2026-2030 strategy pivot toward commercial returns in logistics, mining and AI infrastructure ahead of the Saudi Mega Projects Summit (Sep 28-30), which is expected to showcase a pipeline exceeding $200bn.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street softened into Wednesday's FOMC decision, with the S&P 500, Dow and Nasdaq all lower Tuesday and the 10-year Treasury yield touching its highest level since 2007. CME FedWatch pricing now sits near 87-90% for a 25bp hike today — a marked shift from the near-even odds of just weeks ago, driven by hot inflation prints and a resilient labour market. On the policy front, Congress returned from recess with government funding secured through December 11 but no consensus on an AI regulatory framework, leaving lawmakers with only a handful of legislative days before the November 3 midterms, per Roll Call.
