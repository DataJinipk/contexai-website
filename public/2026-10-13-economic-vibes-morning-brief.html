01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100 Sheds 1,965 Points as Islamabad Imposes KP Emergency The KSE-100 fell 1,964.86 points (−1.18%) to close at 165,124.41 on Monday, reversing from an intraday high of 166,988.32 after President Asif Ali Zardari approved Governor's Rule/emergency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa on Sunday, with a joint sitting of parliament called for today to ratify the move following the collapse of government–PTI talks over counterterrorism policy. Breadth was lopsided at 85 decliners to 13 advancers (2 unchanged) on thinned volume of 104.27 million index shares (Rs14.5bn market-wide), with United Bank (−186.93 pts), Oil & Gas Development (−131.40), Habib Bank (−114.71) and Meezan Bank (−114.08) leading the rout — the 14-bank sub-index alone cost 734 points, 37% of the day's fall — while Colgate-Palmolive (+16.08) and Pakistan Tobacco bucked the trend. Pakera and The Nation confirm the levels. Sentiment: sharp political risk-off.
  • Deadly Houthi Strike on Riyadh Airport Jolts Gulf Markets A Houthi missile attack on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport over the weekend killed more than a dozen people and injured over 300, prompting airlines to cancel flights and sending Gulf equities lower even as oil climbed: the Tadawul All-Share Index closed 0.2% lower at 10,361 (Saudi National Bank −1.1%, Aramco −0.2%) and Qatar's index fell 1.5% to its lowest since May 2020. Brent rose 1.8% to settle at $106.65 and WTI gained 2.0% to $93.74 as traders priced a fresh supply-risk premium on top of ongoing Bab al-Mandeb clashes. Business Recorder and CNBC confirm the moves. Sentiment: security shock outweighs the oil windfall.
  • Fed December Hike Odds Climb Near 88%, Pressuring Gold Fed-funds futures now price roughly 88% odds of a December rate hike (versus just ~17% for the 28 October meeting), consistent with 16 of 19 FOMC members' dot-plot calls for at least one more 2026 move; the 10-year Treasury yield held near 5.24% and the 2-year near 4.80%. The repricing, alongside a firmer dollar and the oil-driven inflation scare, pulled gold down 0.59% to $4,168.90 and silver down 0.96% to $60.12. Kitco and CNBC confirm the pricing. Sentiment: higher-for-longer tightens its grip.
  • Pakistan's $1.2bn IMF Tranche Awaits Board Nod as SBP Holds at 11.5% Islamabad's $1.2 billion staff-level agreement with the IMF on the fourth EFF and third RSF reviews — reached 7–8 October — remains pending Executive Board approval, which would lift cumulative programme inflows to $5.7bn. The SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a third straight meeting on 14 September, September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y from 11.1% in August, and 3-month KIBOR sat near 11.5–11.75%, while USD/PKR held broadly steady at 276.97 interbank. Dawn and Trading Economics confirm the figures. Sentiment: genuine policy progress, overshadowed by politics.
  • US Stocks Mixed as Memory-Chip Slide Offsets Dow's Gain Wall Street closed mixed Monday: the Dow added 0.38% to 51,850.55 while the S&P 500 slipped 0.06% to 7,807.24 and the Nasdaq fell 0.44% to 27,245.90 as memory-chip names sold off on valuation jitters and the oil spike revived inflation concerns; separately, OPEC+ held November output quotas steady on 4 October, keeping Saudi Arabia at 10.5mb/d and Russia at 9.9mb/d as the group manages a market caught between improving physical supply and persistent geopolitical risk. Yahoo Finance and CNBC confirm the details. Sentiment: cautious rotation, no clear direction.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's tape absorbed a fresh political shock: the KSE-100 fell 1,964.86 points (−1.18%) Monday to close at 165,124.41, reversing from an intraday high of 166,988.32 after President Asif Ali Zardari approved Governor's Rule/emergency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa on Sunday under Articles 232/234, following the collapse of government–PTI talks over counterterrorism policy and access to Imran Khan; a joint sitting of parliament is called for today to ratify the move. Breadth was lopsided 85 decliners to 13 advancers on thinned volume (104.27m index shares, Rs14.5bn market-wide), with banks and OGDC — United Bank, Habib Bank, Meezan Bank — leading the rout (the 14-bank sub-index alone cost 734 points, 37% of the fall). The selloff overshadowed genuine progress on the macro front: the IMF's $1.2bn staff-level deal awaits Executive Board approval, the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5%, September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y, and the rupee held steady at 276.97 interbank. Pakera and Dawn.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf markets absorbed a security shock of their own: a Houthi missile strike on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport over the weekend killed more than a dozen people and injured over 300, grounding flights and sending the Tadawul All-Share Index down 0.2% to 10,361 (Saudi National Bank −1.1%, Aramco −0.2%) while Qatar's index slid 1.5% to a post-May-2020 low — a reminder that security risk can outweigh the benefit of pricier crude for Gulf economies. Brent settled up 1.8% at $106.65 and WTI up 2.0% at $93.74, and OPEC+ held November quotas steady on 4 October as the group manages a market still caught between improving physical supply and persistent geopolitical risk. Diversification momentum held firm regardless: Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI sat near a seven-month high of 55.3 in September and the UAE's matched that 55.3 reading, underscoring how domestic demand keeps absorbing external shocks. Business Recorder and CNBC.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street closed mixed Monday as the oil-driven inflation scare and a slide in memory-chip names offset a rotation into value: the Dow rose 0.38% to 51,850.55, while the S&P 500 eased 0.06% to 7,807.24 and the Nasdaq fell 0.44% to 27,245.90. Treasury yields held near multi-decade highs (10-year 5.24%, 2-year 4.80%) as Fed-funds futures now price roughly 88% odds of a December hike versus just ~17% for the 28 October meeting, consistent with 16 of 19 FOMC members' dot-plot calls for at least one more 2026 move; the same repricing pulled gold down 0.59% to $4,168.90 and silver down 0.96% to $60.12. Washington's funding fight stays parked under the continuing resolution through 11 December, though a narrower DHS funding lapse persists separately. Yahoo Finance and Kitco.
