02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

The KSE-100 enters today's session unchanged from Thursday's close of 180,104.61, after the Pakistan Stock Exchange remained shut Friday for the Independence Day holiday (ProPakistani, Business Recorder). Thursday's session itself closed 206.60 points lower (-0.11%, -0.73% on the week) as profit-taking hit fertiliser and pharmaceutical counters even as oil & gas exploration and cement held firm; breadth was negative with 269 decliners against 197 advancers (29 unchanged) on turnover of 891.33 million shares worth Rs36.69bn, led by Cnergyico (+4.35%) and Service Industries (+3.91%) among gainers against Systems Ltd (-1.59%) and Engro Holdings (-0.94%) among laggards (Express Tribune). The SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% at its July 27 review for a second straight pause, with July CPI at 9.2% y/y even as food inflation ran hotter at 10.6% (Business Recorder). Six-month KIBOR stood at roughly 11.51/11.76% as of August 7 (SBP). The interbank USD/PKR was last quoted at 277.65 on Thursday, with banks and the exchange closed Friday for the holiday (Business Recorder). On the IMF front, a mission is due in Islamabad for the fourth EFF review and second RSF review, with roughly $1.2bn combined — about $1bn under the EFF and $200m under the RSF — at stake, with tax collection, energy-sector reforms and privatisation progress under scrutiny (Energy Update).

B. GCC & Middle East

OPEC+ has finalised a further 188,000 bpd output increase for September — completing the alliance's unwinding of 2023's voluntary cuts — with delegates signalling quotas will likely hold steady through year-end pending the next JMMC review on October 4 (Iraq Business News, World Oil). Saudi Arabia's non-oil private sector held expansionary for a fourth straight month, with the Riyad Bank PMI easing to 53.1 in July from 53.3 in June as domestic demand strengthened even as export orders fell for a fifth consecutive month (Business Recorder). The PIF, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, is executing its 2026-2030 strategy across Vision, Strategic and Financial portfolios, prioritising tourism, travel, entertainment and sport — including a July tie-up between SURJ Sports Investment, Live Nation and Oak View Group to launch Radia, and a $2bn co-investment MoU with I Squared Capital — under Vision 2030 (PIF).

C. Global Intelligence

US equities eased back from record territory Friday — the S&P 500 slipped 0.2% to 7,785.76, the Dow shed 0.2% to 53,732.41 and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.3% to 26,729.16 — after a preliminary University of Michigan reading showed August consumer sentiment tumbling to 51 from July's 55.2, well below the 55 expected; even so, the S&P notched a third straight weekly gain (CNBC, TheStreet). The 10-year Treasury yield rose 5bp to about 4.67% (30-year 5.27%) and the 2-year held near 4.17%, as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh kept a hawkish tone on the Fed's strict 2% inflation target ahead of the September 15-16 FOMC meeting, even as prediction markets price roughly a 26% chance of a hike (Advisor Perspectives). Brent and WTI firmed to $88.52 and $82.40 respectively as OPEC+'s finalised September output hike failed to fully offset a lingering Middle East risk premium (Trading Economics). Gold and silver advanced to $4,373.50/oz (+0.53%) and $64.53/oz (+0.32%) as haven demand held firm against the still-hawkish Fed backdrop (Kitco).
