02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's macro picture turned more complicated this week: August CPI jumped to 11.1% y/y from 9.2% in July, its first double-digit print in months, even as the KSE-100 added 0.23% to close at 175,328.82 on Friday on broad-based buying in banks, autos and E&P. SBP foreign reserves rose to $17.12bn in the week to 28 August, with FY26 remittances tracking toward $42bn, and the central bank holds its policy rate at 11.5% into a September MPC that now looks more finely balanced given the inflation resurgence and a firmer oil import bill. USD/PKR interbank held little changed near 277.50. Sources: Business Recorder, ProPakistani.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf non-oil momentum stayed resilient despite the conflict backdrop: the UAE's S&P Global PMI rose to 55.3 in August from 52.7, its fastest pace since December 2024, while Saudi Arabia's Riyad Bank PMI climbed to a six-month high of 53.8. That strength is being tested by a firmer oil tape — Brent near $97.5/bbl on renewed Iran-Kuwait strikes — as OPEC+'s seven core members head into a 6 September meeting expected to hold quotas steady into Q4 after completing their planned 2026 output-cut unwind. Saudi's PIF, meanwhile, is executing its board-approved 2026–2030 strategy prioritising tourism, travel and domestic ecosystem investment as Vision 2030 enters its final stretch. Sources: The National, Arab News.

C. Global Intelligence

A blowout August payrolls report reset the rate-cut narrative: nonfarm payrolls added 162,000 versus 53,000 expected, unemployment held at 4.1%, and Treasury yields jumped — the 10-year to 4.78%, the 2-year to 4.34% — lifting the odds of a hike at the 15–16 September FOMC meeting toward 60% even as Fed Governor Waller argues for patience. Equities retreated in response (S&P 500 −0.38%) while gold slid 1.3% to $4,420/oz on a firmer dollar. The other swing factor is the Middle East: continued US-Iran strikes, including missiles on Kuwait, have pushed Brent within reach of $100/bbl for the first time since Q2. Sources: CNBC, Trading Economics.
