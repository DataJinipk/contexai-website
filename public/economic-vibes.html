02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

The KSE-100 retreated 0.81% (−1,464 pts) to 179,846.68 on Tuesday after touching an intraday high of 181,017, as broad selling in banks, cement, fertiliser, autos, E&P and OMCs offset last week's rally; market breadth stayed mixed with roughly even gainers and losers (Business Recorder). The SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% at its July 27 review for a second straight pause, even as July CPI cooled to 9.2% y/y from 11.1% (food inflation actually accelerated to 10.6%), keeping real rates restrictive (Trading Economics). Six-month KIBOR continues to track a touch above the policy rate (EST.). The interbank USD/PKR was broadly steady at 277.67, down marginally from 277.70 (SBP). On the IMF front, Islamabad is preparing for a fifth EFF/RSF review mission next month that could release roughly $1.2bn, with energy circular-debt targets and privatisation progress the key sticking points (ProPakistani).

B. GCC & Middle East

Seven OPEC+ members — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman — lifted output by a further 188,000 bpd from August, the alliance's fifth straight monthly increase as it continues unwinding voluntary cuts even with Hormuz-linked price risk elevated (Vision2030.ai). Saudi Arabia's non-oil private sector stayed in solid expansion, with the Riyad Bank PMI easing only slightly to 53.1 in July from 53.3 in June as output and new orders kept climbing; Kuwait's reading rebounded over the same stretch (AGBI). The PIF, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, has approved its 2026–2030 strategy, pivoting from rapid asset growth toward value creation with tourism, travel and entertainment as priority sectors under Vision 2030 (Gulf Business).

C. Global Intelligence

US equities closed lower Tuesday — the S&P 500 fell 0.24%, the Dow slipped 0.16% and the Nasdaq dropped 0.55% — as tech weakness and a stalled US-Iran Hormuz negotiation overshadowed industrial-sector gains (Washington Post). The 10-year Treasury yield climbed above 4.7% — its highest since January — as oil-driven inflation risk pushed market-implied odds of a 25bp September Fed hike to roughly 55%, a marked hawkish reversal from July's 9-3 hold vote (Chase). Brent and WTI both surged roughly 5% to settle near $87.72 and $82.13 respectively, extending a fourth straight advance on fears that a naval blockade will keep the Strait of Hormuz constrained (CNBC). Gold and silver caught a safe-haven bid, with gold back above $4,400/oz and silver at a seven-week high near $65.50/oz (Kitco).

04The Day Ahead — KSE-100 Levels & Desk Stance

Levels (EST.): support 178,500 — first floor; deeper support at 176,500 (desk estimate) · pivot 179,847 — Tuesday's close · resistance 181,017 — Tuesday's intraday high; then Monday's close of 181,310.

We read Tuesday's pullback as a geopolitical repricing, not a trend reversal — the KSE-100 gave back barely 0.8% after a strong run, and the drivers (Hormuz impasse, a firmer Fed-hike narrative) are external rather than domestic-earnings-driven. A close back above 181,000 would reopen record territory; a break of 178,500 would point to a deeper retracement toward 176,500. Banks, fertiliser and cement — last week's leaders — are the counters to watch for signs of the dip being bought versus extended. All levels are indicative desk estimates, not recommendations.
