01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100 Retreats From Record Highs on Profit-TakingThe KSE-100 shed 1,138.50 points (−0.68%) to close at 167,441.90 on Thursday, reversing from an intraday high of 169,486.60 as banking and fertiliser heavyweights led late selling. Traded volume fell to roughly 152.07 million shares worth Rs17.72 billion (from 244.16 million shares/Rs22.37 billion on Wednesday), with decliners thumping advancers 81 to 18 (1 unchanged) among index constituents. United Bank (−152.76 index points), Fauji Fertilizer (−85.51), Systems Limited (−54.28), Engro Fertilizers (−54.06) and Pakistan Oilfields (−47.84) capped the index, while Pakistan State Oil (+32.84), Lotte Chemical (+13.54) and Abbott Laboratories (+7.18) were the lone bright spots as refiners rode the crude spike. Business Recorder and Pakera confirm the levels. Sentiment: sharp pullback despite an IMF relief headline.
  • Pakistan, IMF Reach Staff-Level Deal, Unlocking $1.2bnThe IMF and Islamabad reached a staff-level agreement on 7 October completing the 2026 Article IV consultation alongside the fourth EFF review and third RSF review, clearing the way for roughly $1.2 billion (about $1.0bn/SDR 760mn under the EFF and $210mn/SDR 154mn under the RSF) once the Executive Board signs off. The Fund's mission, led by Iva Petrova, held talks in Karachi and Islamabad from 23 September to 7 October, and continues to press for exchange-rate flexibility as a shock absorber even as the rupee held broadly steady at 277.01 interbank. ProPakistani and the IMF confirm the terms. Sentiment: policy win, tranche release still pending board approval.
  • Crude Spikes on Hormuz Tanker Attacks, Hits US TechBrent rose 4.0% to settle at $104.28 and WTI gained 3.6% to $91.49 on Thursday after Iran escalated attacks on tankers transiting the Strait of Hormuz, with a Gulf of Mexico storm threat adding a secondary squeeze; prices eased off session highs after President Trump said Washington would not strike Iran before the November midterms. The inflation scare helped drag the Nasdaq down 1.25% to 27,193.34 and the S&P 500 down 0.47% to 7,765.36, while the Dow eked out a 0.10% gain to 51,231.64 as energy names offset tech weakness; a Financial Times report trimming OpenAI's revenue estimate added separate pressure on semiconductors. CNBC and Yahoo Finance confirm the levels. Sentiment: geopolitical supply risk revives inflation jitters.
  • Fed Keeps Hike Door Open as Waller Flags More TighteningFed Governor Christopher Waller said additional rate hikes will probably be necessary, though the timing remains flexible, keeping alive the hawkish path set by September's first hike since 2023 to 3.75–4.00%. Futures markets price roughly 81–83% odds of a hold at the 28 October FOMC meeting but still assign better-than-75% odds of a hike at the 9 December meeting, consistent with 16 of 18 FOMC members' dot-plot projections for at least one more 2026 move; the 10-year Treasury yield sat near 5.23% and the 2-year near 4.77%. CNBC and Schwab confirm the stance. Sentiment: higher-for-longer narrative intact, oil the new catalyst.
  • Gulf Non-Oil Engines Accelerate as PIF Unveils 2026–30 PlanGulf diversification kept outrunning the headlines: the S&P Global UAE PMI held at 55.3 in September on the fastest output growth since February, while Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI jumped to 55.3 from 53.8 in August, a seven-month high, even as business expectations softened on regional-tension risk. Separately, Saudi Arabia's PIF approved its 2026–2030 strategy, directing roughly 80% of capital into domestic investments (scaled back from a 30% foreign-allocation peak to 20%) across three portfolios anchored by tourism, NEOM and advanced manufacturing. AGBI and PIF confirm the details. Sentiment: diversification momentum offsets oil-driven jitters.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's tape gave back Wednesday's gains: the KSE-100 fell 1,138.50 points (−0.68%) Thursday to close at 167,441.90, reversing from an intraday high of 169,486.60 as profit-taking hit banks and fertilisers — United Bank, Fauji Fertilizer, Systems Limited and Engro Fertilizers were the heaviest drags, while Pakistan State Oil rode the crude spike to a rare gain. Volume thinned to 152.07 million shares (Rs17.72bn), with decliners beating advancers 81 to 18. The session came even as Islamabad and the IMF reached a staff-level agreement on the fourth EFF and third RSF reviews on 7 October, unlocking roughly $1.2 billion pending Executive Board approval, while the rupee held broadly steady at 277.01 interbank, September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y and the SBP's 11.5% policy rate stayed on hold. Business Recorder and ProPakistani.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf fundamentals strengthened on two fronts Thursday. Oil jumped as Iran escalated attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz — Brent settled up 4.0% at $104.28 and WTI up 3.6% at $91.49 — a tailwind for Gulf budgets even as shippers and insurers price a heavier risk premium. Non-oil momentum held firm regardless: the UAE's S&P Global PMI stayed at 55.3 in September on the fastest output growth since February, and Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI rose to a seven-month high of 55.3 from 53.8 in August. Saudi's PIF approved its 2026–2030 strategy, tilting 80% of capital toward domestic sectors — tourism, NEOM, advanced manufacturing — as Vision 2030's diversification drive outpaces the oil-price swings. CNBC and ICIS.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street wobbled Thursday as the Hormuz-driven oil spike revived inflation fears and a Financial Times report trimming OpenAI's revenue estimate hit chipmakers: the S&P 500 fell 0.47% to 7,765.36 and the Nasdaq tumbled 1.25% to 27,193.34, while the Dow eked out a 0.10% gain to 51,231.64 on energy-stock strength. Treasury yields held near multi-decade highs (10-year ~5.23%, 2-year ~4.77%) after Fed Governor Christopher Waller said further hikes are "probably necessary," with futures pricing roughly 81–83% odds of an October hold but better-than-75% odds of a December hike, consistent with 16 of 18 FOMC members' dot-plot projections. Washington's funding risk is parked for now — the continuing resolution signed 2 September keeps the government funded through 11 December. Yahoo Finance and CNBC.
