01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100 Rockets Back Above 168,000The KSE-100 staged a furious reversal on Tuesday, surging 2,592.79 points (+1.56%) to settle at 168,460.10, clawing back most of Monday's 2,288-point rout. The index swung from an intraday low of 165,651.67 to a late-session high of 168,626.18 as afternoon buying accelerated across automobile assemblers, cement, commercial banks, E&Ps, OMCs, power generation and refineries. Volume ran to roughly 193.4 million shares on turnover of Rs7.71 billion across 150,708 trades, with advancers leading decliners 267 to 199 (104 unchanged) across 570 names. Among individual names, LSE Financial Services (+17.2%), First Paramount Modaraba, Samba Bank, Sindh Modaraba, TRG Pakistan and Crescent Jute Products all hit their upper caps, while Crescent Fibres (−9.0%) and Mandviwala Mauser Plastic (−8.1%) led the laggards. Business Recorder and The Express Tribune confirm the levels. Sentiment: sharp broad-based reversal off Monday's lows.
  • Wall Street Sets Fresh Records on Tech StrengthUS equities extended their run on Tuesday as easing long-dated yields and AI-driven tech buying offset a global bond sell-off: the S&P 500 rose 0.58% to a record 7,818.93 (+44.98 pts), the Dow added 0.49% (+253.38 pts) to 51,521.28, and the Nasdaq climbed 0.45% to a record 27,599.79, led by Nvidia and AMD. Minutes from the Fed's 15–16 September meeting — where the FOMC delivered its first hike since 2023, lifting rates to 3.75–4.00% — are due today at 2:00pm ET, with prediction markets pricing roughly 81–84% odds the Fed holds at the 28 October meeting. Yahoo Finance and CNBC confirm the levels. Sentiment: records despite a still-hawkish rate backdrop.
  • Oil Whipsaws as Houthi Strikes Hit Saudi Aramco SitesCrude settled lower Tuesday — Brent at $100.30 (−1.89%) and WTI at $89.18 (−0.28%) — as Middle Eastern crude flows topped 18 million bpd in late September (above pre-war levels), Saudi Arabia cut November Asian official selling prices by up to $5/bbl, and the G7 confirmed a 100-million-barrel emergency reserve release over four months. That relief proved short-lived: Houthi ballistic missiles and drones struck Saudi Aramco facilities in Riyadh and Khurais over the weekend, reviving supply-risk premia and pushing Brent back toward $101.60 and WTI near $90.20 into Wednesday's session. OPEC+'s seven core producers held November output steady at 31 million bpd on 4 October, with Gulf exports ex-Iran still only around 81% of pre-war levels and roughly 40% of flows now bypassing a Strait of Hormuz where UKMTO has logged at least one attack daily since 2 October. Gulf News and CNBC confirm the levels. Sentiment: volatile, Hormuz and Houthi risk still the swing factor.
  • Gold, Silver Firm as Yields EaseBullion extended its climb as long-dated Treasury yields pulled back from Monday's multi-decade highs: spot gold traded near $4,164, up roughly 0.6% on the session, while spot silver added about 0.8% to $61.41, with the move aided by crude's dip below $100 a barrel. Kitco and Fortune confirm the levels. Sentiment: yields relief lifts metals.
  • SBP Holds at 11.5%, CPI at 10.3%, IMF Review AdvancesPakistan's SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a third straight meeting on 14 September, as September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y (from August's 11.1%) — still well above the 5–7% target band. Three-month KIBOR sat near 11.5–11.75% and six-month near 11.71–11.96% as of 18 September, while the rupee held broadly steady near 277.15 interbank. An IMF mission led by Iva Petrova continues the fourth EFF/third RSF review, formally opened by Finance Minister Aurangzeb around 29 September; a successful conclusion would unlock roughly $1.2 billion combined ($1bn EFF, $200m RSF), with Islamabad briefing the mission on Hormuz-linked revenue disruption and committing 10,000 federal employees to digitally declare assets by 30 October. Trading Economics and Profit confirm the details. Sentiment: policy continuity, IMF-dependent outlook.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's tape staged a sharp reversal: the KSE-100 surged 2,592.79 points (+1.56%) Tuesday to 168,460.10, clawing back most of Monday's 2,288-point rout as buying returned broadly across autos, cement, banks, E&Ps, OMCs, power and refineries. Volume rose to roughly 193.4 million shares worth Rs7.71 billion, with advancers beating decliners 267 to 199 across 570 names. The SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% on 14 September as September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y, and the rupee held broadly steady near 277.15 interbank. The IMF's fourth EFF/third RSF review remains the key swing factor, with roughly $1.2bn in disbursements hinging on its outcome and a 30 October deadline for 10,000 federal employees to declare assets digitally. Business Recorder and The Express Tribune.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf fundamentals stayed resilient even as the security backdrop deteriorated. Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI rose to a seven-month high of 55.3 in September (from 53.8 in August), with new orders growing at their fastest pace since February even as export orders fell for a seventh straight month on Red Sea-linked shipping disruption; the Crown Prince's PIF 2026–2030 strategy continues to back national champions and global partnerships under Vision 2030. OPEC+'s seven core producers held November output steady at 31 million bpd on 4 October, with Gulf crude exports ex-Iran near 81% of pre-war levels, before Houthi missile and drone strikes on Saudi Aramco facilities in Riyadh and Khurais over the weekend revived supply-risk premia and pushed Brent back toward $101.60. ICIS and IBTimes.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street extended its run Tuesday — S&P 500 +0.58% to a record 7,818.93, Dow +0.49% to 51,521.28, and Nasdaq +0.45% to a record 27,599.79 — led by Nvidia and AMD, as long-dated Treasury yields eased off Monday's multi-decade highs (10-year near 5.27%, 2-year near 4.80%). Minutes from the Fed's hawkish 15–16 September hike to 3.75–4.00% are due today at 2:00pm ET, with prediction markets pricing roughly 81–84% odds of a hold at the 28 October FOMC meeting. A bipartisan continuing resolution, signed 2 September, keeps Washington funded through 11 December, removing shutdown risk through the midterm run-up even as full-year appropriations remain unresolved. Yahoo Finance and Federal funding tracker.
