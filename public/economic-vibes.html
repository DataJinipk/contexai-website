02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

The KSE-100 slipped 1,109.14 points (0.62%) to close at 176,846.36 on Wednesday, giving back an early rally that had lifted the index to an intraday high of 178,942.31, after media reports that the petroleum ministry would negotiate diesel-price cuts with local refineries triggered broad-based selling in refinery, banking, fertiliser and E&P names; UBL, Engro Holdings, HBL, FFC, Attock Refinery, Meezan Bank, Mari Energies, Systems, Cnergyico and National Bank together erased 859 points, and breadth turned negative (185 gainers vs 271 losers) on volume of 787.9m shares worth Rs39.6bn. The reversal came even as separate reports of government progress on gas-sector circular debt offered some offsetting support. July CPI eased to 9.2% y/y from 11.1% in June, and the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting, with 1-month KIBOR near 11.39/11.89% (bid/offer) as of mid-August. An IMF mission is expected in September for the next EFF/RSF review, with roughly $1.2bn (about $1bn EFF plus $200m RSF) in view, contingent on fiscal consolidation, energy circular-debt reform and climate-linked benchmarks.

B. GCC & Middle East

Brent held near $92.4/bbl and WTI near $85.6/bbl, extending a fourth straight session of gains, as Strait of Hormuz shipping traffic collapsed to roughly three vessels over the weekend against a five-day average of 12 and a pre-conflict norm near 130 daily, with US-Iran talks showing little sign of a breakthrough. Seven OPEC+ members continue raising output by 188,000 bpd a month, a signal of confidence in demand even amid the standoff, while Saudi Arabia's non-oil private sector held a fourth straight month of expansion in July (Riyad Bank PMI 53.1). Separately, the PIF board approved its 2026-2030 strategy, prioritising tourism, travel and entertainment alongside deeper private-sector participation — the medium-term diversification anchor even as elevated shipping and insurance costs weigh on regional trade.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street edged higher Wednesday — S&P 500 +0.2% to 7,707.98, Dow +0.2% to 53,463.05, Nasdaq +0.2% to 26,331.09 — after the US Treasury said it would double liquidity-support buybacks of longer-dated debt to at least $4bn per operation from September, pulling long-dated yields back from multi-decade highs: the 10-year eased to roughly 4.65% and the 30-year to near 5.19% from above 5.3% earlier in the week. The move puts fresh pressure on Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish tilt (nine of 18 FOMC participants project a 2026 hike), effectively easing financial conditions the Fed itself has resisted loosening. Gold jumped roughly 3.7% to $4,493/oz on the lower-yield, softer-dollar backdrop even as silver eased to $63.87/oz, while Brent held near $92.4/bbl on the deepening Hormuz transit disruption.
