01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • PSX Extends Slide Into MondayThe KSE-100 fell for a third straight session on Friday, settling at 168,155.49, down 481.36 points (−0.29%), after shedding 1,332.47 points (−0.78%) on Thursday — a combined roughly 1,814-point, 1.07% slide over two sessions — as local political uncertainty collided with a fresh oil-price surge. Total market volume slipped to 493.96 million shares (from 548.38 million) on traded value of Rs17.49 billion, with decliners leading advancers 321 to 128 (45 unchanged) across 494 names; Commercial Banks dragged the index down 121.77 points while Oil & Gas Exploration Companies added 72.68 points. The index opened Monday under renewed pressure, falling over 1,600 points intraday to 166,554.66 (−0.95%) as the same twin drivers persisted. Business Recorder and Mettis Global confirm the levels. Sentiment: three-day slide extends into Monday's open.
  • Wall Street Rallies as Jobs Miss Cools the FedUS equities surged Friday after September payrolls rose just 29,000 (vs. 84,000 expected) and unemployment ticked up to 4.2%, cementing bets that the Fed holds at its 27–28 October meeting: the S&P 500 gained 0.73% to 7,722.72, the Dow added 0.49% (+250.40 pts) to 51,176.96, and the Nasdaq jumped 1.19% to a record-high 27,190.86. CME FedWatch priced October hike odds at roughly 17–21%, down from 64% a week earlier, even as Fed Vice Chair Jefferson and New York Fed's Williams counselled patience after Chair Warsh's hawkish September debut hike to 3.75–4%. Yahoo Finance and CNBC confirm the levels. Sentiment: soft jobs data, firmer risk appetite.
  • Yields Hold Near Highs as Oil Climbs, OPEC+ Steadies SupplyThe 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.28% and the 2-year at 4.83% — ticking higher even as the weak jobs print cooled October hike bets, underscoring a still-hawkish rate backdrop. Brent settled at $102.25 and WTI at $91.11, up roughly 2% on the week after China suspended oil-product exports and tightened global fuel supply, compounding lingering Strait of Hormuz disruption. OPEC+'s seven core producers agreed on 4 October to hold November output at 31.01 million bpd — extending October's pause after four straight monthly hikes — with Gulf members still pumping 60–80% of normal levels amid the Hormuz-linked export disruption. CNBC and CNBC confirm the levels. Sentiment: yields sticky, oil firm and supply-constrained.
  • Gold, Silver Give Back the Payrolls BounceBullion's early jobs-data rally faded into the close: spot gold settled near $4,142.00, down 0.83% on the session, and spot silver fell 1.04% to $60.23, as a firm dollar and still-elevated Treasury yields outweighed the softer October rate-hike odds. Kitco and Fortune confirm the levels. Sentiment: profit-taking into the close.
  • SBP Holds at 11.5%, CPI Eases to 10.3%, IMF Review AdvancesPakistan's SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a third straight meeting on 14 September, as September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y (from August's 11.1%) — still well above the 5–7% target band, with transport costs up 27.4% y/y. The rupee firmed 3 paisa to 277.07 interbank, while 3-month KIBOR held near 11.78% and 6-month near 11.98% (as of 25 September). An IMF mission under Iva Petrova continues the fourth EFF/third RSF review, kicked off by Finance Minister Aurangzeb on 29 September; a successful conclusion would unlock roughly $1.2 billion combined, with Islamabad also requiring 10,000 federal employees to digitally declare assets by 30 October. Profit and Business Recorder confirm the details. Sentiment: policy continuity, IMF-dependent outlook.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's tape turned decisively risk-off into the weekend: the KSE-100 fell for a third straight session, down 481 points (−0.29%) Friday to 168,155.49 after shedding 1,332 points Thursday, as local political uncertainty collided with a fresh oil-price surge from China's export suspension. Commercial Banks led the damage (−121.77 index points) while Oil & Gas Exploration Companies cushioned the fall (+72.68 points) on firmer crude. The SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% on 14 September as September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y, and the rupee firmed marginally to 277.07 interbank even as the index opened Monday down a further 1,600-plus points to 166,554.66. The IMF's fourth EFF/third RSF review remains the key swing factor, with roughly $1.2bn in disbursements hinging on its outcome. Business Recorder and The Express Tribune.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf fundamentals stayed anchored by non-oil momentum even as oil-market mechanics tightened. Saudi Arabia's Riyad Bank PMI held at a six-month high of 57.8 in September, as the Crown Prince's PIF 2026–2030 strategy cut construction spending by roughly $41 billion while elevating Hormuz-immune digital infrastructure as Vision 2030's new centre of gravity; non-oil activity now accounts for 55% of non-oil GDP. OPEC+'s seven core producers held November output at 31.01 million bpd on 4 October — extending the pause after four consecutive monthly hikes — with Gulf members still pumping only 60–80% of normal levels amid continuing Strait of Hormuz export disruption; the group next meets 1 November. Arab News and CNBC.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street's Friday rally — S&P 500 +0.73%, Dow +0.49%, Nasdaq +1.19% to a record high — came on the back of a weak September payrolls print (+29,000 vs. +84,000 expected) that cut October Fed-hike odds to roughly 17–21% from 64% a week earlier, even as the 10-year Treasury yield held at 5.28% and the 2-year at 4.83% following Chair Warsh's hawkish September hike to 3.75–4%. A bipartisan continuing resolution keeps Washington funded into early-to-mid December, removing shutdown risk through the midterm run-up. Brent and WTI firmed toward $102 and $91 respectively after China suspended oil-product exports, adding a fresh supply-tightening impulse on top of lingering Hormuz disruption. CNBC and NBC News.
