01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100 Snaps Winning Streak on Profit-TakingThe KSE-100 fell 1,733.57 points (−1.01%) on Thursday to close at 170,498.94, ending a three-session rally, after swinging between an intraday high of 172,491.16 and a low of 170,418.38. Volume eased to 759.97 million shares (from 773.59 million) but traded value rose to Rs27.54 billion (from Rs26.45 billion), as 358 stocks declined against just 101 advancers and 36 unchanged. Selling hit automobile assemblers, cement, commercial banks, E&P and OMC counters, with LUCK, HBL, MCB, MEBL, UBL, MARI, OGDC, PPL, PSO and SSGC among the index-heavy laggards; CNERGY (−3.3%), PACE and KOSM led percentage declines. Topline Securities said investors "moved to lock in profits, resulting in a broad-based retreat," with sentiment turning "fragile and risk-averse" on Middle East tensions and Brent crossing $100. Business Recorder and Profit confirm the print. Sentiment: profit-taking breaks a three-day rally.
  • Wall Street Wobbles as Yields ClimbUS equities were mixed-to-lower Thursday: the S&P 500 slipped 0.06% to 7,704.23, the Dow fell 0.31% to 51,349.89 and the Nasdaq dropped 1.1% to 26,936.04, pressured by the surging Treasury curve and crude's renewed advance even as hopes of a Hormuz de-escalation capped some of the losses. Yahoo Finance and TheStreet confirm the close. Sentiment: risk-off, yield- and oil-driven.
  • 10Y Yield Hits Fresh 19-Year HighThe 10-year Treasury yield rose to roughly 5.11-5.12%, its highest since 2007, while the 2-year climbed over 4bps to 4.94%, as Fed Governor Michael Barr said "further policy adjustments are likely to be needed," and President John Williams called another 2026 hike "reasonable." Odds of an October 27-28 hike jumped to roughly 70-77% on CME FedWatch, up sharply from the prior session, under Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish stance. CNBC and CNBC confirm the move. Sentiment: hawkish, multi-decade high.
  • Oil Extends Rally as Hormuz Stays ShutBrent rose 3.2% to $106.39 and WTI gained 2.8% to $94.76, up roughly 22% over the past month, as the Strait of Hormuz remained effectively closed to routine commercial shipping — IMF PortWatch recorded just 1 transit on 20 September against a pre-crisis baseline of 85 per day. OPEC+'s Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee holds its 68th meeting on 4 October, though members' spare capacity to offset the shut-in Gulf barrels remains constrained. Trading Economics and The Conversation confirm. Sentiment: volatile, geopolitically driven rally.
  • Gold and Silver Pull Back on Firmer DollarGold eased to $4,273 (−0.3% / −$13.74 on the day) and silver fell 1.7% to $63.36, staying below $64/oz, as a firmer dollar and hawkish Fed repricing pressured precious metals even against the geopolitical backdrop. USAGOLD and Fortune confirm the pullback. Sentiment: pulling back on a firmer dollar.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's macro backdrop stays a study in contrasts: the KSE-100 snapped its rally, falling 1.01% to 170,498.94, as an IMF mission opened talks on the next EFF/RSF review and PM Shehbaz Sharif met Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva on the sidelines of UNGA to reaffirm reform commitments. August CPI held at 11.15% y/y, well above the SBP's 5-7% target band, with food (+13.89%) and transport (+20.17%) the main drivers; the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% at its 14 September MPC meeting, while 6-month KIBOR sits at 11.72/11.97% (bid/offer) as of 22 September. Separately, the Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2026 was deferred by the Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue, which is seeking a clause-by-clause justification before its next sitting. Profit and Business Recorder confirm.

B. GCC & Middle East

The Gulf's non-oil economies keep outrunning the region's energy disruption: Saudi Arabia's PMI rose to a six-month high of 53.8 in August (from 53.1 in July), while the UAE's non-oil PMI jumped to 55.3 (from 52.7), its fastest pace since December 2024, even as the Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed to routine shipping. Saudi's PIF continues rolling out its 2026-2030 strategy, with the Crown Prince launching CEER's flagship vehicles on 21 September and PIF-backed Tawrid and ASFAR's Gamra Mountain Resort both unveiled the prior week. OPEC+'s JMMC meets 4 October to review production policy against constrained Gulf spare capacity. Arab News and The National confirm.

C. Global Intelligence

The dominant global theme remains the bond market: the 10-year Treasury yield's push to roughly 5.11-5.12%, a 19-year high, reflects Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish path, Governor Barr's and President Williams's comments flagging further tightening, and October rate-hike odds near 70-77% on CME FedWatch — a combination pressuring equities even as crude's advance adds an inflationary complication. Separately, Washington's tariff agenda widens on 29 September, when 100% Section 232 duties on patented pharmaceuticals extend to all importers beyond the 17 large companies already covered, alongside expanded Section 338 tariffs on Canadian goods. CNBC and Wiley Trade Compliance confirm.
