02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

A broad-based breakout to a record close. The KSE-100 closed Wednesday at 172,013.48 (+3,994.62, +2.38%; Tuesday 168,018.86, +0.87%), between 168,355.26 and 172,286.99. KSE-100 volume was 371.0m shares; 90 of 100 constituents advanced and 10 declined. Market-wide the All-Share index rose 2.13% to 103,148.97 on 841.6m shares worth Rs35.4bn, with 368 gainers, 103 decliners and 28 unchanged (Mettis, Dawn) [V]. Top index gainers were BOP +10.00%, AKBL +8.79%, NBP +6.92%, KEL +6.57% and AHCL +5.96%; Business Recorder's intraday report cited buying in autos, cement, commercial banks, E&P, OMCs, power and refineries, with mutual funds the main buyers, and pointed to oil stabilising near $103 as a sentiment driver before the later slide (Business Recorder) [V]. Sector point-contributions and laggards were not published. Macro: October CPI 9.6% y/y (10.3% prior; urban core 7.8%) (Dawn); SBP policy rate 12.0% after the 26 Oct 50bp hike (Mettis); 6M KIBOR 12.42%/12.67% [V]. FX: USD/PKR closed 276.70 interbank (276.71 prior), the 275th consecutive session of rupee appreciation; open market 277.30/280.00 (Mettis) [V]. Fuel: petrol price cut by Rs7.06 and diesel by Rs3.71 per litre (headline; details not read) after a Rs5.45 petrol cut on 3 Nov (Mettis) [V headline]. External: a $3bn Saudi loan rollover and an IMF Board meeting expected next month (headlines only) (Mettis, Mettis) [EST.].

B. GCC & Middle East

Non-oil PMIs cool, TASI rebounds, Brent drop tests Gulf budgets. Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI eased to 54.0 in October (55.3 in September), the seventh month above 50, with export sales falling for an eighth month (Trading Economics / S&P Global) [V]; the UAE non-oil PMI slipped to 54.8 (55.3), with price pressures intensifying (Arab News) [V]. TASI was quoted at 10,038.89 (+1.65%) on 4 Nov, still about 4.2% lower on the month (Trading Economics) [EST., CFD-based quote]. Saudi Arabia posted a $22.38bn Q3 budget deficit (Arab News; headline only) [V headline], which makes Brent's drop below $100 a fiscal watch item. Vortexa data cited by Oilprice show Gulf crude exports averaged 17.1m bpd in October against 18.9m in September, and Iran says four tankers were destroyed by mines near Hormuz; Houthis claimed an attack on Riyadh's King Khalid airport, which Saudi Arabia did not confirm (Oilprice) [EST.]. Qatar is mediating US–Iran talks, though a senior Iranian official says the sides are "far from an agreement" (Kitco) [EST.]. DFM/ADX closes, PIF mandates and OPEC+ decisions dated this week were not found.

C. Global Intelligence — USA & Oil

Democrats take Congress; stocks surge, oil crashes. Wednesday closes: Dow 53,356.12 (+1,569.40, +3.03%), S&P 500 7,688.62 (+1.58%), Nasdaq 25,736.51 (+1.44%), Russell 2000 2,856.96 (+1.58%), the Dow's best post-midterm day in 44 years. Democrats won the House and the Senate; Apple (+6%), Amazon (+7%) and Microsoft (+7%) led after earnings, while energy stocks fell most (TheStreet, CBS News) [V]. Oil: Brent fell $8.94 (−8.6%) to about $94.66 and WTI $8.01 (−8.3%) to $88.08 at 1:06pm ET, after the White House blamed the Iran war for the midterm rout; Trump told the FT there are no immediate plans to strike Iran, and a Democratic Congress could curb Middle East military funding. The EIA week to 30 Oct showed crude −0.9m bbl to 409.8m and distillates −3.6m bbl, and 300,000 bbl were loaned from the Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve. Trading Economics had Brent near $94 on Thursday, about 16% lower on the week; other tickers showed stale Brent prints of $102–110, which the desk disregards (Oilprice, Trading Economics, Kitco) [EST., intraday, no official settlement published]. Rates and Fed: the 10Y yield fell about 6bp to ~4.90% and the 2Y was ~4.60% early Wednesday; DXY ~101.4. The Fed held at 3.75–4.00% on 28 Oct (10–2, Cook and Hammack dissenting for a hike); December hike odds are ~75–80% (Polymarket 74.5%, Kitco ~80%). Data: ADP +86k, JOLTS 7.52m, ISM manufacturing 55.5; jobless claims Thursday, October payrolls Friday, CPI on 10 Nov (Kitco, Rio Times) [EST.]. Policy: Reuters notes a divided government narrows Trump's room to legislate, adds AI-sector and tariff scrutiny, and puts the 11 Dec FY2027 spending deadline in focus (Reuters via Kitco) [EST.]. Metals: gold ~$4,041 (+2.9%) on Wednesday and ~$4,125 on Thursday, silver ~$52–53 (Rio Times) [EST.]. Other: euro-area October inflation 4.0%, a three-year high, with ECB hike bets; BoE and Banxico decide Thursday; Brazil's Copom held at 13.75% [EST.].
