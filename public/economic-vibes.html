01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100 Clears 166,186 as Parliament Ratifies KP Governor's Rule 214-56The KSE-100 extended its rebound, climbing 768.66 points (+0.46%) to close at 166,186.17 after swinging between an intraday low of 165,613.12 and a high of 166,877.02, as 292 stocks advanced against 169 decliners on turnover of roughly 517.6 million shares worth Rs23.57 billion; market capitalisation rose to Rs18.475 trillion. PTC, FFC, HUBC, OGDC and SYS added a combined ~340 points, with buying concentrated in automobile assemblers, commercial banks, oil and gas exploration, oil marketing companies and power generation; Cnergyico PK (62.8m shares) led volumes. The catalyst: a joint sitting of Parliament voted 214-56 on Thursday to ratify the Article 232 emergency and Article 234 governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, with PTI lawmakers loudly opposing the motion before it was adopted on a standing vote. Dawn and Business Recorder confirm the levels and the vote. Sentiment: relief rally, political closure.
  • Dow Jumps 1.1% as AI-Chip Rout Triggers Rotation; S&P, Nasdaq Grind HigherWall Street steadied after a shaky open: the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 556.21 points (+1.08%) to 52,177.69, the S&P 500 added 0.30% to 7,744.06 and the Nasdaq Composite edged up 0.08% to 26,713.03, while the Russell 2000 gained 1.4% to 2,767.27. A worries-driven sell-off in AI and semiconductor names that started in Asia on debt-financing concerns pared its losses through the session as strong third-quarter bank earnings and easing Treasury yields pulled capital into value and small-cap names. Associated Press via WTOP and Yahoo Finance confirm the closes. Sentiment: rotation out of AI trade, not a broad risk-off.
  • Gold Nears $4,170, Silver Up 3.2% as Bond Rally Outruns Hot PPIPrecious metals rallied despite a hotter-than-expected PPI print: COMEX gold rose to $4,169.24, up about 1.6%, while silver jumped 3.2% to $57.31, as a flight-to-safety bond rally tied to the AI-stock jitters pulled the 10-year Treasury yield down to 5.09% and the 2-year to 4.76%. Even so, prediction markets kept December Fed hike odds elevated near 91%, up from the ~83-89% priced a day earlier. Kitco and CNBC confirm the levels. Sentiment: safe-haven bid intact despite hawkish Fed path.
  • Oil Prices Diverge: WTI Jumps 3% on Inventory Draw, Brent Flat as IEA Releases ReservesCrude benchmarks pulled apart Thursday: WTI settled at $97.97, up 3.03%, after EIA data showed a much larger-than-expected 8.6-million-barrel draw in US crude stocks, while Brent eased just 0.13% to $105.92 after the International Energy Agency agreed to release over 20 million barrels of diesel plus the remainder of its March emergency reserve pledge, and Washington weighed easing sanctions on "shadow fleet" tankers. Qazinform and The Nation Thailand confirm the settles. Sentiment: supply-side crosscurrents, net energy-cost relief for importers.
  • Saudi, UAE Non-Oil PMI Hold at 55.3 as Tadawul Slips on PIF Strategy ResetSaudi Arabia's Riyad Bank non-oil PMI rose to 55.3 in September, its strongest since February and a sixth straight month of expansion, while the UAE's S&P Global PMI held at 55.3, matching a 20-month high. ICIS and Investing.com confirm the figures. Sentiment: diversification intact, giga-project ambitions recalibrated.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's rebound carried through a second session: the KSE-100 rose 768.66 points (+0.46%) to 166,186.17, with breadth firmly positive (292 advancers vs 169 decliners) on turnover of 517.6 million shares worth Rs23.57 billion and market capitalisation climbing to Rs18.475 trillion; PTC, FFC, HUBC, OGDC and SYS led, adding roughly 340 points combined. The driver was political closure rather than reversal risk: a joint sitting of Parliament voted 214-56 on Thursday to formally ratify the Article 232 emergency and Article 234 governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, over loud PTI objections, removing the standoff that had weighed on sentiment earlier in the week. The macro backdrop held steady: the rupee was little changed at 277.16 interbank, the SBP policy rate remains at 11.5% since its 14 September MPC decision, September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y from 11.1% in August, 6-month KIBOR sits near 12.16/12.41%, and the IMF reached a staff-level agreement on 8 October for a $1.2bn tranche (4th EFF and 3rd RSF reviews) that now awaits Executive Board approval, expected within four to five weeks. Dawn and Business Recorder.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf markets diverged from the macro story: Saudi Arabia's Tadawul All Share Index fell 0.55% to 10,083, with decliners outnumbering advancers 218 to 99, as Industrial Investment, Energy & Utilities and Building & Construction names led losses. OPEC+'s core seven kept November output quotas unchanged at a combined 31.01 million bpd, extending the pause as Hormuz-linked disruptions keep actual supply below target, with the group reconvening on 1 November. Fundamentals stayed firm: Saudi and UAE non-oil PMI both held at 55.3 in September, with Riyadh posting its strongest reading since February and Abu Dhabi/Dubai matching a 20-month high, even as the PIF's newly approved 2026-2030 strategy scales back costly real-estate megaprojects such as The Line to prioritise financial-return discipline, industry, minerals, AI and tourism amid fiscal pressure from softer oil revenue. ICIS and Gulf Business.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street absorbed an AI-financing scare without breaking: a chip-led sell-off that started in Asia pared its losses as the Dow rose 1.08% to 52,177.69, the S&P 500 added 0.30% to 7,744.06 and the Nasdaq edged up 0.08% to 26,713.03, helped by strong bank earnings and a bond rally that pulled the 10-year Treasury yield down to 5.09%; December Fed hike odds nonetheless held near 91%. Energy markets pulled apart: WTI jumped 3.03% to $97.97 on an 8.6-million-barrel US inventory draw, while Brent eased 0.13% to $105.92 after the IEA agreed to release over 20 million barrels of diesel and the remainder of its March emergency reserves. On the policy front, a bipartisan Senate push — the Trade Review Act, which would force congressional sign-off on new tariffs within 60 days — drew a White House veto threat, underscoring the growing political cost of the administration's tariff programme heading into the midterms. AP via WTOP and Yahoo News.
