01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100 Slides Nearly 1%The benchmark shed 1,692.75 points (0.97%) to close at 173,636.07 on Monday, swinging from an intraday high of 175,353.67 to a low of 173,603.75 as selling accelerated into the close. Volume fell to 679.19 million shares worth Rs23.69bn, and breadth turned decisively negative at 154 advancers to 311 decliners (30 unchanged). UBL, OGDC, PPL, MCB and FFC dragged the index down roughly 811 points; PSEL, AKBL and AICL added a combined 117 points. Topline Securities cited “renewed US-Iran geopolitical tensions and elevated international oil prices, raising concerns over inflationary pressures, the external account, and overall macroeconomic stability.” Sources: Business Recorder, The Nation. Sentiment: risk-off close.
  • Hormuz Tensions Escalate FurtherIran struck Kuwait with missiles and drones in early September in retaliation for US strikes on Iranian rocket launchers in the Strait of Hormuz, and CENTCOM confirmed US forces struck the Iranian crude tanker M/T Kylo in the Gulf of Oman on 5 September. Traffic through the strait — which carried roughly 20% of global oil supply before the war — remains under threat, keeping a war-risk premium bid under Brent near $97.3/bbl. Sources: Washington Post, USNI News. Sentiment: escalating supply risk.
  • Fed Chair Warsh's Hawkish PivotFed Chair Kevin Warsh sharpened his inflation warning at Jackson Hole on 28 August, recommitting to the 2% PCE target and arguing recent readings — PCE at 3.7% y/y — do not show underlying trends have meaningfully improved. CME FedWatch now prices roughly a 58–60% probability of a 25bp hike at the 16 September FOMC, up from near-56% a week earlier, setting up a showdown with the White House's push for lower rates. Sources: CNBC, Bloomberg. Sentiment: hawkish repricing.
  • OPEC+ Holds October Quotas SteadyThe seven-country OPEC+ core group, led by Saudi Arabia and Russia, agreed on Sunday 6 September to keep October output quotas unchanged, sticking to its existing roadmap through year-end as it prepares a capacity assessment ahead of setting 2027 quotas. Gulf non-oil momentum stayed resilient despite the war backdrop: UAE's PMI rose to 55.3 in August, its fastest pace since December 2024, and Saudi's PMI hit a six-month high of 53.8. Sources: Bloomberg, The National. Sentiment: steady supply, resilient non-oil.
  • Pakistan CPI Stays in Double DigitsAugust CPI held at 11.1% y/y, up from 9.2% in July, with urban inflation at 10.4% and rural at 12.2% on higher food, transport and communication costs. The SBP has held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting since July, while 6-month KIBOR sits at 11.86%. The IMF's fourth EFF and third RSF review mission is expected in Pakistan this month, with the country on track to meet six of seven quantitative performance criteria. Sources: Business Recorder, ProPakistani. Sentiment: inflation elevated, IMF review looms.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's tape turned sharply risk-off on Monday: the KSE-100 shed 1,692.75 points (0.97%) to close at 173,636.07, its worst session in weeks, as renewed Iran-Kuwait strikes and a firmer oil tape stoked fears over the external account and inflation. Breadth was decisively negative at 154 advancers to 311 decliners, with heavyweight banks and E&P names UBL, OGDC, PPL, MCB and FFC dragging the index down roughly 811 points as refining-sector penalties for missing the 1 October upgrade-deal deadline added sector-specific pressure. August CPI held in double digits at 11.1% y/y, the SBP's policy rate stays at 11.5%, and USD/PKR interbank held little changed near 277.40. The IMF's fourth EFF review mission is expected this month, with Pakistan on track to meet six of seven quantitative targets. Sources: Business Recorder, ProPakistani.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf non-oil momentum stayed resilient despite the conflict backdrop: the UAE's S&P Global PMI rose to 55.3 in August from 52.7, its fastest pace since December 2024, while Saudi Arabia's Riyad Bank PMI climbed to a six-month high of 53.8. OPEC+'s seven core members agreed on Sunday 6 September to hold October quotas steady, sticking with their existing roadmap into year-end while preparing a capacity assessment ahead of setting 2027 targets — a decision playing out against a firmer oil tape, with Brent near $97.3/bbl on renewed Iran-Kuwait strikes. Saudi's PIF, meanwhile, launched the Gulf Coast Development Company on 7 September to deliver an integrated coastal destination at Al-Khafji, part of its board-approved 2026–2030 strategy prioritising tourism, travel and domestic ecosystem investment under Vision 2030. Sources: Arab News, PIF.

C. Global Intelligence

US markets were shut Monday for Labor Day, so Friday's hot-jobs-driven selloff carries into today's open — the S&P 500 fell 0.38% to 7,718.60, the Dow lost 0.51% and the Nasdaq eased 0.29%, with the 10-year Treasury at 4.78% and the 2-year at 4.37%. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh sharpened his inflation warning at Jackson Hole on 28 August, and CME FedWatch now prices a roughly 58–60% probability of a hike at the 16 September FOMC. The other swing factor is the Middle East: continued US-Iran strikes, including a 5 September US strike on the Iranian tanker M/T Kylo, keep Hormuz risk elevated and Brent within reach of $100/bbl. Gold eased 0.5% to $4,405/oz on the firmer-dollar, hawkish-Fed backdrop. Sources: Washington Post, CNBC.

We'd trim broader beta and refining-sector exposure into the FOMC and any fresh Hormuz escalation, while staying selectively constructive on banks and E&P names that benefit from a firmer oil tape.
