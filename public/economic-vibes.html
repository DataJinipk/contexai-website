02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

The KSE-100 tumbled 2,546.94 points (1.41%) to 177,955.51 on Tuesday, wiping out Monday's energy-led gain after the Supreme Court ordered former PM Imran Khan's transfer to Shifa International Hospital for medical examination compounded fading prospects for a lasting US-Iran deal over Hormuz. The sell-off was unusually broad — banks, oil & gas, cement, fertiliser, power and autos all fell — on volume of 1.03bn shares worth Rs50.7bn, a sign this was genuine de-risking rather than sector rotation. July CPI eased to 9.2% y/y from 11.1% in June, and the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting. An IMF mission is expected next month for the fourth EFF/second RSF review, with roughly $1.2bn (a $1bn fifth EFF tranche plus $200m RSF) in view, contingent on budget implementation, privatisation and energy circular-debt progress.

B. GCC & Middle East

Brent held near $91.0/bbl after a vessel transiting the Strait of Hormuz near Oman was struck by a projectile, sustaining engine-room damage and a crew casualty, following the lapse of the 60-day US-Iran negotiation window; Iran maintains the strait stays "closed" until US sanctions conditions are addressed, even as separate Oman-mediated talks continue. Seven OPEC+ members are raising output by 188,000 bpd from August, a signal of confidence in demand even amid the standoff. Saudi Arabia's non-oil private sector stayed expansionary in July (Riyad Bank PMI 53.1), with the PIF's 2026–2030 strategy (tourism, manufacturing, logistics, NEOM) still the medium-term anchor. Firmer crude is a near-term fiscal tailwind for Gulf exporters, but the fresh tanker attack raises shipping- and insurance-cost risk for the region.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street fell Tuesday — S&P 500 −0.69% to 7,691.76, Dow −0.22% to 53,343.40, Nasdaq −1.33% to 26,289.71 — as a chip-stock sell-off compounded pressure from elevated oil prices and government bond yields. The 10-year Treasury yield stood at 4.74% and the 2-year at 4.19%, with the 30-year still near a 19-year high above 5.3%; Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish July tone — nine of 18 FOMC participants project a 2026 hike — keeps a September rate hike in play, a risk compounded by the fresh Hormuz tanker attack's inflationary impulse. Brent held near $91.0/bbl and WTI near $85.0/bbl on the risk premium, while gold eased 0.6% to $4,388/oz (silver −1.2% to $64.90) as higher yields capped haven demand despite the risk-off mood.
