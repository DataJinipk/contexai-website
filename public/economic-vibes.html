B. GCC & Middle East

PIF looks for new funding as oil slips and Houthi attacks disrupt Riyadh. Reuters reports that Saudi Arabia's PIF (over $900bn of assets) is in early talks on new ways to raise money without injecting more of its own capital. Options include securitising cash streams from portfolio companies, selling stakes in mature assets, trimming listed holdings, international bonds and export-credit debt. The drivers cited are a liquidity squeeze at Saudi lenders and a weak Gulf IPO market. PIF completed a 49% Humain stake sale to Aramco this month for $2.6bn, and Saudi real GDP is projected to shrink 3.6% this year (Reuters via ARY) [V, single source]. Riyadh businesses are moving meetings online and routing visitors through distant airports as Houthi missile attacks continue, a month after a strike at Riyadh's main airport (Circuit) [EST.]. Brent near $91.5 (−1.2%) squeezes Gulf budgets; Bloomberg reports the UAE cut its flagship Murban crude price as Mideast supply rises (headline only) [EST.]. Latest non-oil PMIs (carried): Saudi 54.0 and UAE 54.8 in October (Trading Economics, Arab News) [V]. Not verified: TASI, DFM and ADX closes and OPEC+ decisions.
