01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • PSX Grinds Higher in Volatile TradeThe KSE-100 added 268.58 points (+0.16%) on Monday to close at 171,153.16, after an early rally toward 171,732.62 faded into the afternoon; turnover rose to 692.88 million shares worth Rs20.10 billion. Breadth was narrowly positive — 238 of 494 traded names advanced versus 213 declines and 43 unchanged — with LUCK, ATRL, FFC, MARI and EFERT the top index contributors, adding roughly 254 points combined, while automobile assemblers, cement, banks, E&P and OMCs led sector buying. Daily Independent and Business Recorder confirm the print. Sentiment: grinding higher, narrow breadth.
  • Nasdaq Sets Record on AI-Led RallyUS equities posted their best session since early August: the S&P 500 jumped 1.49% to 7,765, the Nasdaq surged 2.26% to a record 27,122, and the Dow rose 0.71% to 52,049, as AI and tech names — led by Meta — rallied on cooling oil prices and easing yields, with sentiment also lifted by optimism ahead of this week's Trump-Xi summit in Washington. 02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Monday's 0.16% PSX gain to 171,153.16 capped a volatile, low-conviction session — an early 800-point advance faded as profit-taking set in — on turnover of 692.88 million shares (Rs20.10bn), with breadth narrowly positive at 238 advancers versus 213 decliners. The State Bank held its policy rate at 11.5% at its September 14 MPC meeting even as August CPI accelerated to 11.1% y/y (urban 10.4%, rural 12.2%) from 9.2% in July, per Business Recorder; 6-month KIBOR sits near 11.99% per SBP. The rupee edged firmer to 277.22 interbank, per Business Recorder. The IMF mission arrives today, September 22, for the fourth EFF and third RSF reviews plus a first Article IV consultation in two years, with the government's Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill 2026 — now before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance & Revenue — among the governance reforms under scrutiny, per Business Recorder.

B. GCC & Middle East

Oil fell sharply — Brent to $100.06 and WTI to $95.59 — on tentative signs of US-Iran de-escalation around the Strait of Hormuz, even as the tanker war continues; OPEC+ has held its combined output target unchanged for October, with its next review set for October 4. Non-oil momentum remains mixed across the bloc: Saudi Arabia's PMI held at a six-month high of 53.8 in August, per Arab News, while the UAE's non-oil gauge has cooled toward the 50-point break-even line amid war-related disruption, per Semafor. Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund has approved its 2026-2030 strategy, chaired by the Crown Prince, prioritizing tourism, urban development, advanced manufacturing, logistics, clean energy and Neom as Vision 2030's diversification push continues.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street posted its best session since early August: the S&P 500 jumped 1.49% to 7,765, the Nasdaq hit a record 27,122 (+2.26%), and the Dow rose 0.71% to 52,049, per CNBC. The 10-year Treasury eased to 4.96% even as the Fed's September 16 hike to 3.75%-4.00% — its first since 2023 — keeps at least one more increase priced in for this year, per CNBC. Chinese President Xi Jinping arrives in Washington September 23-25 for talks on trade, Taiwan and AI, while Congress's stopgap funding law signed September 2 keeps federal agencies funded through December 11, removing near-term shutdown risk, per Defense One.
