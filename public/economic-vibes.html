01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • PSX SelloffThe KSE-100 shed 2,541.20 points (−1.49%) on Monday to close at 167,970.66, its worst session in weeks, as fresh Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia and Iranian attacks on Gulf shipping triggered broad de-risking. Breadth was ugly — just 83 of 492 traded names advanced against 373 decliners — while volume thinned to 570.5m shares from 687.5m the prior session. Business Recorder and The Nation confirm the print. Sentiment: risk-off.
  • Tuesday ReboundBuying returned at the open Tuesday, with the index up 1,999.58 points (+1.19%) to 169,970.23 by 9:35am as the State Bank's FX reserves hit an all-time high of $21.4bn on Eurobond inflows. Index-heavy names — OGDC, PPL, MARI, POL, MCB, MEBL, HBL and NBP — led, with autos, cement, banks, E&P, OMCs, power and refinery all in the green per Business Recorder. Sentiment: cautiously firmer.
  • Fed On DeckThe US 10-year Treasury yield briefly topped 5% intraday for the first time since 2023 before settling near 4.99%, while the 2-year climbed to 4.66%, its highest since mid-2024. Wall Street slipped in response — S&P 500 −0.48% to 7,619.98, Dow −0.29%, Nasdaq −0.56% — with forecasters split ahead of Wednesday's decision: J.P. Morgan sees a 25bp hike while Goldman Sachs calls one "very unlikely." CNBC and FedRateCalc. Sentiment: hawkish tension.
  • Hormuz Risk PremiumBrent settled at $108.15/bbl, a four-month high, and WTI at $102.64, both up over 2%, after Oman postponed Gulf-Iran talks on Strait of Hormuz shipping and Saudi Arabia's drone-damaged East-West pipeline stayed shut. Vessel transits through the Strait fell to single digits over the weekend, reviving the crude risk premium per Vantage Markets and Trading Economics. A costlier import bill is a direct headwind for Pakistan's external account. Sentiment: import-bill headwind.
  • Metals PullbackGold eased roughly 1% to $4,310/oz and silver slid to about $63.30, both retreating from richer levels as a firmer dollar and near-90% odds of a Wednesday rate move pressured the complex — silver's industrial linkage leaves it more exposed than gold's safe-haven bid. Per Kitco and Yahoo Finance. Sentiment: profit-taking.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Monday's 1.49% PSX rout — driven by renewed Houthi and Iranian attacks in the Gulf — gave way to a firmer Tuesday open as the State Bank's reserves hit a record $21.4bn on Eurobond proceeds. The SBP has held its policy rate at 11.5% for a third straight meeting even as August CPI ran hot at 11.15% y/y, well above the 5–7% target band, per Trading Economics. Six-month KIBOR sits at 11.86%. The IMF's fourth EFF review mission lands September 23 for a near two-week visit, with roughly $1bn in tranche disbursement riding on progress against fiscal targets that have slipped on wheat and sugar market interventions. Meanwhile the Finance Bill 2026 tax overhaul — salaried-slab relief, a 1.25% minimum tax, and digital-invoicing enforcement — continues to work through implementation.

B. GCC & Middle East

The Hormuz stand-off is the region's dominant swing factor: Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline remains shut after drone strikes, and Oman's postponement of Gulf-Iran talks has pushed Brent to a four-month high near $108. Against that backdrop, OPEC+ continues its gradual output normalisation, having added 188,000 bpd in August across seven members. Non-oil momentum stays intact — the bloc's composite PMI held at 51.5 for a 21st straight month above the 50 mark, with UAE's reading climbing to 54.2 and Saudi Arabia's at 56.6. Riyadh's PIF Board approved its 2026-2030 strategy, pivoting from giga-project scale toward commercial returns in logistics, mining and AI infrastructure ahead of the Saudi Mega Projects Summit (Sep 28-30), which will showcase a pipeline exceeding $200bn.

C. Global Intelligence

US markets softened Monday as the 10-year Treasury yield briefly breached 5% for the first time since 2023, AI-IPO safety jitters weighed on chipmakers, and crude's Hormuz-driven spike added to inflation angst. The Fed's Wednesday decision is now the week's pivot: consensus is split between a 25bp hike (J.P. Morgan) and a hold (Goldman Sachs) — a rare divergence this close to a meeting. On the policy front, Congress returned from recess with government funding secured through December 11 but no consensus on an AI regulatory framework, leaving the field to a 29-state patchwork with only four legislative days left before members turn to the November 3 midterms.
