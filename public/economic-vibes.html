01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100 edges up 153 points to 174,930 after volatile session, breadth improvesThe benchmark inched up 153.09 points (0.09%) to close at 174,929.68 on Thursday, recovering a slice of Wednesday's 1,690-point rout after swinging between an intraday high of 175,796.24 and a low of 174,893.67. Ready-market volume held at 627.45 million shares worth Rs25.19bn across 494 companies, with breadth turning modestly positive at 252 advancers vs 207 decliners (35 unchanged). OGDC, PACE, TPL and TELE led gainers (+1.4% to +3.7%), while Pakistan Refinery, Bank Makramah and Cnergyico lagged (down 3.8%–6.2%); Topline Securities flagged renewed rotation into automobiles, cement and oil & gas exploration even as Gulf tensions capped the upside. Sources: Business Recorder, ARY News. Sentiment: choppy but stabilizing.
  • Wall Street jumps over 1% as Treasury yields ease and Fed's Waller turns dovishThe S&P 500 jumped 1.06% (+81.00) to 7,747.71, the Dow surged 623.57 points (1.18%) to 53,686.11 and the Nasdaq climbed 1.40% (+366.23) to 26,584.06 on Thursday, the strongest session in weeks, as the 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.77% off its multi-year high and the 2-year held near 4.35%. Fed Governor Christopher Waller struck a dovish tone, saying he was willing to “sit and be patient” as inflation data cools and flagging scope for downward core-CPI revisions, tempering — but not eliminating — the roughly 66% odds CME FedWatch still assigns to a hike at the 16 September FOMC meeting. Credit-sensitive and cyclical sectors led the advance. Sources: Investrade, CNBC. Sentiment: risk-on, yields easing.
  • Gold spikes over 3% to near $4,489 as Iran war reignites haven demand; silver tops $67Spot gold surged roughly $155 (3.5%) to trade near $4,489/oz on Thursday, its sharpest one-day jump in weeks, as renewed US-Iran hostilities and President Trump's warning that the fighting “wouldn't take too long” left traders unconvinced the conflict is truly winding down. Silver rallied in tandem to reclaim $67/oz, with the gold/silver ratio holding near 67. Dovish remarks from Fed Governor Waller added a second tailwind, easing the real-yield pressure that had capped bullion for weeks. Sources: Fortune, CNBC. Sentiment: flight-to-safety, rate-cut hopes.
  • Brent, WTI little changed as Oman denies Iran's Hormuz transit-fee claim; Trump warns MuscatBrent settled near $95.5/bbl (−$0.11) and WTI edged up to $91.3/bbl (+0.32%) on Thursday, holding a tight range even as the Gulf conflict entered its seventh month. A New York Post report said Oman rejected Iran's claim that the two sides had agreed to Strait of Hormuz transit fees for shipping, while President Trump separately threatened to bomb Oman if it “gets in the way” of talks to restore commercial passage through the strait — underscoring how fragile any de-escalation remains. Israel's defence minister also warned Iran that further strikes would draw retaliation against “all” of the regime's infrastructure, including energy sites. Sources: Investrade, Al Jazeera. Sentiment: geopolitical risk elevated, prices contained.
  • Saudi, UAE non-oil PMIs extend multi-month highs as OPEC+ eyes steady Q4 quotasThe UAE's S&P Global non-oil PMI held at 55.3 in August, its fastest expansion since December 2024, with new orders rising at the joint-quickest pace since March 2024 despite the ongoing conflict. Saudi Arabia's Riyad Bank PMI matched its six-month high of 53.8, with output growing at its strongest pace since early 2026. The resilience comes as OPEC+'s seven active members — having completed their 188,000 bpd September output increase on 2 August — head into a 6 September meeting expected to hold quotas steady through year-end, barring further Hormuz escalation. Sources: The National, World Oil. Sentiment: resilient non-oil momentum.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

The KSE-100 recovered a modest 153.09 points (0.09%) to close at 174,929.68 on Thursday, rebounding from Wednesday's 1,690-point rout on ready-market volume of 627.45 million shares worth Rs25.19bn, with breadth improving to 252 advancers vs 207 decliners of 494 traded. OGDC, PACE, TPL and TELE led gainers while Pakistan Refinery, Bank Makramah and Cnergyico lagged, as Topline Securities noted fresh rotation into autos, cement and E&P even with Gulf risk capping the upside. Pakistan's CPI inflation is running near 11% y/y in August on higher food and fuel costs, and the SBP is widely expected to hold its policy rate at 11.5% at the 14 September MPC; six-month KIBOR stood near 11.80%, and USD/PKR interbank was little changed around 277.52 (bid 277.42/offer 277.62). The Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill 2026 remains before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance ahead of the IMF's 4th EFF and 3rd RSF reviews, due to open this month. Sources: Business Recorder, Express Tribune.

B. GCC & Middle East

Brent held near $95.5/bbl and WTI near $91.3/bbl on Thursday as the Iran conflict entered its seventh month without a fresh flare-up: Oman denied Iranian claims of a Hormuz transit-fee deal, President Trump warned Muscat against obstructing shipping talks, and Israel's defence minister vowed retaliation against Iranian energy infrastructure if attacked again. OPEC+'s seven active members completed their 188,000 bpd September output increase on 2 August and head into a 6 September meeting expected to hold quotas steady into Q4. Non-oil momentum stayed firm: the UAE's PMI held at a 55.3 multi-month high while Saudi Arabia matched its six-month high of 53.8, both defying the war's overhang.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street posted its best session in weeks Thursday: the S&P 500 jumped 1.06% to 7,747.71, the Dow surged 623.57 points (1.18%) to 53,686.11 and the Nasdaq climbed 1.40% to 26,584.06, as the 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.77% off its multi-year high and the 2-year held near 4.35%. Fed Governor Christopher Waller struck a dovish note, saying he could “sit and be patient” as inflation cools, tempering but not erasing the roughly 66% odds CME FedWatch assigns to a hike at the 16 September FOMC meeting. Gold surged 3.5% to near $4,489/oz and silver reclaimed $67/oz as the still-unresolved Iran conflict kept haven demand bid even as equities rallied. Sources: Investrade, Fortune.

04The Day Ahead — KSE-100 Levels & Desk Stance

Levels (EST.): support 174,900 — Thursday's intraday low; deeper support at 174,000 · pivot 174,930 — Thursday's close · resistance 175,800 — Thursday's intraday high; then 176,467 (last week's close).

We read Thursday's modest 0.09% KSE-100 gain as tentative stabilization rather than a durable reversal: breadth improved to 252 advancers versus 207 decliners, and the index clawed back a slice of Wednesday's 1,690-point rout, but volume actually fell (627.45m shares vs 610.18m) so conviction remains thin. The bigger signal is the divergence between hard and soft assets: gold's 3.5% spike toward $4,489 alongside Wall Street's best session in weeks tells us global investors are pricing both easier Fed policy (post-Waller) and lingering Hormuz tail risk simultaneously, while oil stayed range-bound near $95.5 Brent as Oman rebuffed Iran's transit-fee claims. For Pakistan, that combination is a mixed blessing — a calmer oil tape eases the import-bill math into the SBP's 14 September MPC, but a stronger dollar-gold bid and 66% Fed hike odds still argue against near-term PKR strength. We favour a selective tilt toward E&P, banks and IMF-programme beneficiaries while staying alert to the 6 September OPEC+ decision, the SBP's MPC, and any fresh Hormuz escalation.
