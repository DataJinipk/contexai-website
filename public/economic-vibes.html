02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Wednesday's session saw the KSE-100 surge 1.66% to 180,014.93, adding 2,931.71 points to reclaim the 180,000 level after slipping to an intraday low of 178,512.09 in early trade. Breadth turned firmly positive — 303 advancers against 158 decliners (35 unchanged) on 740.6 million shares (Rs35.9bn) — while foreign investors were net buyers to the tune of Rs771.9m. Commercial banks, fertiliser, cement, exploration & production, oil marketing companies, power generation, investment companies and technology stocks led the advance, with HBL, UBL, Meezan Bank, Engro Holdings, Fauji Fertiliser, MCB, Lucky Cement, Systems Ltd and Hub Power among the top performers. The rally was underpinned by easing US-Iran tensions, Brent's slide from the mid-$80s toward $78, and reports that Islamabad is seeking Chinese refinancing of a $1.3bn commercial loan. On the macro front, headline CPI eased to 9.2% YoY in July from 11.1% in June, giving the SBP room to hold its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting on 27 July. Six-month KIBOR is tracking near 11.70–11.90% (EST.), broadly in line with the policy rate. The IMF's Executive Board cleared the third EFF/second RSF review on 8 May, unlocking roughly $1.1bn (EFF) and $220m (RSF); the next review is expected in H2 2026.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf non-oil momentum held up in July: Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI stayed at a robust 53.1 (53.3 in June) on firmer domestic demand, even as export orders fell for a fifth straight month on elevated freight costs. The UAE's non-oil PMI jumped to 52.7 from 50.8, with new orders at a five-month high and export orders growing for the first time since March, as Gulf economies dig out from wartime lows. Oil kept sliding on the diplomatic thaw: Brent fell to $79.15 and WTI slid toward $75 after Iran reportedly reached an agreement with Oman on a Strait of Hormuz shipping route — a bearish setup for crude that nonetheless keeps the Gulf's non-oil diversification story (Vision 2030, PIF-backed giga-projects) intact. Washington's Section 301 forced-labor tariff investigation, covering roughly 60 economies including Saudi Arabia and the UAE, remains a live overhang on Gulf exporters even as new corporate-transparency rules bed in.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street closed mixed on Wednesday: the Dow added 263 points (+0.5%) to a record 54,349, its fifth straight record close, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.17% to 7,723.55 and the Nasdaq fell 0.83% to 26,363.44, snapping a four-day rally as a tech rotation offset broader gains. Bonds stayed cautious: the 10-year yield sits near 4.63% and the 2-year at 4.21%, with markets pricing September rate-hike odds near 61% — down from over 82% before the July FOMC but still elevated — after Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish-tinted 29 July hold left his inflation-fighting credibility under scrutiny. Washington's trade agenda keeps moving: a 50% tariff on Canadian goods takes effect 19 August — now five days out — while Pakistan's own reciprocal tariff with the US sits at a comparatively favourable 19% (down from a proposed 29%), and Section 301 forced-labor duties of 10–12.5% on roughly 60 economies remain in force since 24 July.
