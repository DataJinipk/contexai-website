01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100 Rebounds 641 Points as KP Ratification Clears NA and SenateThe KSE-100 reversed two sessions of selling, climbing 641.07 points (+0.39%) to close at 165,417.51 on Wednesday after swinging between an intraday low of 164,060.34 and a high of 165,629.35. Buying in ENGROH, LUCK, FFC, BAHL and NBP added a combined 381 points to the index, while refinery names led by Pakistan Refinery Limited drew fresh interest; market-wide turnover held firm at roughly 586 million shares worth Rs 20.1 billion, with WTL topping the volume chart at 87 million shares. The catalyst: the National Assembly and Senate both ratified the federal emergency proclamation in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa on Wednesday — PTI lawmakers staged a walkout in protest — clearing the way for today's 11:30am joint sitting to formally ratify Governor's Rule under Article 234, with PPP having ended its boycott to back the resolution. Business Recorder and Daily Times confirm the levels. Sentiment: relief rally, political resolution in sight.
  • Hot US CPI Jolts Wall Street, December Fed Hike Odds SurgeUS stocks sold off Wednesday after September CPI came in hotter than expected at 3.7% y/y (vs 3.6% forecast) with core CPI at 2.6% y/y (vs 2.5% forecast): the Nasdaq lost 1.88% to 26,692.87, the S&P 500 fell 1.11% to 7,718.94 and the Dow slipped a milder 0.09% to 51,621.48. Fed-funds futures and prediction markets repriced sharply, with December rate-hike odds jumping to roughly 83–89% from ~72% a day earlier, and traders now debating whether the Fed delivers a double hike or a 50bp move before year-end. TheStreet and CNBC confirm the figures. Sentiment: risk-off, hawkish repricing.
  • Gold, Silver Slammed as Inflation Surprise Revives Rate-Hike BetsPrecious metals took the CPI shock hardest: COMEX gold settled at $4,120.82, down about 1.0%, after briefly trading below $4,100 intraday, while silver cratered 5.9% to $55.70, giving back a chunk of its recent run as the firmer dollar and higher yields pulled speculative longs out. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.19% after an earlier CPI-driven spike, while the 2-year held near 4.82%. Comex Live and Business Standard confirm the settles. Sentiment: sharp pullback, dollar/yields the driver.
  • OPEC+ Holds November Quotas Steady as Hormuz Risk Keeps Oil ElevatedThe seven-nation OPEC+ core group — led by Saudi Arabia and Russia — kept November output quotas unchanged at a combined 31.01 million bpd, extending the pause it started in October as conflict-related disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz continue to keep actual output below targeted levels; the group reconvenes on 1 November. Brent held near $105.43 and WTI near $95.17, keeping the energy-import bill elevated for oil-importing economies including Pakistan. World Oil and Trading Economics confirm the levels. Sentiment: supply discipline, risk premium intact.
  • Tadawul Slides 1.74% in Broad Gulf Risk-Off, Even as Oil Holds FirmSaudi Arabia's Tadawul All-Share Index fell 179.31 points (−1.74%) to close at 10,138.83, with breadth decisively negative (252 decliners vs just 15 advancers) on turnover of roughly SR3.9 billion (~$1.04bn); the parallel Nomu index dropped 0.90% to 21,832.37. The pullback came despite firm oil prices, underscoring that the region's risk-off impulse — tied to the broader Hormuz/security overhang and the global rates repricing — is currently outweighing the energy tailwind. Arab News confirms the close. Sentiment: broad-based pullback despite firm oil.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's tape found its footing: the KSE-100 rebounded 641.07 points (+0.39%) to 165,417.51 after an early dip to 164,060.34, as buying in ENGROH, LUCK, FFC, BAHL, NBP and the refinery counters offset the prior session's losses. The driver was political progress rather than reversal: the National Assembly and Senate both ratified the KP emergency proclamation on Wednesday — PTI staged a walkout in protest — clearing the runway for today's 11:30am joint sitting to formally impose Governor's Rule under Article 234, backed by an 11-member advisory council, after PPP ended its legislative boycott to support the resolution. The macro backdrop stayed supportive: the rupee held effectively flat at 276.95 interbank (+Re0.01), the SBP policy rate remains at 11.5% ahead of its next review on 26 October, September CPI was last confirmed at 10.3% y/y, and the $1.2bn IMF tranche still awaits Executive Board sign-off. Business Recorder and Business Recorder.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf markets moved the other way: Saudi Arabia's Tadawul All-Share Index fell 1.74% to 10,138.83 on broad-based selling (252 decliners vs 15 advancers, SR3.9bn turnover), with the Nomu parallel market off 0.90% to 21,832.37 — a reminder that regional risk appetite remains fragile even as energy prices hold firm. OPEC+ kept November output quotas unchanged at a combined 31.01 million bpd, extending its pause as Hormuz-linked disruptions keep actual supply below target, with the group's core seven reconvening 1 November; Brent held near $105.43 and WTI near $95.17. The UAE's VAT Executive Regulation amendments (Cabinet Decision No. 149) remain in force, continuing to weigh on SME compliance costs even as the broader non-oil diversification story (Saudi and UAE non-oil PMI both near 55.3 in September) stays intact. Arab News and World Oil.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street absorbed a hawkish inflation surprise: September CPI printed 3.7% y/y (vs 3.6% expected) with core at 2.6% y/y, sending the Nasdaq down 1.88% to 26,692.87, the S&P 500 down 1.11% to 7,718.94 and the Dow off a milder 0.09% to 51,621.48. Treasury yields whipsawed before settling — the 10-year eased to 5.19% and the 2-year held near 4.82% — while December Fed hike odds jumped to roughly 83–89% from ~72% a day earlier, with some desks now debating a 50bp move. The repricing hit precious metals hardest: gold settled at $4,120.82 (−1.0%) and silver slid 5.9% to $55.70. On the fiscal side, Washington remains funded under the continuing resolution through 11 December, pushing the next shutdown risk past the midterms. TheStreet and CNBC.
