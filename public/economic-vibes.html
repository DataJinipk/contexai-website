02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's headline CPI eased to 9.2% y/y in July from 11.1% in June, still above the SBP's 5–7% target band, after the Monetary Policy Committee held the policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting. The KSE-100's push through 180,500 on Monday was powered almost entirely by energy names riding firmer international crude, while banks and fertilizers lagged — breadth stayed negative (43 gainers vs 57 losers), a reminder the rally lacks broad participation. Attention now turns to September, when an IMF mission is expected in Islamabad for the fourth EFF/second RSF review that could unlock roughly $1.2bn, with energy-sector circular-debt reforms the key test.

B. GCC & Middle East

Saudi Arabia's non-oil private sector stayed expansionary for a fourth straight month, with the Riyad Bank PMI easing only slightly to 53.1 in July from 53.3 in June, even as export orders fell for a fifth month on high freight costs. The PIF's newly published 2026–2030 strategy prioritises tourism, urban development, advanced manufacturing, logistics, clean energy and NEOM, alongside reporting strong FY2025 revenue and profit growth. The Hormuz standoff — the interim US-Iran memorandum expired Monday without a successor deal — is a near-term fiscal tailwind for Gulf exporters via firmer crude, even as it raises shipping and insurance-cost risk for the region.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street fell Monday — S&P 500 −0.5% to 7,745.06, Dow −0.5% to 53,459.78, Nasdaq −0.3% to 26,644.91 — after the 60-day US-Iran memorandum governing the Strait of Hormuz expired without a successor deal, President Trump threatened action against Oman, and a senior Iranian official floated an "offensive policy" if diplomacy fails. The 30-year Treasury yield surged past 5.31%, its highest since 2007, and the 10-year rose to 4.72% as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh signalled a hike may not be his preferred tool against inflation, compounding debt-driven pressure on long yields. WTI jumped 3.1% to $84.95/bbl on the Hormuz risk premium, while gold held above $4,400/oz (silver $65.66, +1.3%) on softer US retail sales, both still well off 2026's record above $5,000.

04The Day Ahead — KSE-100 Levels & Desk Stance

Levels (EST.): support 179,500 — first floor below Monday's intraday low of 180,282; deeper support at 177,700 · pivot 180,502 — Monday's close · resistance 181,159 — Monday's intraday high; then round-number resistance at 182,000.
