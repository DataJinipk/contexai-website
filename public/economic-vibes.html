01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • PSX Rebounds on IMF OptimismThe KSE-100 opened firmly and surged as much as 1.12% intraday to a session high of 171,492.53 before late profit-taking pared gains, with the index settling at 169,969.33, up 368.92 points (+0.22%). Volume rose to 591.2 million shares (from 568.04 million) on traded value of roughly Rs20.2 billion, with advancers leading decliners 253 to 195 (47 unchanged) across 495 names. Sentiment was buoyed by expectations of progress in the IMF mission's fourth EFF review talks, the Finance Ministry's new Strategic Action Plan for the local-currency bond market, and a proposal allowing private firms to directly import LNG; HBL, Meezan Bank, Lucky Cement, Fatima Fertiliser, FFC, Engro Holdings, Engro Fertilizers, UBL, Systems and Mari Energies together added 467 points. TechJuice and Profit confirm the print. Sentiment: rebound stalls into the close, breadth positive.
  • Wall Street Diverges as Fed Hike BitesUS equities finished September mixed: the S&P 500 slipped 0.25% to 7,651.54 and the Dow fell 443.87 points (−0.86%) to 50,906.05, while the Nasdaq Composite edged up 0.24% to 26,861.06. For the month the S&P fell 0.5%, the Dow dropped 4.3% and the Nasdaq added 1.9%, as the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.29% and the 2-year to 4.92% following the Fed's 25bp hike to 3.75–4% on 16 September — its first increase since July 2023 under new Chair Kevin Warsh. A funding deal signed 2 September keeps the government open through 11 December, removing a near-term shutdown risk. CNBC and Yahoo Finance confirm the levels. Sentiment: yield-driven divergence, rate-hike hangover.
  • Oil Steadies Near $98 as Hormuz Flows RecoverBrent crude settled at $97.46 (the new front-month December contract closed at $98.03, +1.9%) and WTI at $90.60, holding well below mid-September's spike that followed Saudi Arabia's East–West pipeline closure. Middle East crude exports hit roughly 16.3 million barrels a day in September — the highest since the war began in February — with Kpler estimating Hormuz throughput near 80% of pre-war levels; tanker insurance costs remain elevated and Tehran says it is ready for both defensive and offensive action if strait-reopening talks stall. Al Jazeera and Fortune confirm the levels. Sentiment: risk premium easing but fragile.
  • Gold, Silver Slip as Real Yields BiteBullion gave back part of September's rally: spot gold fell 0.60% to $4,168.01 an ounce and silver dropped 1.39% to $61.13, as a firmer dollar and rising real yields overwhelmed softer core inflation data. Kitco and Rio Times confirm the levels. Sentiment: profit-taking after a strong month.
  • SBP Holds at 11.5%, Rupee Steady as IMF Review AdvancesThe SBP's Monetary Policy Committee held the policy rate at 11.5% for a third straight meeting on 14 September, balancing a cooling inflation trend — August CPI at 11.1% y/y, with brokerages pencilling September near 10.2–10.3% y/y (EST.) ahead of the PBS print — against persistent energy-price pressure. The rupee firmed a marginal Re0.01 to 277.11 interbank, while 3-month KIBOR held near 11.58/11.83 and 6-month at 11.80/12.05. An IMF mission under Iva Petrova began the fourth EFF and third RSF reviews on 23 September, with Finance Minister Aurangzeb holding a kick-off meeting on 29 September; a successful review could unlock roughly $1 billion under the EFF and $200 million under the RSF. Business Recorder and The Nation confirm the details. Sentiment: policy continuity, IMF-dependent outlook.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's macro narrative turned cautiously constructive into month-end: the KSE-100 rebounded 0.22% to 169,969 as the government briefed the visiting IMF mission (fourth EFF, third RSF review, begun 23 Sep) and unveiled a Strategic Action Plan for the local-currency bond market alongside a proposal letting private firms directly import LNG. The SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% on 14 September for a third straight meeting, with August CPI at 11.1% y/y and September estimated near 10.2–10.3% y/y (EST.) by brokerages. The rupee firmed marginally to 277.11 interbank. Risks remain skewed to Gulf-linked oil costs and the pace of IMF disbursement, with 170,000 acting as the index's psychological pivot. The Nation and TechJuice.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf fundamentals stayed split between resilient non-oil growth and a lingering Hormuz risk premium. Saudi Arabia's Riyad Bank PMI rose to 57.8 in September (from 56.4), a six-month high, as the Crown Prince's PIF 2026–2030 strategy doubled down on tourism, travel and entertainment to diversify away from crude. OPEC+ held its October output target unchanged at 31.1 million bpd, confirming the group has fully unwound its 1.65 million bpd of voluntary cuts, with a formal decision due at the 4 October meeting. Mid-September's Saudi East–West pipeline shutdown after drone strikes from Iraq, and UAE pump-price rises of up to 16.6% for October, underline that the Hormuz premium — even as flows normalise toward 80% of pre-war levels — has not fully unwound. Arab News and The National.

C. Global Intelligence

The Fed's 25bp hike to 3.75–4% on 16 September — its first increase since July 2023 under Chair Kevin Warsh — continues to reverberate: the 10-year Treasury yield sits at 5.29% and the 2-year at 4.92%, both at multi-year highs, even as a 2 September funding deal keeps Washington open through 11 December. Equities closed September mixed, with the Nasdaq up on the month and the Dow down 4.3%. In trade policy, the EU's finalised framework with Washington locks in a 15% tariff on most EU exports to the US (with carve-outs for aircraft, generic pharma and scarce raw materials) while Section 232 steel and aluminium tariffs remain, keeping bilateral friction alive into year-end. Separately, the SEC's proposed 'Regulation Crypto Assets' framework is in public comment through 20 October, a step toward clearer digital-asset rules. CNBC and Supply Chain Dive.
