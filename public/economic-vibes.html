01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100 Notches a Third Straight GainThe KSE-100 added 266.28 points (+0.16%) on Friday to close at 170,765.22, its third consecutive positive session, after swinging between an intraday high of 171,159.73 and a low of 169,890.56. KSE-100 volume eased to 212.46 million shares while broader-market turnover fell to 482.76 million shares (from 759.97 million) on traded value of Rs19.28 billion, with 57 stocks advancing against 41 decliners and 2 unchanged. Commercial banks (+161.84 index points), OMCs (+55.64) and cement (+52.48) led, with HBL (+71.32), UBL (+37.69), BAFL (+34.81), BAHL (+34.36) and PSO (+27.53) the top individual contributors; PSO also confirmed a Rs10/share dividend alongside lower quarterly earnings. Technology & communication, power generation and pharmaceuticals lagged, dragged by MEBL (−56.63), FFC (−38.25) and HUBC (−21.44). Arif Habib's Ali Najib said the index "traded in both directions before ending on a flattish note ahead of the weekend." The Express Tribune and Mettis Global confirm the print. Sentiment: grinding higher on narrow breadth.
  • Wall Street Closes Out a Strong WeekUS equities finished Friday higher across the board: the S&P 500 rose 0.51% to 7,743.41, the Dow gained 478.64 points (+0.93%) to 51,828.62, and the Nasdaq Composite added 0.5% to 27,068.72, with all three notching weekly gains as fresh tech optimism offset a bruising bond sell-off and easing oil prices into the close. Yahoo Finance and TheStreet confirm the closes. Sentiment: firmer into the weekend.
  • 10-Year Yield Hits a Fresh 19-Year HighThe 10-year Treasury yield finished Friday at 5.17%, its highest since 2007, while the 2-year ended at 4.81%, as markets continued to price roughly a 70% chance of a further Fed hike at the 27-28 October FOMC meeting under Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish stance. Advisor Perspectives and Trading Economics confirm the levels. Sentiment: hawkish, multi-decade high.

EnergyNow and CNBC confirm. Sentiment: volatile, geopolitically driven.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's tape stayed constructive: the KSE-100 logged a third straight gain, up 0.16% to 170,765.22, led by banks and OMCs even as breadth stayed narrow (57 advancers vs 41 decliners). The backdrop is dominated by the IMF's fourth EFF / third RSF review mission, led by Iva Petrova, which arrived in Islamabad on 23 September for a near two-week visit running into early October — examining fiscal performance (mostly on track but for a revenue shortfall) and flagging government intervention in wheat and sugar markets as a policy slippage, alongside the Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2026 still before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue. August CPI held at 11.15% y/y, well above the SBP's 5-7% target band; the SBP kept its policy rate at 11.5% at its 14 September MPC, with 6-month KIBOR at 11.72/11.97% (bid/offer) as of 22 September. Successful completion of the review could unlock roughly $1 billion under the EFF and $200 million under the RSF by late November/early December. Dawn and Mettis Global confirm.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf non-oil momentum remains a bright spot against a fraught energy backdrop: Saudi Arabia's PMI held at a six-month high of 53.8 in August (from 53.1 in July), while the UAE's non-oil PMI hit 55.3 (from 52.7), its fastest pace since December 2024. That resilience is being tested again: President Trump rejected Iran's latest proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days, sending Brent back above $105 on Monday, while the region is still absorbing the fallout from Houthi missile and drone strikes on Saudi energy and industrial infrastructure earlier this month. OPEC+'s Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee holds its 68th meeting on 4 October to review production policy against constrained Gulf spare capacity, and Saudi's PIF continues executing its 2026-2030 strategy — approved by the Crown Prince in April — across travel & tourism, NEOM, advanced manufacturing and clean energy. Arab News and Al Jazeera confirm.

C. Global Intelligence

The dominant global theme stays the bond market: the 10-year Treasury yield's climb to 5.17%, a 19-year high, reflects Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish path and October hike odds near 70% on CME-tracked pricing — yet Wall Street still closed out the week higher on fresh tech optimism. Crude is the other swing factor: Friday's dip on US-Iran truce hopes reversed Monday as Trump rejected Tehran's Hormuz-reopening proposal, pushing Brent back toward $106. Separately, Washington's tariff agenda widens on 29 September, when the 100% Section 232 duty on patented pharmaceuticals and APIs extends to all remaining importers beyond the large firms already covered since July, with generics and biosimilars still exempt pending a mandated one-year review. Advisor Perspectives and Federal Register confirm.
