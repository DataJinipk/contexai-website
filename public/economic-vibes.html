01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100's Fifth Straight GainThe KSE-100 rose 830.43 points (+0.48%) on Wednesday to close at 172,232.51 — its fifth consecutive positive session and highest close since early September — after touching an intraday high of 172,762.41. Volume jumped to 773.59 million shares worth Rs26.45 billion (from 641.83 million/Rs18.47 billion), the heaviest turnover since 4 September, as 262 stocks advanced against 196 decliners. Refineries led on Pakistan Refinery's upper circuit, with FFC, UBL, MEBL, ATRL and ENGROH adding roughly 493 index points combined; market capitalisation rose Rs84 billion to Rs19.178 trillion. Topline Securities and Arif Habib's Ali Najib both cited easing international oil prices and improving US-Iran diplomatic signals as the catalyst. Business Recorder and Daily Independent confirm the print. Sentiment: fifth straight gain, broadening breadth.
  • Wall Street Slides on Yield SpikeUS equities fell across the board Wednesday: the S&P 500 closed at 7,706.03 (−0.76%), the Dow dropped 1.03% to 51,511.59 and the Nasdaq lost 0.69% to 26,936.04, pressured by a surging Treasury yield curve, a weak five-year note auction and hotter-than-expected business-activity data. McDonald's (−4.89%), Alphabet (−4.68%) and Home Depot (−2.80%) led losses while Salesforce (+1.84%), Chevron (+1.38%) and Boeing (+0.95%) held up. Yahoo Finance and Motley Fool confirm the close. Sentiment: risk-off, yield-driven selloff.
  • 10Y Yield Hits 19-Year HighThe 10-year Treasury yield jumped over 13bps to 5.10%, its highest level since 2007, while the 2-year climbed past 4.92% on stronger-than-expected US activity surveys, hawkish commentary from Fed Governor Michael Barr on the need for further hikes, and a poorly received five-year note auction — compounding September's 25bp hike to 3.75-4.00%. CNBC and NBC News confirm the move. Sentiment: hawkish, yields at multi-decade highs.
  • Oil Snaps Losing Streak on Hormuz StandoffBrent rose 3.9% to $103.08 and WTI gained 1.8% to $92.16, snapping a five-day losing streak after Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian vowed at the UN that Tehran "will not surrender," dimming hopes of a near-term reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. The IEA has called the ongoing disruption the largest in the history of the global oil market, with over 10 mb/d of Gulf output still shut in and Kuwait's production down roughly a quarter on lost transit capacity. CNBC and AGBI confirm. Sentiment: volatile, geopolitically driven rally.
  • Gold and Silver Slide as Dollar FirmsGold fell to $4,290 (−1.5% / −$66.70 on the day) and silver tumbled 3.1% to $65.06 as a hawkish Fed narrative firmed the dollar and lifted real yields, denting precious metals despite the region's geopolitical backdrop. Fortune and USAGOLD confirm the pullback. Sentiment: pulling back on a firmer dollar.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's macro backdrop remains a study in contrasts: the KSE-100 notched a fifth straight gain to 172,232.51 even as August CPI accelerated to 11.15% y/y from 9.2% in July — food (+13.89%) and transport (+20.17%) the main drivers — pulling headline inflation back into double digits and well above the SBP's 5-7% target band. The SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% at its 14 September MPC meeting, while 6-month KIBOR sits at 11.72/11.97% (bid/offer). The IMF mission began talks this week on the fourth EFF review, with roughly $1.2 billion potentially unlocked (a $1 billion EFF tranche plus $200 million under the RSF) contingent on energy-sector reform, circular-debt targets and the Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill now before the Senate. Business Recorder and Daily Independent confirm.

B. GCC & Middle East

The Gulf's non-oil economies are outrunning the region's energy disruption: Saudi Arabia's PMI held at 56.6 in September, a 25th straight month of private-sector growth, while the UAE's non-oil PMI hit 55.3 in August, its fastest pace since December 2024, even as the Strait of Hormuz standoff keeps over 10 mb/d of Gulf crude output shut in and Kuwait's production down roughly 25%. Saudi's PIF has approved its 2026-2030 strategy, prioritising private-capital mobilisation and portfolio efficiency as Vision 2030 shifts from rapid build-out to sustained value creation. Gulf News and PIF confirm.

C. Global Intelligence

The dominant global theme is the bond market: the 10-year Treasury yield's surge to 5.10%, a 19-year high, reflects a hawkish Fed (September's 25bp hike to 3.75-4.00%, with Governor Barr flagging more to come), resilient US activity data and a weak five-year auction — a combination that is repricing risk assets even as equities stay resilient on an AI-led earnings narrative. Separately, Washington's tariff agenda widens: a 100% duty on patented pharmaceuticals and expanded Section 338 tariffs on Canadian goods both take effect 29 September. CNBC and Wiley Trade Compliance confirm.
