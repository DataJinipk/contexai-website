02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

The KSE-100 eased 0.07% (−119.74 pts) to close Monday at 181,310.28, trading in a 180,820.22–182,347.50 range, while the broader All-Share Index rose 0.21% to 109,402.32; index breadth was evenly split at 50 gainers versus 50 losers on volume of 434.73m shares, with the broader market trading 917.27m shares worth Rs44.81bn. CNERGY (+9.97%), ATRL (+7.25%) and PABC (+4.93%) led gainers while HUBC (−5.42%), AICL (−3.72%) and TRG (−3.41%) led decliners, as Power Generation & Distribution (−412.33pts) and Fertilizer (−134.19pts) offset gains in Oil & Gas Exploration (+251.44pts) and Refinery (+165.42pts) — a direct read-through from Monday's 5% oil spike (Business Recorder). On the macro front, CPI eased to 9.2% YoY in July from 11.1% in June, and the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting on 27 July; six-month KIBOR is tracking near 11.70–11.90% (EST.), broadly in line with the policy rate. SBP-held FX reserves stood at $17.04bn as of end-July, while the rupee held steady near 277.70/USD in interbank trade. Separately, the EU's 16 July GSP+ compliance assessment flagged continued concerns on labour rights and enforced disappearances, putting a portion of Pakistan's $8.96bn in EU-bound exports — more than 85% of which enter duty-free — under renewed scrutiny.

B. GCC & Middle East

OPEC+'s seven-member subgroup confirmed on 2 August a further 188,000 bpd increase for September, a sixth consecutive monthly hike that completes the unwind of 2023's voluntary cuts, though roughly 2m bpd of separate 2022-vintage cuts remain in place through year-end. Gulf non-oil momentum held up despite regional disruptions: Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI eased only slightly to 53.1 in July from 53.3 in June, a fourth straight month of expansion, even as export sales fell for a fifth consecutive month on elevated freight costs. Saudi's Public Investment Fund continues deploying its 2026-2030 strategy, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, pivoting from rapid capital deployment toward localisation, Saudisation and long-term industrial capability, with tourism and entertainment prioritised. Oil itself surged on the region's unresolved diplomacy and fresh supply scares: Brent settled 5% higher at $87.72 and WTI rose 5.1% to $82.13 as Iran-Oman talks on reopening the Strait of Hormuz remained stalled and an attack on a Saudi refinery near the Red Sea compounded supply concerns.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street took a breather from last week's record run: the S&P 500 eased 0.06% to 7,753.11, the Dow slipped 0.11% to 53,975.98 and the Nasdaq fell 0.32% to 26,605.36, pressured by a roughly 3% drop in Nvidia and the fresh jump in oil prices. The 10-year Treasury yield held near 4.65% and the 2-year near 4.25%, still digesting the Fed's own 29 July decision to hold rates at 3.50%-3.75% on a divided 9-3 vote — the first unified three-way dissent since September 2016, with Cleveland's Hammack, Minneapolis's Kashkari and Dallas's Logan all favouring a hike. September rate-hike odds, which had fallen to roughly 44-46% after July's weak payrolls print, are rebuilding again as Hormuz-driven oil gains revive inflation risk ahead of Wednesday's July CPI release. Gold ticked up to roughly $4,427/oz (+0.8%) and silver held near $66.3 on the same haven bid. Washington's trade agenda keeps moving too: a 50% Section 338 tariff on Canadian-origin dairy, alcohol and motor-vehicle exports is set to take effect 19 August, now eight days out.
