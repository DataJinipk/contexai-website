01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100 Plunges On War FearsThe benchmark shed 3,078.55 points (1.79%) to close at 168,865.04 on Thursday, its steepest single-day drop in weeks, as intensifying US-Iran strikes on tankers near the Strait of Hormuz and a surging oil tape triggered broad-based panic selling. The index swung from an intraday high of 171,945.83 to a low of 168,471.78; ready-market volume jumped to 628.67 million shares (Rs27.02 billion) from Wednesday's 477.67 million (Rs22.64 billion) as participation surged. Of 499 companies traded, only 58 advanced against 406 decliners; Pakistan Refinery (-4.24%), SSGC (-4.14%) and TPL (-3.4%) led sector laggards, while Cnergyico PK topped volume with 99.4 million shares. Investors are now bracing for the SBP's monetary policy announcement due Monday. Sources: Business Recorder, The Express Tribune. Sentiment: sharp risk-off, heaviest loss in weeks.
  • Brent Tops $101 On Tanker WarBrent crude settled up $3.29 (3.4%) at $101.21/bbl, its highest close since May, while WTI climbed $3.02 (3.25%) to $96.05, after the US and Iran traded their biggest wave of tanker strikes yet near the Strait of Hormuz — a corridor that carried roughly a fifth of global oil supply before the seven-month-old conflict, now running well below pre-war transit levels. Goldman Sachs flagged the risk of Brent surging above $120 if the escalation persists. Sources: Business Recorder, CNBC. Sentiment: supply-risk premium building.
  • Wall Street Falls A Fourth DayUS equities fell for a fourth straight session Thursday: the S&P 500 slipped 0.58% to 7,591.70, the Dow dropped 0.60% (-316.56 pts) to 52,064.10 and the Nasdaq eased 0.65% to 26,081.72, as August PPI data (+0.4% m/m, +5.4% y/y) reaccelerated wholesale inflation and oil topped $100/bbl. The 10-year Treasury yield jumped nearly 10bps to 4.94%, its highest since October 2023, while the 2-year hovered near 4.38% as markets priced roughly a 71% chance the Fed delivers a 25bp hike at next week's 15-16 September FOMC meeting under Chair Kevin Warsh. Sources: CNBC, Tipp Insights. Sentiment: broad-based risk-off, hawkish repricing.
  • Gulf Non-Oil Momentum HoldsAway from the war premium, Gulf non-oil economies kept expanding: the UAE's S&P Global PMI rose to 55.3 in August from 52.7 in July, its fastest growth since December 2024, while Saudi Arabia's Riyad Bank PMI climbed to 53.8, a six-month high, with output and new orders both accelerating. OPEC+'s seven core producers held October quotas unchanged at their 6 September virtual meeting, keeping the combined ceiling near 31.01m bpd as the group prioritises stability over added volume amid the Hormuz disruption. Sources: Arabian Business, ANI. Sentiment: resilient despite war premium.
  • Gold, Silver Ease Ahead Of FedGold slipped to $4,383/oz (-0.3%) and silver eased to $66.62/oz (-1.0%) on Thursday as investors squared positions ahead of the PPI print and next week's FOMC decision, even as haven demand stayed underpinned by the unresolved Hormuz conflict; ETF inflows into gold have totalled roughly $18bn this year. 02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

The KSE-100 plunged 3,078.55 points (1.79%) to close at 168,865.04 on Thursday — its steepest single-day fall in weeks — as intensifying US-Iran tanker strikes near the Strait of Hormuz and a surging oil tape drove panic selling across the board, led by Pakistan Refinery, SSGC and TPL. Ready-market volume surged to 628.67 million shares (Rs27.02bn) from Wednesday's 477.67 million (Rs22.64bn), with only 58 of 499 traded names advancing. On policy, the government's Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2026 remains before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue, a governance overhaul the IMF is expected to scrutinise when its fourth EFF/third RSF review mission lands on 23 September. August CPI held elevated at 11.15% y/y, the SBP's policy rate stays at 11.5% ahead of Monday's rate announcement, 6-month KIBOR sits near 11.87%, and USD/PKR interbank was broadly steady at 277.35 versus Wednesday's 277.36. Sources: Business Recorder, Dawn.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf oil benchmarks surged as the US-Iran conflict intensified around the Strait of Hormuz: Brent settled at $101.21/bbl (+3.4%), its highest close since May, and WTI climbed to $96.05, even as OPEC+'s seven core producers — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman — held October quotas unchanged at their 6 September meeting, keeping the combined ceiling near 31.01m bpd and prioritising stability over volume. Away from the war premium, non-oil momentum held up well: the UAE's PMI hit 55.3 in August, its best since December 2024, with new business at a joint two-year high, and Saudi Arabia's PMI rose to a six-month high of 53.8 as output and new orders accelerated. Sources: Business Recorder, Gulf News.

C. Global Intelligence

US equities fell for a fourth consecutive session Thursday as August PPI data reaccelerated wholesale inflation (+0.4% m/m, +5.4% y/y) and oil topped $100/bbl, denting the outlook for margins and rate cuts: the S&P 500 slipped 0.58% to 7,591.70, the Dow dropped 0.60% to 52,064.10 and the Nasdaq eased 0.65% to 26,081.72. The 10-year Treasury yield jumped nearly 10bps to 4.94%, its highest since October 2023, and the 2-year held near 4.38%, with fed funds futures now pricing roughly a 71% chance of a 25bp hike at next week's 15-16 September FOMC meeting under Chair Kevin Warsh, who struck a hawkish tone at Jackson Hole. Congress has already passed a continuing resolution extending federal funding through 11 December, removing a near-term shutdown risk. Gold eased to $4,383/oz and silver to $66.62/oz as investors squared positions ahead of the Fed decision. Sources: CNBC, Federal News Network.
