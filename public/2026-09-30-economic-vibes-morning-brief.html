01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100 Extends Losses on Hormuz Escalation The KSE-100 opened firmly at 170,944.34 but selling accelerated after 2pm as reports that President Trump had rejected an Iranian ceasefire proposal on the Strait of Hormuz rattled sentiment, dragging the index to an intraday low of 169,538.22 and a close of 169,600.40, down 825.22 points (−0.48%). Volume rose to 568.04 million shares (from 421.01 million) on traded value of Rs20.81 billion, with decliners swamping advancers 305 to 154 (37 unchanged) across 496 names. Selling was broad-based across automobile assemblers, cement, commercial banks, fertiliser, E&P and OMCs, with heavyweights HUBCO, MARI, OGDC, PPL, PSO and HBL all trading lower; the government separately briefed the IMF that Gulf-conflict disruptions have caused revenue losses and slowed economic activity. Business Recorder and ARY News confirm the print. Sentiment: broad-based selloff, breadth deeply negative.
  • Wall Street Softens as Yields Hit Multi-Decade Highs US equities drifted lower as the bond selloff continued: the S&P 500 slipped 0.17% to 7,671, the Dow fell 130.52 points (−0.25%) to 51,350, and the Nasdaq Composite eased 0.09% to 26,797. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed 4bps to 5.28% while the 30-year touched its highest since 2002 at 5.61%; the 2-year eased slightly to 4.89%. Losses were pared after New York Fed President John Williams suggested the central bank could hold until December before hiking again, even as CME FedWatch still prices 68% odds of an October move and 95% for December. CNBC and Investrade confirm the levels. Sentiment: cautious, yield-driven drift lower.
  • Gold, Silver Rebound From Monday's Selloff Bullion clawed back Monday's sharp losses as softer US labour-market and consumer-confidence data cooled October rate-hike bets: spot gold rose 1.42% to $4,172.40 an ounce and silver gained 1.23% to $61.27, both recovering from the prior session's 3%-plus slide. Kitco AM and Kitco PM report confirm the rebound. Sentiment: tentatively firmer.
  • Oil Splits: Brent Holds Hormuz Premium, WTI Eases Crude benchmarks diverged: Brent held near $105.31, barely changed on the session as the Strait of Hormuz risk premium persists, while WTI slid $3.22 (−3.48%) to $89.38 as Strategic Petroleum Reserve tapping and a partial easing of US-Iran rhetoric weighed on the domestic contract. Trading Economics and IEA confirm the spread. Sentiment: mixed, Gulf risk premium intact.
  • GCC: Saudi Non-Oil PMI Cools, OPEC+ Holds October Output Saudi Arabia's non-oil private sector notched a 25th straight month of growth, but the PMI eased to 56.6 in September from 57.7 in August as output and new-order growth moderated from recent peaks. Separately, OPEC+'s seven core members (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman) held October production at September levels, with Saudi output steady near 9.89mn bpd and the group's next meeting set for 4 October. Gulf News and IEA confirm the readings. Sentiment: resilient but decelerating.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's tape stayed on the back foot: the KSE-100 shed 0.48% to 169,600.40 as a fresh Hormuz escalation — Washington's rejection of an Iranian ceasefire proposal — drove broad selling across autos, cement, banks, fertiliser, E&P and OMCs, with breadth deeply negative (305 decliners vs 154 advancers). The government briefed the IMF that Gulf-conflict disruptions have already caused revenue losses and slowed activity, a headwind for the ongoing fourth EFF review. Domestically, the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a third consecutive meeting on 14 September even as CPI inflation held in double digits at 11.15% YoY in August (from 9.20% in July), with 6-month KIBOR tracking near 12.6%, per Trading Economics. Today (30 September) is also the FBR's final, non-extended deadline for salaried individuals and AOPs to file Tax Year 2026 returns, with late filers facing a jump in penalties from Rs1,000 to Rs25,000 and loss of active-filer status from 1 October, per Business Recorder.

B. GCC & Middle East

GCC markets stay anchored by a firm oil tape and a resilient, if decelerating, non-oil economy: Brent held near $105.31 on the Hormuz risk premium even as WTI eased on SPR tapping, while OPEC+'s seven core producers held October output at September levels (Saudi near 9.89mn bpd), with the next meeting set for 4 October, per IEA. Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI eased to 56.6 in September from 57.7 in August — still a 25th consecutive month of expansion but a step down from recent highs — per Gulf News. The PIF's 2026-2030 strategy, approved in April, continues to anchor Vision 2030 deployment across the Vision, Strategic and Financial portfolios.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street drifted lower as the bond selloff extended: the S&P 500 fell 0.17% to 7,671, the Dow lost 0.25% and the Nasdaq eased 0.09%, while the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.28% and the 30-year touched its highest since 2002 at 5.61%, per CNBC. Losses were cushioned after NY Fed President John Williams floated a December pause, though futures still price 68% odds of an October hike and 95% for December. On trade, new Section 338 tariff revisions and Canadian import restrictions took effect 29 September, while Congress's stopgap funding bill — passed in August — keeps federal agencies funded through 11 December, averting a shutdown at today's fiscal year-end, per NPR.
