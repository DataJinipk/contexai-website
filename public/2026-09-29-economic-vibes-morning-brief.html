01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100 Gives Back Early Gains The KSE-100 opened the week firmer — up as much as 273.36 points to 171,038.58 by mid-morning — before profit-taking and renewed Middle East jitters dragged it to an intraday low of 170,120.50 and a close of 170,425.62, down 339.60 points (−0.20%). Volume eased to 421.01 million shares (from 482.76 million) on traded value of Rs17.74 billion (from Rs19.28 billion), with decliners outnumbering advancers 267 to 181 (48 unchanged) across 496 names. Selling was broad-based across automobile assemblers, cement, commercial banks, fertiliser, E&P and OMCs, with index heavyweights HUBCO, MARI, OGDC, PPL, PSO and HBL all trading in the red as the oil-driven inflation scare offset earlier optimism around the IMF's fourth EFF review. Business Recorder and The Express Tribune confirm the print. Sentiment: range-bound, giving back early gains.
  • Wall Street Opens the Week Lower US equities fell broadly on Monday as resurgent US-Iran tensions lifted oil and Treasury yields jumped into a data-heavy week: the S&P 500 dropped 0.77% to 7,683.69, the Dow fell 347 points (−0.70%) to 51,481.51, and the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.92% to 26,820.38. Yahoo Finance and TheStreet confirm the closes. Sentiment: broadly weaker to start the week.
  • 10-Year Yield Extends to a Fresh 19-Year High The 10-year Treasury yield rose to roughly 5.21% on Monday, its highest since 2007, while the 2-year climbed to about 4.94%, its richest level since May 2024, as hawkish commentary from New York Fed President John Williams and Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson kept markets pricing close to a 70% chance of another quarter-point hike at the 28 October FOMC meeting under Chair Kevin Warsh. The move came alongside a record-weak final University of Michigan sentiment reading of 48.1 and one-year inflation expectations of 4.6%. Babypips and CNBC confirm the levels. Sentiment: hawkish, multi-decade high.
  • Oil Rebounds as Trump Rejects Iran's Hormuz Offer Brent settled Monday at $105.28 (after trading as high as $108.83) and WTI at $92.60 (intraday high $96.54), both climbing roughly 2-3% on the session after President Trump confirmed he had rejected Tehran's latest proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, reviving the Middle East risk premium just as OPEC+'s seven core members agreed on 6 September to hold October output at September levels. CNBC and Trading Economics confirm. Sentiment: volatile, geopolitically driven.
  • Gold and Silver Sink Over 3% as Yields Bite Gold slid 3.19% to $4,148.69 and silver dropped 4.31% to $61.53 on Monday — a seven-week low for gold — as the fresh oil-driven inflation scare hardened Fed rate-hike bets and lifted Treasury yields, overwhelming any safe-haven bid from Middle East tension. USAGOLD and Kitco confirm the closes. Sentiment: sharply weaker on rate-hike bets.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's tape opened the week constructively before fading: the KSE-100 shed 0.20% to 170,425.62 as selling in autos, cement, banks, fertiliser, E&P and OMCs offset an early rally, with breadth turning negative (267 decliners vs 181 advancers). The macro backdrop stays cautiously supportive — the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a third consecutive meeting on 14 September, even as CPI inflation returned to double digits at 11.1% YoY in August (from 9.2% in July), per Business Recorder and the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics. The IMF has begun its fourth EFF review and third RSF review, with roughly $1.1bn (SDR 760m plus SDR 153.8m) riding on the government meeting six of seven quantitative targets and finalising the Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill now before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance.

B. GCC & Middle East

GCC markets remain anchored by a firmer oil tape and a resilient non-oil economy: Brent's rebound toward $105 on renewed Hormuz risk follows OPEC+'s 6 September decision by its seven core members to hold October output at September levels, having already unwound the 1.65mn bpd voluntary-cut tranche (a further 2mn bpd 2022-vintage cut stays in place through year-end), per EnergyConnects. Non-oil momentum stayed solid into August, with Saudi Arabia's PMI at 53.8 (a six-month high) and the UAE's at 55.3 — its fastest pace since late 2024 — per Arab News and Gulf Today. Saudi's PIF kept up its Vision 2030 deployment pace through September, launching the Tawrid supply-chain financing platform, unveiling CEER's flagship vehicles, and standing up the Gulf Coast Development Company in Al-Khafji, per PIF.

C. Global Intelligence

Global markets opened the week on the back foot: US equities fell broadly (S&P −0.77%, Nasdaq −0.92%, Dow −0.70%) as the 10-year Treasury yield hit a fresh 19-year high near 5.21% and the 2-year touched 4.94%, with markets pricing roughly 70% odds of another Fed hike on 28 October under Chair Kevin Warsh amid a record-weak University of Michigan sentiment print of 48.1. Fiscal risk was defused for now — President Trump signed a stopgap funding bill on 2 September that funds the government through 11 December, averting the shutdown that would otherwise have begun 1 October, per Breaking Defense — but the underlying driver of Monday's selloff was oil: Brent's jump toward $105 after Trump rejected Iran's Hormuz proposal revived inflation fears just as the Fed leans hawkish.
