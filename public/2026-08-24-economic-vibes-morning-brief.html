02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

The KSE-100 rebounded 574.76 points (0.33%) to close at 177,166.52 on Friday, recovering from an intraday low of 176,110.75 as value-hunting in oil & gas names — Attock Refinery (+5.09%), OGDC (+1.99%), Hub Power (+1.25%) — offset drag from Thal Limited, United Bank and The Bank of Punjab; breadth turned positive (246 gainers vs 216 losers) on volume of 689.2m shares worth Rs35.66bn, with foreign investors modest net buyers (~Rs125m). July CPI eased to 9.2% y/y from 11.1% in June, and the SBP held its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting, with 6-month KIBOR near 11.54/11.79% (bid/offer) as of 20 August. An IMF mission is expected in September for the next EFF/RSF review, with roughly $1.2bn (about $1bn EFF plus $200m RSF) in view, contingent on fiscal consolidation, energy circular-debt reform and climate-linked benchmarks.

B. GCC & Middle East

Brent held near $94.4/bbl and WTI near $87.3/bbl as Strait of Hormuz shipping disruption kept a geopolitical premium in crude, even as seven OPEC+ members continue raising output by 188,000 bpd a month. Saudi Arabia's non-oil private sector held a fourth straight month of expansion in July (Riyad Bank PMI 53.1) even after the kingdom's Q2 real GDP contracted 4.8% y/y — its first quarterly contraction since 2023 — on Hormuz-linked output curbs, while Kuwait's non-oil PMI rebounded to 50.8 from June's 46.4. Separately, the PIF published its 2026-2030 strategy on 12 August, shifting from rapid expansion toward "value realisation" with deeper private-sector leadership across tourism, entertainment and advanced manufacturing — the medium-term diversification anchor even as elevated shipping and insurance costs weigh on regional trade.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street closed higher Friday — S&P 500 +0.4% to 7,674.37, Dow +1.0% to 53,277.01, Nasdaq +0.4% to 26,180.45 — but all three indices still logged a weekly loss after a bond-market selloff pushed the 10-year Treasury yield to 4.74% and the 2-year to roughly 4.18%, with the mid-week rally sparked by the US Treasury's expanded long-bond buyback plan fading as yields climbed back through Thursday and Friday. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish tilt remains unmoved by the Treasury's intervention, keeping the tension between fiscal yield-management and monetary policy in focus ahead of the next FOMC meeting. Gold climbed to a three-month high near $4,603/oz (+~1.9%) on renewed fiscal-sustainability hedging, with silver near $68.97/oz, even as elevated yields argued against the rally on paper.
