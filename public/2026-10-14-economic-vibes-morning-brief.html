02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Pakistan's tape stayed politically hostage: the KSE-100 opened over 900 points higher before late selling cut it to a close of 164,776.44, down 347.97 points (−0.21%), after the National Assembly and Senate sessions summoned for 6pm Tuesday failed to ratify the KP Governor's Rule as hoped — PPP has signalled it will back the government's resolution, but a joint sitting is now pushed to Thursday, 15 October. Index-heavy names (LUCK, DGKC, HBL, MCB, MEBL, OGDC, PSO) traded green intraday on hopes of a quick resolution, and market-wide volume rose to 383.75m shares from 362.95m, before profit-taking reasserted itself. The macro backdrop stayed constructive: September CPI confirmed at 10.3% y/y, the SBP held at 11.5% for a third straight meeting, the rupee held flat at 276.96 interbank, and the $1.2bn IMF tranche still awaits Executive Board approval. Business Recorder and The Nation.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf markets stabilised after Monday's security shock: Saudi Arabia's Tadawul All-Share Index rebounded 0.33% to close at 10,318.14 on SR3.28bn turnover, though breadth stayed soft (104 advancers vs 146 decliners), while the Nomu parallel market added 0.37% and the MSCI Tadawul 30 gained a similar margin — a sign risk appetite is returning even as the Hormuz/tanker-attack overhang persists with Saudi forces reportedly striking Houthi positions. Brent held near $105.67 and WTI near $94.14 after OPEC+ kept November quotas unchanged, giving Gulf energy names a steady tailwind. Diversification momentum stayed intact: Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI climbed to 55.3 in September, its strongest reading since February and a sixth straight month of growth, while the UAE's non-oil PMI held at 55.3 with output prices rising at their fastest pace in over 15 years. Arab News and The National.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street closed lower Tuesday as memory-chip weakness and mixed bank earnings outweighed an otherwise resilient tape: the Dow fell 0.35% to 51,670.10, the S&P 500 eased 0.02% to 7,805.45 and the Nasdaq lost 0.15% to 27,204.38, with traders positioning cautiously ahead of Wednesday's US CPI print. Treasury yields stayed elevated (10-year 5.21%, 2-year 4.81%, 30-year just below 5.58%) even as Fed-funds futures trimmed December hike odds to roughly 72% from last week's ~88%; the same mix of a softer-but-still-firm dollar and persistent geopolitical risk pulled gold down 0.24% to $4,160.80 and silver down 1.82% to $59.09. Washington's broader funding picture stays parked under the continuing resolution through 11 December, though the narrower DHS funding lapse remains unresolved separately. TheStreet and Kitco.
