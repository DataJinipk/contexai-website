02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Thursday's session saw the KSE-100 add 0.98% to close at 181,776.59, gaining 1,761.66 points as the rally that reclaimed 180,000 a day earlier extended into a fourth session. Breadth stayed constructive — 75 of 100 index constituents advanced against 25 decliners (282 gainers across the broader 494-company market) — on turnover of 345.78 million shares (Rs40.31bn), with commercial banks (+500.49 index points), oil & gas exploration (+264.49), cement (+251.19), fertiliser (+224.08) and power generation (+124.47) leading the advance; UBL, MARI, FFC, LUCK and BAHL were the biggest index contributors, while NPL, GADT, MLCF, PAEL and NML topped the gainers' board (Mettis Global). On the macro front, headline CPI eased to 9.2% YoY in July from 11.1% in June, giving the SBP room to hold its policy rate at 11.5% for a second straight meeting on 27 July. Six-month KIBOR is tracking near 11.70–11.90% (EST.), broadly in line with the policy rate. The IMF's Executive Board cleared the third EFF/second RSF review on 8 May, unlocking roughly $1.1bn (EFF) and $220m (RSF), with reform milestones including a new tax-audit manual due by end-August.

B. GCC & Middle East

OPEC+'s seven-member subgroup agreed on 2 August to add a further 188,000 bpd from September, extending the gradual unwind of the April 2023 voluntary cuts even as the oil market stays jittery on Hormuz headlines. Gulf non-oil momentum held up in July: Saudi Arabia's non-oil PMI eased only slightly to 53.1 (53.3 in June) on firmer domestic demand, while the UAE's climbed to a four-month high of 52.7 from 50.8, with new business at a five-month high and employment growth returning. Saudi's Public Investment Fund board approved its 2026-2030 strategy, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, pivoting from rapid capital deployment toward localisation, Saudisation and long-term industrial capability across six ecosystems including travel & tourism, urban development and NEOM. Oil itself reversed higher on the region's diplomacy: Brent jumped 4.9% to $83.34 and WTI rose 3.37% to $77.75 after Iran said a Hormuz reopening remains contingent on Washington lifting its naval blockade, unwinding much of this week's de-escalation rally.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street pulled back from this week's record highs: the Dow shed 464 points (−0.85%) to 53,885, the S&P 500 slipped 0.18% to 7,709.96 and the Nasdaq eased 0.06% to 26,348.35, as rising Treasury yields (10Y 4.61%, 2Y ~4.18%) and a jump in oil prices weighed on risk appetite even as softer US ADP jobs data kept September rate-cut hopes alive. Gold climbed to a seven-week high near $4,271 (+2.38%) and silver added 0.6% to $61.83 on the same haven bid. Washington's trade agenda keeps moving: a 50% Section 338 tariff on roughly $20bn of Canadian dairy, alcohol and motor-vehicle exports takes effect 19 August — now 12 days out — while Pakistan's own reciprocal tariff with the US remains a comparatively favourable 19%.
