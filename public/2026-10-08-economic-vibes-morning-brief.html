01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • KSE-100 Grinds to a Third Straight Gain The KSE-100 edged up 120.30 points (+0.07%) to close at 168,580.40 on Wednesday, a narrow, choppy session that swung between an intraday high of 169,271.98 and a low of 168,065.86. Overall market volume rose to roughly 586.86 million shares worth Rs22.37 billion across 304,709 trades in 501 companies, with advancers outnumbering decliners 272 to 184 (114 unchanged) across 570 names. United Bank (+97.41 index points), Systems Limited (+65.08), Pakistan State Oil (+48.44), PTCL (+45.78) and K-Electric (+43.98) led the index higher, while Mari Energies (−64.25), Meezan Bank (−35.05), Lucky Cement (−32.67), Hub Power (−28.92) and OGDC (−26.03) capped the gains. Business Recorder and Pakera confirm the levels. Sentiment: narrow, choppy third straight advance.
  • Wall Street Retreats From Records on Yields, Fed Minutes US equities pulled back Wednesday after Tuesday's records: the S&P 500 fell 0.22% to 7,801.77, the Dow lost 0.66% (−341.41 pts) to 51,179.87, and the Nasdaq slipped 0.22% to 27,538.69 as long-dated Treasury yields climbed back up (10-year near 5.32%, 2-year near 4.80%) and the dollar firmed. Minutes from the Fed's 15–16 September meeting — where the FOMC delivered its first hike since 2023 to 3.75–4.00% — were released at 2:00pm ET and kept a further 2026 move on the table, even as prediction markets price roughly 83–84% odds the Fed holds at the 28 October meeting following a soft September payrolls print (+29,000 vs. +80,000 expected). Yahoo Finance and U.S. News confirm the levels. Sentiment: records give way to a yields-driven pullback.
  • Oil Firms as OPEC+ Holds November Output, PIF Unveils 2026–2030 Strategy Crude firmed Wednesday — Brent at $100.87 (+0.29%) and WTI at $90.09 — after OPEC+'s seven core producers (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman) agreed on 4 October to hold November output targets unchanged, extending the pause as Hormuz- and Red Sea-linked disruptions keep actual flows below quota. Separately, Saudi Arabia's PIF, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, approved its 2026–2030 strategy, pivoting from rapid expansion toward value creation across tourism, NEOM, advanced manufacturing and clean energy as the kingdom's non-oil growth engine under Vision 2030. CNBC and PIF confirm the details. Sentiment: supply discipline and Gulf diversification support the tape.
  • Gold Slumps to Two-Month Low as Fed Minutes, Firm Dollar Bite Bullion sold off Wednesday as the Fed minutes kept a December hike risk alive alongside elevated long-end yields and a stronger dollar: spot gold fell roughly 1.3% to near $4,110, its lowest in about two months, while spot silver dropped about 1.1% to $60.68. Kitco and TMGM confirm the levels. Sentiment: yields and the dollar pressure non-yielding metals.
  • SBP Holds at 11.5% as IMF Pushes Market-Based Rupee Pakistan's SBP told the IMF its 11.5% policy rate remains appropriate, as September CPI eased to 10.3% y/y (from 11.1% in August) — still above the 5–7% target band — while three-month KIBOR sat near 11.97% and six-month near 12.39% as of 5 October. The IMF mission has shared a first draft of the Memorandum of Economic and Financial Policies toward a staff-level agreement, but Islamabad and the Fund remain split on exchange-rate policy — the government favouring rupee stability versus the IMF's push for a fully market-based rate — even as the rupee firmed marginally to 277.02 interbank and the current account deficit narrowed 36% y/y to $543 million in July–August FY27. ProPakistani and Trading Economics confirm the details. Sentiment: policy continuity, exchange-rate dispute the key swing factor.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf fundamentals stayed firm as oil prices edged higher: Brent settled at $100.87 (+0.29%) and WTI at $90.09 after OPEC+'s seven core producers held November output targets unchanged at their 4 October meeting, extending the pause that began in October as Hormuz- and Red Sea-linked disruptions keep real flows below quota. Saudi Arabia's PIF, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, approved its 2026–2030 strategy, shifting from rapid asset growth toward sustained value creation across tourism, NEOM, advanced manufacturing, logistics and clean energy — continuing to lean on non-oil diversification under Vision 2030 even as the security backdrop around regional shipping lanes stays fragile.
