02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

The KSE-100 fell for a third straight session, shedding 698.56 points (0.40%) to close at 171,943.60 on Wednesday — a nearly 3,400-point slide over three days — as the widening US-Iran conflict and a surging oil tape drove broad-based selling in banks, E&P, cement, fertiliser and OMC names, led by UBL, OGDC, PPL, MCB, Fauji Fertiliser and Lucky Cement. Ready-market volume eased to 477.67 million shares from Tuesday's 722.62 million. On the policy front, the government's Sovereign Wealth Fund (Amendment) Bill, 2026 remains before the Senate Standing Committee on Finance and Revenue, a governance overhaul the IMF is expected to scrutinise when its fourth EFF/third RSF review mission arrives this month, with Pakistan tracking to meet six of seven quantitative targets. August CPI jumped to 11.15% y/y from July's four-month low of 9.2%, the SBP's policy rate stays at 11.5% (held since 27 July), 6-month KIBOR sits at 11.86%, and USD/PKR interbank has drifted to roughly 279.40 from Tuesday's 277.37 as the oil-driven import bill widens. Sources: Business Recorder, Business Recorder.

B. GCC & Middle East

Gulf oil benchmarks surged as the US-Iran conflict intensified around the Strait of Hormuz: Brent topped $100/bbl for the first time since July (+3%) and WTI climbed to about $96.2, even as OPEC+'s seven core producers — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman — held October quotas unchanged at their 6 September meeting, keeping the combined ceiling near 31.01m bpd and prioritising stability over volume. Away from the war premium, non-oil momentum held up well: the UAE's S&P Global PMI hit 55.3 in August, its best reading since December 2024, and Saudi Arabia's PMI rose to a six-month high of 53.8. Saudi's PIF continues executing its board-approved 2026–2030 strategy, having launched the Gulf Coast Development Company on 7 September to build an integrated coastal destination at Al-Khafji under the Vision 2030 tourism and urban-development push. Sources: CNBC, Arabian Business.

C. Global Intelligence

US equities fell for a third consecutive session Wednesday as rising oil prices and firm Treasury yields dented the outlook for corporate margins and consumer demand: the S&P 500 slipped 0.48% to 7,636.46, the Dow dropped 0.77% to 52,381.02 and the Nasdaq eased 0.64% to 26,253.34. The 10-year Treasury held near 4.80% and the 2-year near 4.40%, both close to multi-month highs, as traders weighed a coin-flip 16 September FOMC decision under Chair Kevin Warsh against Governor Waller's dovish lean. Congress continues working toward a stopgap funding bill to avert a shutdown at the 30 September fiscal-year deadline, with prediction markets pricing only modest odds of a lapse. Gold reclaimed $4,400/oz (+1.1%) and silver rose to $66.58/oz as haven demand built against the hawkish-Fed, oil-shock backdrop. Sources: Investrade, CNBC.
