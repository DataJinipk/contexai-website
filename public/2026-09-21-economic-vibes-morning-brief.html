01Five Things You Need To Know To Start Your Day

  • PSX Rallies on Broad-Based Buying The KSE-100 surged 1,841.39 points (+1.09%) on Friday to close at a record 170,884.58, extending Thursday's rebound as buying swept automobile assemblers, cement, commercial banks, exploration & production and OMCs; index heavyweights LUCK, MCB, MEBL, NBP, UBL, MARI, OGDC, POL, PPL and PSO all traded in the green. Breadth was decisively positive — 351 of 496 traded names advanced versus 110 declines — on turnover of roughly 165.8 million KSE-100 shares, with Media Times, WorldCall Telecom and Cnergyico topping the volume table. Business Recorder and Daily Independent confirm the print. Sentiment: broad-based rally.
  • Wall Street Ends Fed-Hike Week Mixed US equities closed a volatile week quietly: the S&P 500 edged up 0.17% to 7,650.50 and the Nasdaq gained 0.39% to 26,522.55, while the Dow slipped 0.18% to 51,682.64 — its third straight losing week and worst since March. The move came as traders digested Wednesday's first Fed rate hike in three years and priced rising odds of a follow-up increase at the October 28 meeting. TheStreet and BBN Times. Sentiment: mixed, Fed-path driven.
  • 10-Year Nears 5% as Hike Odds Build The 10-year Treasury yield rose to about 5.01%, its highest level of the run-up, while the 2-year holds near 4.75%, as markets moved to price a roughly 58% chance of another 25bp increase at the Fed's October 28 meeting after 16 of 18 officials' dot-plot projections showed room for at least one more hike this year. Yahoo Finance and CNBC. Sentiment: higher-for-longer repriced.
  • Oil Extends Its Slide Brent eased to $103.87/bbl (−0.91%) and WTI fell 1.6% to $100.30, extending the week's pullback as Saudi Arabia's drone-damaged East-West pipeline nears full repair; both benchmarks remain well above pre-attack levels. Separately, OPEC+ confirmed it will pause its monthly output increases for October, holding the group's target at 31.1 million bpd with the next review set for October 4. CNBC. A softer barrel remains a tailwind for Pakistan's import bill even as OPEC+ signals no further easing near-term. Sentiment: import-bill relief, supply capped.
  • Gold & Silver Extend Their Run Gold climbed 0.60% to about $4,368.60/oz, touching a weekly high near $4,440, while silver jumped 3.02% to $67.18, extending the post-Fed rally for a second straight session as easing oil dragged yields off their highs and compressed the gold/silver ratio to roughly 65 — its tightest of the week. In Washington, Congress's stopgap funding law signed September 2 keeps the government open only through December 11, setting up the next fiscal cliff just as the Fed weighs an October hike. USAGOLD and NPR. Sentiment: safe-haven bid intact.

02Regional Market & Economic Matrix

A. Local Market — Pakistan Focus

Friday's 1.09% PSX rally to a record 170,884.58 capped a strong week as banks, autos, cement and E&P led broad-based buying, with breadth firmly positive (351 advancers vs. 110 decliners) on roughly 165.8 million KSE-100 shares traded. The State Bank held its policy rate at 11.5% at its September 14 MPC meeting even as August CPI printed at 11.1% y/y (urban 10.4%, rural 12.2%) — up from 9.2% in July — per Business Recorder; 6-month KIBOR sits near 11.98% per SBP. The rupee held steady at 277.25 interbank, per Business Recorder. An IMF mission is due in Islamabad around September 22-23 for the fourth EFF review, third RSF review and the first Article IV consultation in two years; Pakistan has reportedly met six of seven end-June performance targets, with tax-net broadening the lone laggard, per PhotoNews Pakistan.

B. GCC & Middle East

Oil eased further to close the week — Brent at $103.87 and WTI at $100.30 — as Saudi Arabia's damaged East-West pipeline nears full repair, per CNBC. OPEC+ confirmed it will pause its monthly output increases for October, holding the group's combined target at 31.1 million bpd after months of steady restoration, with the next review set for October 4. Non-oil momentum stays firm — Saudi Arabia's PMI held at a six-month high of 53.8 in August, per Arab News, while the UAE's composite reading climbed to 55.3, its strongest since late 2024, per The National.

C. Global Intelligence

Wall Street closed a volatile, Fed-dominated week on a quiet note: the S&P 500 edged up 0.17% to 7,650.50 and the Nasdaq rose 0.39% to 26,522.55, while the Dow slipped 0.18% to 51,682.64 for its third straight losing week, per TheStreet. The 10-year Treasury climbed to about 5.01% as markets priced a roughly 58% chance of another 25bp hike at the Fed's October 28 meeting, following Wednesday's first increase in three years to 3.75%-4.00%, per Yahoo Finance. Separately, Congress's stopgap funding law signed September 2 keeps federal agencies funded only through December 11, setting up the next shutdown cliff.
